Citibank has opened its first Vienna branch, transforming a former restaurant space into one of the bank’s first locations built around a new, more flexible design.

The bank began operating Jan. 27 at 201 Maple Avenue East and marked its grand opening Tuesday morning with a ribbon-cutting ceremony, remarks from elected officials and food from neighboring businesses Call Your Mother and Lily’s Chocolate and Coffee.

The 2,304-square-foot corner suite was occupied by Noodles & Company for years. It is now Citibank’s fourth Fairfax County location and only the second new branch the company has opened in the Washington region in more than a decade, following a Feb. 11 grand opening in Clarendon.

Mindy Mercaldo, who leads Citibank’s U.S. branch network, said Vienna’s central Northern Virginia location, proximity to Washington and combination of residential and commercial activity made it attractive to the bank. The Maple Avenue corner also offers substantial nearby retail activity.

Citigroup has been cutting jobs under a restructuring plan, but the company is expanding its physical presence around Washington, where it currently operates 32 branches. An Anacostia location is planned, while the Georgetown branch is set to move.

## A Different Kind of Bank Interior

The Vienna branch is among the first to use a new prototype developed with customer input. Although smaller than earlier full-service branches, its open floor plan and movable furniture are intended to make the space feel larger and allow it to host events.

Instead of a traditional teller line, the branch has a central customer-service desk. It also includes a partially enclosed area for more private conversations and a large interactive digital display.

Mercaldo said the layout reflects the changing purpose of physical bank branches. With routine deposits and withdrawals increasingly handled online, branches are becoming places where customers seek financial guidance and personal assistance.

A wood etching depicting Vienna’s street map covers the wall behind the service desk, adding a distinctly local feature to the space. Mercaldo described the goal as combining personal service with a digital experience while connecting the branch to the surrounding community.

Del. Holly Seibold called the branch an inviting and accessible space that could broaden local access to financial support while contributing to Vienna’s economic growth.

Mayor Linda Colbert said she is looking forward to educational seminars Citibank plans to hold at the location. She also pointed to rumors that the bank may add a mural to the bare brick exterior facing Park Street, saying such a project would signal its desire to become part of the community.
