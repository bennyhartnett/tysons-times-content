Chef Victor Albisu is preparing to open Electric Bull, a live-fire steakhouse and butcher shop that will challenge the familiar steakhouse formula on Vienna’s Maple Avenue.

The Northern Virginia chef behind Taco Bamba is returning to the traditions that shaped his career: butchery, grilling and bold Latin American flavors. Albisu describes the new restaurant as an “anti-steakhouse,” designed to offer serious cooking without the formality of a conventional special-occasion dining room.

Electric Bull will occupy a 2,500-square-foot space at 176 Maple Ave. W., a few doors from Taco Bamba. Plans call for approximately 80 seats, a full bar and an outdoor patio for warmer weather.

## Lesser-known cuts meet live fire

Rather than building its menu around filet mignon and New York strip, Electric Bull will showcase flavorful butcher’s cuts such as picanha, hanger steak, teres major, flat iron and flank steak. South American-style short ribs are also planned.

Much of the cooking will take place over live fire, drawing inspiration from the parrilla grilling traditions of Argentina and Uruguay. The restaurant’s approach will emphasize robust flavors, straightforward ingredients and a relaxed neighborhood atmosphere.

Steak will anchor the menu, but it will not stand alone. A raw bar is expected to serve ceviches, crudos, carpaccio, tartares and both raw and grilled oysters.

South American influences will also appear among the starters and side dishes. One planned offering is fugazetta, an Argentine pizza topped with caramelized onions and mozzarella.

An all-day egg menu will include steak and eggs, omelets and scrambles, with some dishes receiving additions such as caviar or shaved truffles.

Albisu also plans to revive several familiar dishes, including his burger and a rolled skirt steak served with chimichurri. The skirt steak previously helped him defeat Bobby Flay in a televised cooking competition.

## A butcher shop inside the restaurant

The concept reflects Albisu’s childhood in Northern Virginia, where he spent time in his mother’s Latin grocery store and butcher shop in Falls Church. His early exposure to meat cutting and grilling later influenced his culinary career.

At Electric Bull, an in-house butcher counter will display dry-aged steaks, specialty cuts and sausages made on the premises. Diners will also be able to buy meats, marinades and rubs for grilling at home.

Butcher and charcuterie specialist Justin Severino and chef Adam Hoffa, who previously led kitchens in Arlington, are joining Albisu on the culinary team. Their work will include house-made charcuterie, inventive meat preparations and live-fire cooking.

Electric Bull’s interior is planned as a moody, energetic space that remains welcoming rather than stuffy. Albisu’s goal is to create a restaurant Vienna residents can visit regularly for dinner and drinks—not simply reserve for an annual celebration.
