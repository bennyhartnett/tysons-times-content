Falls Church Mayor Letty Hardi says recent White House decisions are affecting her city despite its location eight miles from Washington, D.C., with federal employment uncertainty already influencing local economic and budget trends.

Speaking Aug. 16, Hardi expressed concern about the federal policing takeover in Washington and the deployment of troops in the capital. She called the federal government’s actions an “overreach” and questioned whether crime was the true reason for them.

“As a data person, I’ve looked at the statistics and data, and yes, there’s crime in D.C., but it’s actually at one of the lowest rates it’s ever been,” Hardi said. “I fear this is mainly a distraction from everything else that’s going on.”

Hardi urged Falls Church residents to respond by participating in local elections, following budget decisions, volunteering and becoming more involved in civic life.

“Take action,” she said, describing local engagement as an “antidote to anxiety” during broader political turmoil.

## Federal data concerns reach Falls Church

Hardi also raised alarms about firings at the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and indications that the Trump administration could halt the monthly publication of federal jobs reports.

“You can’t make good decisions without good data, and you have to be able to trust that the data is objective,” she said. Hardi added that political influence over government statistics could undermine public confidence in the information officials use to make decisions.

The employment data is particularly important in Northern Virginia, where federal workforce changes can quickly ripple through local economies. Hardi said Falls Church’s unemployment rate has increased and city officials are closely watching residents who have lost jobs or fear they may be affected.

That uncertainty is beginning to show up in city revenue, she said. Sales and meals tax collections have started to level off, potentially limiting government programs, influencing budget decisions and affecting the city’s ability to support residents.

## Protests draw praise from mayor

Hardi welcomed the turnout at recent No Kings demonstrations in Falls Church, saying the city should remain a place where people can exercise their First Amendment rights.

She described protests along East Broad Street and North and South Washington streets as both a response to national events and an opportunity to strengthen community ties.

“It was a way to bring people together and for them to know they’re not alone,” Hardi said.

Her closing message to residents was brief: “Stay focused and stay strong.”
