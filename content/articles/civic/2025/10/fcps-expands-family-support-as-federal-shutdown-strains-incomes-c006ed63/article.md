Fairfax County Public Schools said Oct. 1 that it is prepared to help students and families affected by the federal government shutdown, which could disrupt incomes for the many federal workers and military personnel living in the county.

The school system pledged to maintain a safe, stable routine for students while connecting families with meal assistance, virtual mental health care and local financial and housing resources.

Families experiencing income interruptions because of furloughs, layoffs or job losses may immediately apply for free or reduced-price school meals. Applications are accepted throughout the school year, and newly eligible households can qualify under federal income guidelines.

Once approved, meal benefits remain in place for the rest of the school year and up to 30 school days into the next school year, even if the household’s income later changes.

Students can also receive free virtual mental health sessions from home through FCPS’ partnership with Hazel Health. The service provides support for concerns including stress, anxiety and worry that may intensify during the shutdown.

Families facing financial hardship can consult the Fairfax County Human Services Resource Guide for food, financial and housing assistance available through the county and local organizations.

Low-income residents ages 18 to 65 may also qualify for half-price Fairfax Connector fares through the Transit Ridership Incentive Program.

Other free county resources remain available. FCPS students have a Library Equity Access Pass through Fairfax County Public Libraries, allowing them to borrow as many as three items for six weeks. County parks and recreation areas are also open and free to residents.

Superintendent Dr. Michelle C. Reid said FCPS will continue providing students with a dependable learning environment while the shutdown continues.
