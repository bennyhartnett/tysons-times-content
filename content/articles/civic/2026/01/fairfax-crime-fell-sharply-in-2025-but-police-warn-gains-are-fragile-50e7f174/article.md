Fairfax County recorded declines across several major crime categories in 2025, including homicides, robberies, burglaries and vehicle thefts. Police Chief Kevin Davis credited new technology, specialized enforcement teams, improved staffing and a sustained focus on dangerous driving.

Homicides dropped 15% from 2024, with 12 murders reported during the year. Davis said police made arrests in every homicide case.

Nonfatal shootings involving contact fell 37%, while robberies declined 20%. The county’s results mirrored broader reductions in violent crime across the region and nationwide.

Davis cautioned that suspects closely watch how law enforcement operates, including its expanding use of public cameras. He said police strategies must continue evolving as people committing crimes adapt to surveillance and enforcement tactics.

Vehicle thefts decreased 19%, and burglaries fell 28%. Davis pointed to the department’s full-time auto crime enforcement detectives as one factor in those reductions. He said vehicle thefts and home break-ins can profoundly damage residents’ sense of privacy.

Retail theft also reversed a multiyear upward trend. Shoplifting offenses declined 13% countywide and 22% in Tysons, where a dedicated urban team patrols the entire community rather than focusing exclusively on the mall.

Davis characterized much of the problem as organized retail crime rather than isolated thefts of inexpensive necessities. He said those offenses increasingly operate across Fairfax County, the region and the country.

Traffic deaths showed another significant decline. Fatal motor vehicle deaths fell 57% in 2025. Although pedestrian deaths rose seasonally as daylight hours shortened, fatal pedestrian incidents finished the year down 20% overall.

Opioid deaths declined 30%. The department has full-time detectives assigned specifically to fatal and nonfatal overdoses.

At the same time, arrests for driving while intoxicated increased by about 7%. Police expanded campaigns targeting speeding, unsafe lane changes, tailgating and distracted driving.

Davis said the purpose of traffic enforcement is to change dangerous behavior, not generate citation revenue. He also argued that visible traffic enforcement can help prevent other serious offenses by increasing the likelihood that armed suspects or people driving stolen vehicles encounter police.

Fairfax County police ended the year with a 3% vacancy rate, the department’s lowest in years. That staffing level has allowed all eight district stations to operate neighborhood policing teams that monitor local trends and respond to crime hot spots.

Hundreds of residents and businesses have also given police access to their camera systems. Davis said business checks and regular communication with school resource officers have contributed to the broader decline.

Despite the favorable numbers, Davis warned against assuming the trend will continue without sustained effort.

“Just because we and others in the region and across the country had a really successful, in terms of data and numbers, 2025, that doesn’t mean that 2026 is going to be an automatic,” he said.
