Fairfax County Executive Bryan Hill expects to unveil a plan within weeks that could consolidate publicly supported organizations promoting Tysons, tourism, volunteerism and other community interests.

Hill told the Board of Supervisors’ Budget Policy Committee on March 24 that his recommendations were ready and should reach supervisors within two to three weeks—earlier than the summer deadline established by the board.

Supervisors unanimously directed Hill in early February to examine possible consolidation of Visit Fairfax, Volunteer Fairfax and the Tysons Community Alliance. The review also extends to other organizations performing similar work with county tax dollars.

Providence District Supervisor Dalia Palchik, who developed the directive, noted that Hill appeared to be ahead of schedule.

Board Chairman Jeff McKay said combining organizations could reduce overhead and make their operations more agile. While describing their work as valuable, he said the county must determine whether it can be performed more efficiently.

The groups operate independently but receive some local government funding. McKay acknowledged that restructuring organizations with separate leaders could face resistance, but said Fairfax County’s difficult budget conditions warrant serious consideration.

Other supervisors supported exploring the options. Dranesville District Supervisor Jimmy Bierman called for a more strategic approach to organizations with potentially overlapping duties, while Mount Vernon District Supervisor Dan Storck said the county should seek better results from its spending.

Supervisors are expected to review the recommendations during the spring and summer. A tentative discussion is scheduled for September as the county begins preparing its fiscal year 2028 budget.

## Volunteer Fairfax Seeks Another $100,000

The consolidation discussion comes as county staff recommends giving Volunteer Fairfax an additional $100,000 from money identified during a fiscal year 2026 budget review.

Staff said the nonprofit’s fundraising fell below expectations and that the county funding would help close the gap. Sully District Supervisor Kathy Smith raised concerns because Volunteer Fairfax has reported fundraising shortfalls for two consecutive years, saying the situation deserves closer examination.

Supervisors are scheduled to consider the $100,000 allocation on April 28 alongside other proposals for spending funds available after the county’s third-quarter financial review.

Fairfax County collected more than $51 million beyond projections made when the fiscal year 2026 budget was adopted last May. Staff attributed the additional revenue to personal property tax collections, investment interest and cautious forecasting.

A related consolidation is already underway. Hill’s advertised fiscal year 2027 budget, proposed Feb. 17, cuts $750,000 from Celebrate Fairfax as the organization merges into Visit Fairfax.

Celebrate Fairfax previously organized the annual Celebrate Fairfax! Festival. After that event ended following the pandemic, the nonprofit shifted to smaller programs, including local events marking the United States’ 250th anniversary.
