Fairfax County is highlighting its pivotal place in American history as the nation’s 250th birthday approaches, with events honoring the Virginia Declaration of Rights and programs expected to continue into 2027.

The county’s Board of Supervisors voted unanimously on June 9 to designate June as “Declaration Day,” marking 250 years since the Virginia Convention of Delegates adopted the Declaration of Rights on June 12, 1776.

Written at least partly by Fairfax County statesman George Mason, the document declared that political power comes from the people and outlined inherent rights including life, liberty, property, happiness and safety.

Mount Vernon District Supervisor Dan Storck said the declaration is inseparable from the story of America’s 250th anniversary.

Dranesville District Supervisor Jimmy Bierman read Article 1 of the document during the meeting and noted its similarities to language Thomas Jefferson used in the Declaration of Independence, adopted less than a month later.

Christopher Eck, executive director of George Mason’s Gunston Hall, acknowledged the influence with a quip: “Tom did copy a bit.”

Gunston Hall in Mason Neck plans to commemorate the independence era with several events, including Declaration Day celebrations on June 12 and 13.

The 17-member Fairfax County 250th Commission is also creating, supporting and promoting events that examine the past while looking toward the future. Commission Chair Elizabeth Maurer told supervisors that an extensive schedule is planned for June, July and the remainder of the year.

Board Chair Jeff McKay praised the commission’s efforts to publicize Fairfax County’s history and connect residents with local commemorations.

After the county’s July 4 events, the commission intends to form another committee focused on developing legacy programs for 2027.
