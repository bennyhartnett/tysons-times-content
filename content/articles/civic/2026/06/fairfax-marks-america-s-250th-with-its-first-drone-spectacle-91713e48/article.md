Fairfax County will launch its first drone light show Saturday, June 27, as Providence District begins celebrating the nation’s 250th anniversary alongside a packed schedule of government updates, environmental initiatives and summer events.

The Lake Fairfax 250th Independence Day Celebration will replace traditional fireworks with hundreds of synchronized drones forming patriotic images to music above Lake Fairfax Park. The event, part of Experience Fairfax 250, will also feature live music, food trucks and family activities, while visitors can enjoy the park’s trails, boating and camping facilities.

## Spotted lanternflies return

Spotted lanternflies are again appearing across the area, currently in their nymph stage. The glossy, white-spotted insects are about the size of a pea but will grow, develop red coloring and gain wings by mid-to-late summer.

Residents can help limit the invasive species by recording sightings through the iNaturalist app, stomping or vacuuming the insects and scraping egg masses from trees, fences, outdoor furniture and vehicles.

The insects do not bite or sting people or pets, but they threaten trees and native habitats.

## Two bond questions headed to voters

The Fairfax County Board of Supervisors approved two bond referenda totaling $255 million for the Nov. 3, 2026, ballot.

A proposed $180 million parks bond would pay for recreation center renovations, accessibility upgrades, park development, land acquisition and open-space preservation.

The other referendum would provide $75 million for community health and human services facilities. Planned investments include modernizing the Public Health Laboratory and renovating the Tim Harmon Campus, with the goal of supporting round-the-clock services as demand for care rises.

The board also recognized Fairfax County Public Schools teams and athletes who won Virginia High School League championships during the 2026 spring season.

A separate housekeeping amendment would align Fairfax County’s traffic and motor-vehicle code with state law.

## Tysons contribution rate increases

The public-facilities contribution expected from new Tysons rezoning applications will rise from $2.28 to $2.34 per square foot of gross floor area on July 1, 2026.

The inflation-adjusted contribution helps finance public facilities needed as Tysons develops.

## Federal changes strain local benefit programs

Fairfax County officials are preparing for federal policy changes affecting the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program and Medicaid.

Beginning Oct. 1, 2026, the federal share of SNAP administrative costs will fall from 50% to 25%. That change is expected to leave Fairfax County with a $6.7 million revenue loss in fiscal 2027, which the county has already incorporated into its budget.

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, Medicaid eligibility redeterminations must occur every six months instead of annually. The board previously authorized 13 positions to manage the additional workload.

County staff also presented a four-phase plan to convert School Age Child Care sites to the Beyond the Bell model by the 2029-30 school year. Neighborhood and Community Services plans to seek funding for 18 additional positions through the fiscal 2026 carryover review for the first phase. Staff said revenue from serving more children should fully offset the expansion costs.

## Community notices and opportunities

Providence District has vacancies on the Human Services Council and Information Technology Policy Advisory Committee. Interested residents may contact Magali Ceballos for information.

County officials are warning residents about false advertisements promoting shredding events at county facilities.

Fairfax County is also reviewing a possible zoning amendment involving accessory living units. Virtual community meetings are scheduled for July 15 and July 20.

Fairfax Connector service changes take effect June 27. Some routes will have reduced service or be discontinued, though officials say many affected riders will retain access to nearby routes and stops offering Metrorail connections.

During the summer, individuals may use school blacktops, playgrounds, outdoor basketball and tennis courts, and tracks without reservations. The availability does not extend to organized groups.

## Pollinator pathways take root

Residents can join an initiative intended to create connected stretches of insecticide-free yards, parkland and homeowners association property planted with or preserving native species.

Plant NOVA Natives is offering 7.5-inch metal Pollinator Pathway signs to participants working toward contiguous or nearly contiguous habitats. Existing pathways are concentrated mainly in Oakton, Clifton, Arlington and Alexandria. Participants may add their properties to a regional map, but doing so is not required.

Fairfax County also announced the winners of the Sustain Fairfax Earth Month Youth Art Contest. Student artwork interpreting the theme “My Actions, Our Climate” highlighted steps including conserving energy, reducing waste and planting trees.

## Music, movies and food fill the summer calendar

Nottoway Nights will bring concerts to Nottoway Park from 7:30 to 8:30 p.m. Thursdays, July 2 through Aug. 20.

Arts in the Parks will run from 10 to 10:45 a.m. Sunday mornings at Strawberry Park in the Mosaic District from June 28 through Aug. 9, excluding the Fourth of July weekend.

The Mosaic Summer Music Series will take place at Strawberry Park from 7 to 8 p.m. Saturdays through Aug. 15, also pausing for the holiday weekend.

Movies in Strawberry Park will return with outdoor screenings of blockbusters and family films. Shows begin at 7 p.m. and are scheduled for rain or shine unless otherwise announced. Visitors should bring chairs or blankets.

Capital One Center’s second annual Culinary Week is scheduled for July 27-31. Seven ticketed events at the destination’s restaurants will raise awareness and money for World Central Kitchen, the crisis-response food nonprofit founded by chef José Andrés in 2010. The inaugural Culinary Week raised more than $10,000 for the organization.
