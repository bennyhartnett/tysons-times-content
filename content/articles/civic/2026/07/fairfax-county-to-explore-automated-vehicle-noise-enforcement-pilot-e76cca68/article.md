The Fairfax County Board of Supervisors voted unanimously July 14 to direct County Executive Bryan Hill to report by fall on a timeline for implementing automated vehicle-noise monitoring.

The state-authorized equipment may detect vehicle noise above 95 decibels. "This is not a trivial matter," Board Chairman Jeff McKay said. "This is absolutely a quality-of-life issue."

Governor Abigail Spanberger signed the 2026 enabling legislation, which was sponsored by Delegate Rip Sullivan. Sullivan secured passage of a similar bill in 2025, but then-Governor Glenn Youngkin vetoed it, saying that "allowing only certain localities to use noise cameras with specific decibel limits would create inconsistent enforcement and confusion for motorists across the state."

The legislation authorizes a two-year pilot that participating localities must end June 30, 2028, unless the General Assembly extends their authority. A vehicle operator cited through the program may face a civil penalty of up to $100.

A conspicuous warning sign must be placed within 1,000 feet of each monitoring device, and localities must publish all device locations online. Automated vehicle-noise violations will not be reported to the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles or insurance agencies. Fine revenue must be used either to offset program operating costs or to support general transportation-safety initiatives.

Fairfax County police issued 1,185 loud or altered vehicle-exhaust citations in 2025 and 662 more through early July 2026. "Officers cannot be everywhere at once," McKay said.

Fairfax supervisors are expected to consider program implementation details at an autumn meeting of the Board's Safety and Security Committee. The county has not approved device locations, a vendor, program costs, or a launch date.
