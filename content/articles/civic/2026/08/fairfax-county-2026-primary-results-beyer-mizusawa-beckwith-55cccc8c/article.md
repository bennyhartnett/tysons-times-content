Fairfax County voters chose nominees in Democratic and Republican congressional and U.S. Senate primaries Aug. 4, setting up three Nov. 3 general-election contests while reported results remain unofficial pending certification.

Don Beyer won the Democratic nomination in Virginia’s 8th Congressional District and will face Republican Tony Sabio in November. Bert Mizusawa won the Republican U.S. Senate nomination and will face incumbent Sen. Mark Warner. Dave Beckwith won the Republican nomination in the 10th Congressional District and will face incumbent Rep. Suhas Subramanyam.

The Associated Press called Beyer’s race at 7:34 p.m., Mizusawa’s Senate primary at 7:29 p.m. and Beckwith’s 10th District contest later that evening. Beyer received about 70% of reported Fairfax County Democratic votes with 78 of 83 precincts reporting in the figures cited. Mizusawa received about 47% of Republican votes with all 265 county precincts reporting, while Beckwith received about 66% with all eight relevant precincts reporting.

More than 20,000 Democratic ballots and more than 17,000 Republican ballots were counted in Fairfax County, according to the supplied results report.

“With so much at stake for our community and our country at this moment, I am grateful to Northern Virginians for again making me their Democratic nominee to fight for Virginia’s 8th District,” Beyer said.

Mizusawa said his campaign would now turn toward the general election. “We campaigned hard across Virginia and thanks to you, we now move on to defeat Senator Mark Warner,” he said.

Warner also addressed the November contest, saying, “I’m proud to be on the ballot alongside this exceptional Democratic ticket, and I know that if we knock doors, make calls, and organize, we can hold this Senate seat.”

Virginia does not register voters by party, allowing voters to choose either a Democratic or Republican primary. The only Democratic congressional primary in Fairfax County was in the 8th District; the 10th and 11th districts did not have Democratic primaries in the county.

Turnout was limited early in the day. As of noon Aug. 4, 0.38% of registered 8th District voters had voted in person and 4.28% had voted early. In the Republican primaries countywide, 0.39% of registered voters had voted in person and 0.80% had voted early.

All 265 Fairfax County precincts were scheduled to remain open until 7 p.m. Same-day registration was available through Election Day using provisional ballots subject to post-election verification. Mail ballots could be returned by 7 p.m. Aug. 4, mailed if postmarked that day and received by noon Aug. 7, or placed in drop boxes.

The U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division planned to send election monitors to Fairfax County and sought information about the county’s bilingual election program for Spanish- and Vietnamese-speaking voters.
