Pamela Huffman transforms painful family history into layered symbols of resilience in “In the Absence of Nurture… Nature,” a solo exhibition running through June 15 at Rare Bird Coffee Roasters in Falls Church.

The mixed-media and acrylic artist explores adoption, a sibling’s death, divorce, abandonment and the desire to belong within a family coming apart. Alongside those experiences, Huffman celebrates the natural elements that comforted her during difficult periods, including trees, stones, flowers, water, sunsets and summer warmth.

Love remained present in her childhood home, Huffman said, but shame, trauma and grief left family members unable to provide consistent attachment and nurturing. She found refuge in nature instead, an emotional journey that became the foundation of the exhibition.

Two pieces honor her husband, Michael, whom she credits with providing the nurturing she had long sought.

Huffman became deeply involved in making art in 2018. Her early abstract acrylic work followed intuitive impulses, but her practice gradually became more deliberate as she incorporated old family photographs, documents and other personal artifacts into mixed-media compositions.

“The Work of a Lifetime,” the final piece she created for the show, reflects her effort to interrupt generational trauma and give her two children what she felt was missing from her family of origin.

Its flowers carry specific meanings: ivy represents attachment, eucalyptus protection, Queen Anne’s lace sanctuary and dogwood the belief that love can overcome adversity. Huffman said she now looks forward to seeing which symbolic flowers her children might offer future generations.

Another work, “Family Secrets,” gives physical form to the burdens she has carried. Huffman wrote down secrets from her family’s past and sewed them into small fabric packets. She arranged the sachets over a forest image with pieces of porcelain berry vine gathered near her home—an invasive plant capable of overwhelming a forest when left unchecked.

Carissa at Tint Maker Space helped Huffman select fabrics for the piece. A friend and neighbor also contributed keys from her personal collection, which Huffman concealed throughout the work.

Seeing community members engage with such intimate material has been emotional for Huffman. During a March 18 visit to Rare Bird, she described how much it meant to identify herself as the artist when approached by another customer. That feeling of connection, she agreed, stands in sharp contrast to the abandonment explored throughout the show.

Beyond her own practice, Huffman works as a curator and arts organizer. As chair of the Future Planning Committee at Falls Church Arts, she has facilitated more than 30 exhibitions and assists with juror outreach. She is also a co-founder of Lift Creative Collective, which supports female artists in the Washington area.

In 2024, Huffman received a grant to serve as Curating Artist in Residence at Idylwood Studios in Falls Church. She is also training to become a docent at the Smithsonian American Art Museum and its Renwick Gallery.

“In the Absence of Nurture… Nature” opened March 16 and continues through June 15 at Rare Bird Coffee Roasters, 230 W. Broad St. The Falls Church café presents four solo exhibitions by local artists each year.
