Women rebuilding their lives after incarceration will put their creative work on display April 9 during Guest House’s annual art show at Principle Gallery on King Street.

About half of Guest House’s current residents are participating in the 6-8 p.m. exhibition, according to Katie Shapiro. This year’s theme is “metamorphosis,” reflecting the major adjustments residents face after leaving prison.

Guest House provides a six-month residential program for newly released women convicted of nonviolent offenses. Residents must establish new routines, attend classes, try unfamiliar activities and build relationships as they prepare for life outside the program.

The art show gives them a chance to discover what brings them joy, Shapiro said. Past exhibitions have included paintings, sculptures, photography, poetry and written music presented with recordings.

For one resident, the program revived a long-dormant interest in poetry. Another was struck by the prospect of seeing her work displayed in a gallery beside pieces valued at thousands of dollars.

One participating artist is also using the experience to develop professional connections and prepare for an art career after leaving Guest House. Shapiro said the artist encourages quieter residents to take part while continuing to refine her own skills.

An art therapy intern from George Washington University supports the residents for five to 10 hours each week. She leads open studio sessions at the residential houses and helps women use art to decompress after stressful days.

The exhibition will be held at Principle Gallery, 208 King Street. Established in 1994, the gallery features emerging, mid-career and established artists with distinctive approaches.
