A traveling museum exploring Virginia’s central—and often complicated—role in the nation’s founding has opened at Vienna Elementary School.

The VA250 Mobile Museum began its three-day visit with a ribbon-cutting ceremony at 10 a.m. Thursday, July 10, 2025. The expandable tractor-trailer is stationed in the school parking lot at 128 Center Street South and will welcome visitors from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily through Saturday, July 12.

Its “Out of Many, One” exhibit examines subjects ranging from the Declaration of Independence to the wealth, geography and diverse population that distinguished Virginia among England’s 13 colonies.

Interactive displays and artifacts also highlight women’s contributions to the Revolution and confront the contradiction of Founding Fathers who championed liberty while enslaving people. The broader experience focuses on Virginia’s continuing influence on the country’s pursuit of democracy and equality.

The museum’s visit is part of Vienna’s fifth annual Liberty Amendments Month, which began June 14 with a Juneteenth parade and celebration. Established by the town in 2021, the observance commemorates the 13th, 14th, 15th and 19th amendments, which abolished slavery and expanded rights involving citizenship and voting.

Closing-weekend activities also include a Crooked Sparrow concert at 6:30 p.m. Friday on the Town Green and a final opportunity to view educational signs installed around Vienna.

The mobile museum is traveling statewide as Virginia marks the 250th anniversary of the American Revolutionary War. Organized by the VA250 Commission, it is scheduled to return to Fairfax County from Aug. 31 through Sept. 3 at George Mason’s Gunston Hall, 10709 Gunston Road in Mason Neck.

Those programs, along with events being developed by the Fairfax County 250th Commission, are building toward the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence’s adoption on July 4, 2026.
