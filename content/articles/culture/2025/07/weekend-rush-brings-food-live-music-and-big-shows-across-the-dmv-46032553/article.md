## An untold chapter of computing history

Author and internet law professor Kathy Kleiman will discuss and sign copies of *Proving Ground: The Untold Story of the Six Women Who Programmed the World’s First Modern Computer* from 2 to 4 p.m. July 12 at Mary Riley Styles Public Library. The program, held in the upper-level conference room, is intended for teens and adults.

The book recounts how the U.S. Army recruited young women with mathematics backgrounds during World War II to calculate artillery trajectories by hand. Six of those women were later assigned to program ENIAC, an experimental general-purpose electronic computer, despite having no manual or programming language to guide them.

Kleiman knew four of the six original ENIAC programmers and recorded extensive oral histories about their work. Her 2014 documentary, *The Computers: The Remarkable Story of the ENIAC Programmers*, won the 2016 UNAFF Grand Jury Award for best short documentary. *Proving Ground* received the IEEE William and Joyce Middleton Electrical Engineering History Award in 2023 and a Sejong Book Award in 2024.

## Comedy, pop and theater take the stage

The hosts of the LOL Podcast — Cash, Kate, Harper and Maverick — bring their Laugh Out Loud World Tour to Warner Theatre, 513 13th St. NW, at 3:30 p.m. Saturday, July 12. The live show includes games, personal stories and material not heard on the podcast. Tickets start at $40, with some packages offering meet-and-greet access or a pre-show gathering.

Mamma Mania!, a New York-based ABBA tribute group, performs Friday, July 11, at The Vault in Capital One Hall, 7750 Capital One Tower Road in Tysons. Doors open at 7 p.m. The show recreates the band’s 1970s sound with costumes and vocal harmonies.

Riki Lindhome’s *Dead Inside* begins preview performances July 9 at Woolly Mammoth Theatre, 641 D St. NW. Directed by Brian McElhaney and presented by Ali Wong and Bill Hader, the musical combines solo comedy, personal storytelling and original songs drawn from Lindhome’s experience with infertility.

Preview shows are scheduled for 8 p.m. July 9 through July 11, 2 and 8 p.m. July 12, and 2 and 8 p.m. July 13. Artist talkbacks will follow the July 17 and July 24 performances. The production contains explicit language and discussions of infertility, miscarriage, abortion and sexual acts.

Greg Kalleres’ comedy *Apropos of Nothing* opens July 12 at Keegan Theatre, 1742 Church St. NW, and runs through Aug. 10. The story follows five friends after Owen unexpectedly admits that he loves his best friend’s wife. Performances are generally at 8 p.m. Thursdays through Saturdays and 3 p.m. Sundays, with selected Monday and Wednesday shows. Tickets are $54 for adults and $44 for seniors, students and patrons under 25, plus a $5 service charge. Kalleres will join a free post-show discussion after the July 13 matinee.

Arena Stage’s world-premiere musical adaptation of *A Wrinkle in Time* continues through July 20 at 1101 Sixth St. SW. Based on Madeleine L’Engle’s Newbery Medal-winning novel, the production follows Meg Murry, Calvin and Charles Wallace on a journey through space to rescue Meg’s missing father.

*The Untitled Unauthorized Hunter S. Thompson Musical* enters its final weekend at Signature Theatre, 4200 Campbell Ave. in Arlington, with performances through July 13. Joe Iconis’ rock musical, directed by Christopher Ashley with choreography and musical staging by Jon Rua, traces Thompson’s life from the 1940s to 2005, including the creation of Gonzo journalism, his political battles and the consequences of his drug use.

The approximately two-hour, 35-minute production is recommended for audiences 18 and older. It includes adult language, drug use, violence, strobe lights, theatrical haze and gunshot and explosion sounds. A post-show discussion is scheduled after the 7:30 p.m. performance July 9.

## New and continuing art exhibitions

The second portion of the Falls Church Arts All-Member Show features 67 artists whose surnames begin with M through Z. It runs at 700-B W. Broad St. from July 12 into August, with a meet-the-artists reception from 7 to 9 p.m. Saturday, July 12.

Photographer Jessica Caso’s *Côte d’Azur Mon Amour* remains at Rare Bird Coffee Roasters, 230 W. Broad St., through Sept. 28. The exhibition presents images from Nice, Èze and other locations along the French Riviera.

*Neither/Nor*, a collaboration between George Mason University MFA candidates Traci Reynolds and Liz Johnson, is on display through Sept. 5 at Fenwick Gallery, 4400 University Drive in Fairfax. Using photography and handmade paper, the artists explore the body as a nonconforming and changing space.

Johnson’s self-portrait series *Ripe and Rot* pairs her body with decaying fruit to consider fertility, time and physical transformation. Reynolds’ large handmade-paper sculpture *Raise* incorporates hair and latex while examining trans and non-gender-conforming bodies through ideas drawn from body horror, identity, protection and reconstruction.

Reston Art Gallery’s eighth annual Invitational Show continues through July 27 at 11400 Washington Plaza West. It includes original work by gallery members and their invited artists.

*Vivian Browne: My Kind of Protest* remains at the Phillips Collection through Sept. 28. The exhibition brings together paintings, prints, works on paper and archival material from Browne’s four-decade career. Browne helped establish the Black Emergency Cultural Coalition, belonged to the SOHO20 women’s art cooperative and taught at Rutgers University from 1971 to 1992.

At the Smithsonian American Art Museum, Shahzia Sikander’s 10-minute animated work *The Last Post* runs continuously through July 12, 2026. The 2010 piece uses Indo-Persian miniature-painting traditions, Indian court architecture, Chinese cut-paper silhouettes and a map of South Asia to examine the British East India Company and the legacy of colonial power. Its soundtrack is by Pulitzer Prize-winning composer Du Yun. Admission is free.

ArtRave’s Dupont Artisan Market will run from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. Saturday, July 12, at Dupont Circle, 1913 Massachusetts Ave. NW. Local vendors will offer paintings, sculptures, carved goods, jewelry, baked products, home décor and other handmade items. The market returns every other Saturday.

## Lotus blooms and one last shot into the water

A guided Lotus Bloom Nature Therapy walk will take place from 9 to 11 a.m. July 12 at Meadowlark Botanical Garden, 9750 Meadowlark Gardens Court in Vienna. Certified nature and forest therapy guide Ilana Rubin will lead adults along pond and woodland paths for sensory exercises, quiet reflection and tea.

The group will cover approximately one mile or less at a relaxed pace over paved and unpaved terrain. The program costs $35, requires advance registration and proceeds in rain unless conditions become hazardous. Participants should meet at the visitor center and arrive 10 to 15 minutes early.

The Wharf’s Waterfront Driving Range holds its final session from 6 to 9 p.m. July 9 at Transit Pier. Splash City Golf supplies clubs and certified biodegradable balls, allowing participants to aim at a floating target in the Washington Channel.
