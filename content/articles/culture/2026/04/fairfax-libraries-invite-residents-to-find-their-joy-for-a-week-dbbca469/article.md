Fairfax County Public Library will celebrate National Library Week from April 19-25, 2026, with free activities, special observances and an invitation for residents to discover something they love at their local branch.

This year’s theme, “Find Your Joy,” reflects the upbeat message of honorary chair Mychal Threets, an award-winning librarian, author and new host of the children’s program “Reading Rainbow.” Threets frequently shares the many ways libraries can inspire happiness, connection and confidence.

His debut picture book, *I’m So Happy You’re Here: A Celebration of Library Joy*, was published earlier this year in English and Spanish and is available to borrow from Fairfax County Public Library.

Throughout the week, visitors can share what they appreciate about their library by filling out a paper flower at an FCPL branch. The flowers will become part of a colorful garden display.

Residents can also explore the library’s free books and audiobooks, programs for all ages, online courses, storytimes, MakerLabs, language groups and classes, author events and volunteer opportunities. The system’s Library of Things allows cardholders to borrow useful items beyond traditional reading materials.

Several national observances are planned during the week:

- Monday, April 20, is Right to Read Day, when readers and library supporters are encouraged to defend and celebrate the freedom to read. The American Library Association will release its State of America’s Libraries Report and list of the 10 most challenged books.
- Tuesday, April 21, is National Library Workers Day, recognizing the contributions of library employees.
- Wednesday, April 22, is National Library Outreach Day, formerly National Bookmobile Day, honoring professionals who bring library services directly to patrons.
- Thursday, April 23, is Take Action for Libraries Day, focused on rallying public support for libraries.

National Library Week dates to concerns in the 1950s that Americans were spending less on books and more on radios, televisions and musical instruments. The American Library Association and American Book Publishers formed the National Book Committee in 1954 to encourage reading and promote its benefits.

The committee developed the National Library Week initiative in 1957, based on the belief that people inspired to read would use and support libraries. The first celebration followed in 1958 under the theme “Wake Up and Read!”
