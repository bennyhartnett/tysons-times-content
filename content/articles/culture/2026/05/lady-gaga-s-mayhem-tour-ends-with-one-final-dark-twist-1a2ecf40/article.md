Lady Gaga closed her yearlong MAYHEM tour in New York City after 86 performances, capping the run with the announcement of *MAYHEM Requiem*, a concert film and live album.

Built around her seventh studio album, the roughly two-and-a-half-hour production unfolded across four acts and a finale. Gaga framed the show as “The Art of Personal Chaos,” using gothic-romantic imagery to explore fame, identity and self-acceptance.

The opening act, “Of Velvet and Vice,” began with Gaga performing “Bloody Mary” atop a 15-foot bloodred dress. The costume opened to reveal a cage-like structure containing a dozen dancers, establishing the show’s eerie atmosphere.

Gaga descended from the dress during “Abracadabra” and led the dancers out of the cage before moving into “Judas” and “Poker Face.” The act also introduced the Mistress of Mayhem, a character embodying Gaga’s public image and complicated relationship with fame.

That conflict intensified during Act II, “And She Fell Into a Gothic Dream.” Songs including “Disease,” “The Beast,” “Perfect Celebrity” and “Paparazzi” examined the divide between Gaga and the identity she performs for the public. Sand pits, crutches and other theatrical props reinforced the production’s portrayal of the pain and effort behind celebrity.

“Killah” opened Act III, “The Beautiful Nightmare That Knows Her Name,” as the story shifted toward acceptance. Rather than resisting her inner disorder, Gaga began embracing it through energetic performances of “LoveDrug,” “Applause” and “The Dead Dance.”

The reconciliation reached its peak in Act IV, “Every Chessboard Has Two Queens.” During ballads such as “Million Reasons” and “Shallow,” Gaga and the Mistress of Mayhem finally came together, presenting the two identities as inseparable parts of the same person.

Elaborate scenery, dramatic costumes and tightly coordinated dancers carried the story throughout the production, while Gaga’s forceful vocals anchored its biggest emotional moments.

*MAYHEM Requiem* brings the tour’s theatrical spectacle to theaters while also serving as a farewell to the MAYHEM era. With the tour finished, Gaga is stepping back from the road and turning toward other creative projects.
