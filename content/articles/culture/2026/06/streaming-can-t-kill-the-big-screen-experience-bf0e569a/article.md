Streaming services have made staying home easier than ever, but convenience has not erased what makes a trip to the movies special. Netflix, Disney+, Hulu and Max may offer thousands of choices from the couch, yet they cannot fully reproduce the immersion and shared excitement of a theater.

At home, viewers can pause a film, check their phones, answer messages or tackle chores. A theater’s enormous screen, powerful sound and darkened room make distractions less tempting and allow the movie to command the audience’s attention.

The crowd also becomes part of the experience. Laughter, gasps and cheers can turn major scenes into lasting memories.

“I love to see the reactions of strangers around me,” junior Eva Braccia said. “It just makes the overall experience more exciting.”

Going to the movies can also bring friends, families and couples together. Even when a film disappoints, the outing itself can still be memorable—and the conversation afterward may be part of the fun.

“You can bond over the experience together and if the movie had a large impact on you, it’s fun to talk about it after with your friends,” freshman Mia Winn said.

Cost remains one of the strongest arguments against theaters. Tickets have risen substantially in price over the years, while candy, popcorn and drinks can make the total even steeper. Compared with a streaming subscription that provides extensive entertainment at home, one theater visit may feel expensive.

“The price of a ticket usually makes me nervous to go to a movie because I don’t want to spend so much money on a ticket or candy,” sophomore Jude Murray said. “I usually see going to the theater as more of a treat.”

Still, audiences routinely pay for experiences they cannot recreate at home, including concerts, sporting events, amusement parks and vacations. A movie ticket similarly buys more than access to a film—it provides an event and the memories surrounding it.

Major releases continue to attract millions of theatergoers each year, showing that many viewers would rather see certain films on the big screen than wait for streaming.

“I think the movie theater experience is appealing because it’s fun to get out of the house and relax in the theater with a slushie and popcorn,” sophomore Savannah Alton said. “It’s also fun to be around people who are experiencing it at the same time.”

Streaming is convenient and firmly established, but it has not replaced the anticipation of buying a ticket, settling into a seat, watching previews and seeing a story unfold on a massive screen. As entertainment technology changes, theaters remain worthwhile because they offer immersion, excitement and connection that the living room cannot quite duplicate.
