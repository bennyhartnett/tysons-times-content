A sprawling Lego recreation of Fairfax County landmarks has taken over a section of Tysons Corner Center, giving shoppers an unexpected tour of the region's most recognizable sites—all rendered in colorful plastic bricks.

The Northern Virginia Lego User Group unveiled the installation Wednesday near the mall's future Dick's House of Sport location as part of Fairfax County's 250th anniversary celebrations. The display will remain through January 2027.

## From Great Falls to the Metro

NOVA-LUG's builders spent months constructing custom recreations of sites spanning all nine county districts. The Smithsonian's Udvar-Hazy Center rises in miniature beside Great Falls Park. The Historic Fairfax Courthouse stands near Lake Accotink. Even the Franconia-Springfield Metro platform got the brick treatment.

The display doesn't stop at famous landmarks. Tiny Lego figures play pickleball, cycle along the Washington & Old Dominion Trail, and grab drinks at a pint-sized Bunnyman Brewing.

"They have pieces representing all nine districts, so when people see this, they see the array of attractions we have for residents and visitors," said Visit Fairfax President and CEO Barry Biggar.

## A Project Born From Anniversary Planning

The collaboration began when organizers working on the county's semiquincentennial events approached a NOVA-LUG member who had previously partnered with the county. Work officially started in March, with the county providing a list of suggested sites while giving builders creative freedom.

"I think there's even one or two of the landmarks we chose to build that wasn't on that original list," said NOVA-LUG President Tanner Johnson, who coordinated the project with member Ben Rome. Grist Mill Park in Mount Vernon made the cut after builders pitched the idea themselves.

Tysons Corner Center emerged as the natural home for the display. The mall draws more than 20 million visitors annually, making it one of the county's busiest destinations.

## Living Display Will Change With the Seasons

NOVA-LUG plans to keep the installation fresh by updating smaller scenes to reflect holidays and seasonal changes. Builders hope to add different housing styles found throughout the county, along with Halloween and Christmas decorations.

"We'll do a lot of those micro-adjustments to really show and celebrate life in the county," Johnson said.

What happens after January remains uncertain. Some county supervisors have expressed interest in finding permanent homes for landmarks tied to their districts. Providence District Supervisor Dalia Palchik suggested the Fairfax County Government Center as a future stop.

Founded in 2024, NOVA-LUG has more than 35 members who gather for monthly meetings and public events at parks and libraries. Johnson sees the 250th anniversary display as an extension of the group's mission to promote creativity at any age.

"We want to encourage everybody to find the different things that are meaningful to them and allow them to continue playing and exercising that creativity," he said.
