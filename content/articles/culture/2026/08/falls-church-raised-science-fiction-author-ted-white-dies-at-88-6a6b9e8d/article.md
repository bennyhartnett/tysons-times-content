Theodore Edwin “Ted” White, a science-fiction author and editor raised in Falls Church, died May 24, 2026, at age 88, according to a death confirmation cited by PulpFest and the Science Fiction Encyclopedia. The synthesis reports that White experienced health problems after a fall at his home; that account should be independently verified.

White later worked as a copy editor for the Falls Church News-Press. His career in science fiction included writing or co-writing numerous novels and stories, sometimes under the pseudonyms Ron Archer and Norman Edwards.

White’s first professionally published story was “Phoenix,” a 1963 collaboration with Marion Zimmer Bradley. That year, he also became assistant editor of The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction, a position he held through 1968.

His first novel, *Invasion from 2500*, was published in 1964 under the name Norman Edwards. Between 1964 and 1978, White wrote two science-fiction series and 11 stand-alone novels, including a Captain America novel. The publication counts and detailed career chronology should be independently checked before publication.

White co-chaired the 1967 World Science Fiction Convention in New York. In 1968, he won the Hugo Award for Best Fan Writer and began editing *Amazing Stories* and *Fantastic*. He edited both magazines from October 1968 through October 1978.

His work extended beyond fiction and magazine publishing. White was a jazz music critic, radio host and musician who played keyboards and saxophone. From 1977 to 1979, he hosted the Friday-afternoon Dr. Progresso radio show on WGTB-FM. He became editor of *Heavy Metal* in August 1979 and remained in that role until August 1980. In 1999, he became music editor of the Collecting Channel website.

White’s Falls Church connection remained part of a life that included science-fiction writing, editing, music criticism and broadcasting. Further verification is needed for the reported cause-of-death account and several detailed biographical claims.
