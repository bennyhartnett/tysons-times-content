The McLean Community Center will launch a year of 50th-anniversary festivities Saturday with a free celebration highlighting the local institution’s origins and early history.

The kickoff begins at 7 p.m. in the Alden Theatre, 1234 Ingleside Avenue. Local historian and former MCC Governing Board member Carole Herrick will discuss the decade-long community effort that led to the center’s establishment.

A commemorative anniversary video will premiere after Herrick’s presentation, followed by a reception. Before the program, the McLean Project for the Arts’ Emerson Gallery will open at 6 p.m. for visitors to see “Vibrant Matter: Works from Wood.”

The Friends of the McLean Community Center is hosting the event with support from the McLean Project for the Arts. Admission is free and open to the public, though attendees are asked to RSVP by emailing feedback@mcleancenter.org.

MCC Executive Director Betsy May-Salazar said a committee of current and former board members, residents and staff spent years preparing for the anniversary.

Plans include a mural at the Old Firehouse Center, 1440 Chain Bridge Road, and a sculpture for the plaza at MCC’s entrance. Other projects will feature a commemorative tree planting, a kiosk tracing the center’s history and an online “My MCC Memories” collection where community members can contribute photographs, stories and recollections.

The yearlong festivities will culminate Oct. 18 with a 50th Anniversary Community Celebration featuring music, food and activities representing each decade of MCC’s existence.

Opened in 1975, the community center emerged from 10 years of organizing, lobbying and fundraising by the group that became the Friends of the MCC. Its members helped identify and acquire the property, campaigned for funding through a special real estate tax surcharge approved by voters in 1970, and pushed for oversight by an elected governing board.

Today, MCC provides educational programs, cultural activities and community events, including the annual McLean Day festival. It also operates the Alden Theatre and the Old Firehouse Center for teens.
