Arlington Preparedness Month returns in April with a push for residents to review emergency plans, refresh supply kits and ensure their families, workplaces and communities are ready for a crisis.

“Preparedness is the foundation of building a resilient community,” said William Flagler Jr., director of the Department of Public Safety Communications and Emergency Management. He encouraged residents to take simple precautions now to help create a safer future.

Spring thunderstorms can produce damaging winds, tornadoes, large hail and flooding. Residents should learn which hazards pose the greatest risk where they live and how to respond when severe weather approaches.

Arlington Alert offers free, real-time updates about weather conditions, road closures and emergencies. Officials said registration takes only a few minutes.

Digital security is also part of emergency readiness. Recommended measures include using strong, unique passwords, enabling multi-factor authentication and remaining alert for phishing attempts and scams powered by artificial intelligence.

Preparing for disasters does not have to be expensive. Free or low-cost steps include identifying common local emergencies, photographing important documents and creating an emergency communications plan.

Residents can find more guidance through Arlington County’s Preparedness Month resources and the Ready Arlington social media account.
