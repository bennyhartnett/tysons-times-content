Fairfax County is asking residents to help meet its climate targets after 2024 became the hottest year recorded in the county. Prolonged heat and rising utility costs have intensified concerns about the effects of climate change and economic pressure on local households.

The county’s community-led climate action plan, adopted in 2021, calls for reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 50% by 2030 and achieving carbon neutrality by 2050.

County government is working to reduce its own emissions, but community activities account for most of Fairfax County’s carbon footprint. Reaching the broader goals will therefore require changes by residents throughout the county.

The Sustain Fairfax campaign is intended to help people take practical steps, including conserving household energy, using efficient appliances and adjusting thermostats. Residents can also reduce transportation emissions by choosing electric vehicles, public transit, carpools, bicycles or walking.

Other options include installing solar energy systems, composting food waste and improving household waste practices.

The county has also created the Sustain Fairfax Challenge, an interactive online platform offering locally tailored guidance, information about rebates and suggestions for saving money while reducing emissions. Participants can measure their impact, earn achievement badges, work with neighbors and take part in community sustainability challenges.

County officials say these changes can produce benefits beyond lower emissions. Energy-efficiency measures may reduce utility bills and improve home comfort, while cleaner vehicles and greater transit use can help reduce air pollution and traffic congestion.

The campaign also highlights preparations for extreme weather, including storm readiness, flood mitigation and cooling centers during heat waves. Together, those efforts are designed to make Fairfax County healthier and more resilient while advancing its long-term climate goals.
