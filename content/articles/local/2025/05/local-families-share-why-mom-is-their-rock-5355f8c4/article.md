Mother’s Day may last just 24 hours, but families say the mothers and maternal figures who shape their lives deserve far more recognition.

Celebrations of motherhood stretch back to ancient Greek and Roman festivals honoring the mother goddesses Rhea and Cybele. In the United States, the modern holiday grew from Anna Marie Jarvis’ effort to honor her mother, Civil War-era social activist and community organizer Anna Jarvis, who died May 9, 1905.

The idea spread nationwide, and President Woodrow Wilson officially recognized Mother’s Day in 1914.

As local families prepare for the holiday, children and grandchildren are reflecting on what makes their loved ones special.

Antione Barksdale described his mother, Hazel Barksdale, as the foundation of their family.

“My mother is the rock of the family,” he said. “We wouldn’t be where we are in life if it weren’t for her. She put a lot of work into us.”

Her grandson, Frank Barksdale III, also expressed his appreciation.

“My grandmother has done so much for our family,” Frank said. “It means so much being able to be living here with her.”

Brothers Leo and Lev shared their admiration for their mother, Elisheva Dienstfrey.

“Mom is the greatest person to ever exist,” Leo said. “We are so lucky to have a mom as great as her that can balance both her work and home life so beautifully. I am grateful to have such an amazing mom. I love her so much.”

Lev kept his tribute brief: “What he said.”
