West Potomac High School captured its first state lacrosse championship since 1990 with a dramatic last-second goal against the Cosby Titans in the Virginia Class 6 final on Saturday, June 14.

The Wolverines grabbed an early lead in Richmond before Cosby rallied. With the title hanging in the balance, West Potomac scored the game-winner in the closing second.

Team captain Jake Benningfield credited the victory to the Wolverines’ ability to regroup under pressure.

“At the end we came back together and held out for the win,” Benningfield said.

West Potomac reached the championship game by defeating Fairfax County powerhouse Robinson High School on Robinson’s home field. Robinson had won the Class 6 title in 2024, following Madison High School’s championship in 2023.

Coach John Stamos said the Wolverines strengthened their teamwork and resilience throughout the season while following the program’s “REPS” motto: “Respect + Effort = Performance Success.”

Stamos also credited defensive coordinator John Falvey and offensive coordinator Peter Stamos, his eldest son, for their roles in the championship run.

For Benningfield, both the title-clinching finish and the semifinal victory over Robinson will remain defining memories.

“To beat them on their home turf was a great moment,” he said.

Benningfield plans to attend the University of South Carolina in the fall and continue playing lacrosse.

The sport has a substantial presence in Fairfax County, where 25 high schools field lacrosse teams alongside more traditionally prominent programs in football, basketball and baseball.
