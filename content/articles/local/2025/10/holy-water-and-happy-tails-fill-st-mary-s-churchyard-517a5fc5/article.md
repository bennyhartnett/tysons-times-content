Dogs settled beside their owners, sniffed out new friends and occasionally added a bark to the prayers during St. Mary’s Episcopal Church’s Blessing of the Animals on Saturday, Oct. 4.

The outdoor ceremony took place in the churchyard at The Point in observance of St. Francis of Assisi, who is honored on Oct. 4 for his love of nature and emphasis on the connection between people and animals.

Interim rector the Rev. Meg Ingalls noted that churches around the world mark the occasion by blessing animals. This gathering drew almost entirely dogs, along with one cat and, as Ingalls observed, “no snakes this time.”

The ceremony opened with the traditional greeting, “The Lord be with you,” followed by the congregation’s response, “And also with you.” A few respectful barks joined in.

Attendees were invited to call out the names of pets who had died, remembering the companionship they had provided.

Ingalls and the Rev. Victoria Herd then moved among the folding chairs to bless each animal. Using an evergreen branch dipped in holy water, Ingalls approached the first row of dogs.

“Little Bear, may the blessings of God be with you always,” she said.

Melissa and David Urban sat in the back row holding their dog, Gabby. The couple had driven from San Antonio to visit David’s sister, one of the priests leading the ceremony. David said he is a retired Methodist minister.

Nearby, Herd reached the carrier holding Cinderella, the only cat in attendance. She carefully unzipped it and sprinkled water over Cinderella’s head.

After a final prayer asking for health, peace and safety, the dogs received bone marrow-filled MacroSnacks—a welcome reward for the mostly patient, well-behaved crowd of wagging tails.
