Benjamin Shaul, 36, survived a cardiac arrest at his home after his wife quickly called 911 and began chest compressions.

Shaul had experienced feelings of indigestion before going into cardiac arrest on May 17, 2025. When emergency medical personnel arrived, they continued CPR and used a defibrillator once, restoring his pulse.

He left the hospital the following day neurologically intact.

Fairfax County Fire and Rescue reunited Shaul and his wife with the first responders who treated him during a Resuscitation Reunion on Friday, Oct. 17.

Immediate action is critical during cardiac arrest. A person’s chance of survival falls by about 10% for every minute that passes without CPR. Roughly 70% of cardiac arrests occur in private homes, meaning a bystander may need to help a family member or friend before emergency crews arrive.

Hands-Only CPR involves two steps: call 911, then push hard and fast in the center of the person’s chest. During the first few minutes of cardiac arrest, it can be as effective as conventional CPR that includes rescue breaths.

A heart attack can lead to cardiac arrest, making it important to recognize symptoms and seek help immediately.

## Free CPR open house planned

The Bailey’s Crossroads Fire Department will host a free Hands-Only CPR open house from 10 a.m. to noon Saturday, Oct. 25, 2025, at Fire Station 10, 3601 Firehouse Lane in Falls Church.

The drop-in event is open to everyone, with no registration required. Visitors can practice chest compressions, learn how to use an automated external defibrillator and build confidence responding to an emergency.

Those unable to attend can learn the technique through Fairfax County Fire and Rescue’s two-minute Hands-Only CPR instructional video.
