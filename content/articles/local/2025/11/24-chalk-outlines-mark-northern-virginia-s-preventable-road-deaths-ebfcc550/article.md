Twenty-four chalk body outlines covered the entrance to Gateway Park in Arlington on Nov. 16, each representing a person killed in a Northern Virginia traffic crash during the previous year.

Families of victims, crash survivors, residents and members of Northern Virginia Families for Safe Streets gathered for the World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, observed annually on the third Sunday in November.

Michael Doyle, the organization’s founder and executive director, said every crash represents a family coping with death or serious injury—and that such tragedies can be prevented.

Doyle said the region recorded 24 traffic fatalities and 133 serious injuries during the previous year. Another 13 people had been killed since the start of the current year.

Crash survivor Helen Harris then described how her life changed seven years earlier at the same intersection where the remembrance ceremony was held.

Harris was crossing Fort Myer Drive and Langston Boulevard with her 4-month-old daughter in a stroller when a left-turning truck struck her. She pushed the stroller toward safety as the vehicle approached, and her daughter escaped injury.

Harris sustained catastrophic injuries and underwent an above-the-knee amputation. She spoke about the physical and emotional struggle of adapting afterward, while describing herself as fortunate to have survived.

“I am one of the lucky ones,” Harris said. “Unfortunately, there are those who did not make it through to share their stories themselves.”

She called for data-driven safety measures and pointed to the passage of SB 1416, which increases accountability for drivers who injure vulnerable road users.

Arlington County Board Chair Takis Karantonis called the gathering a “symbolic funeral” for people who died in avoidable circumstances. He said Arlington’s Vision Zero commitment includes redesigning streets so preventable crashes are stopped through safer planning.

Sen. Adam Ebbin, a Democrat representing the 39th District, discussed SB 1417, legislation intended to protect vulnerable road users and encourage local governments to build safer infrastructure.

Del. Patrick Hope highlighted Virginia’s adoption of Intelligent Speed Assistance for repeat reckless-driving offenders. He said the technology can be installed in vehicles to prevent drivers from exceeding the posted limit by more than roughly five to seven mph.

Alexandria Police Chief Tarrick McGuire emphasized regional cooperation and pledged that his department would back its safety commitments with action.

Ben Allard, president of the Arlington Families for Safe Streets board, closed the ceremony with a warning that future traffic victims are people who are alive today.

“Every action that we don’t take is imperiling the lives of people who would be victims in the future,” Allard said.

He urged advocates to seek greater local authority for speed-safety cameras and also supported creating car-free zones. Some participants later joined other Families for Safe Streets chapters from across the Washington region for a noon rally at the Lincoln Memorial.

Northern Virginia Families for Safe Streets is seeking broader authority to place speed cameras on high-crash roads and continued progress toward eliminating traffic deaths and serious injuries in Alexandria, Arlington and Fairfax.
