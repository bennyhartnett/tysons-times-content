The Alexandria Police Department marked the United States Marine Corps’ 250th birthday with a Nov. 12 ceremony honoring officers who previously served or continue to serve as Marines.

Held at APD Headquarters, the event highlighted the shared sense of duty connecting military and law enforcement service. Marines past and present were recognized alongside other veterans who continued serving their community through the department.

Police Chief Tarrick McGuire thanked the veterans for protecting the country at home and abroad.

“Thank you for the sacrifice you have made for our country to keep us safe in a domestic setting but also abroad,” McGuire said. “I am grateful that we have men and women in our organization that not only have served in our country but continue to serve to keep our community safe. From a place of gratitude, I thank you.”

The program included a Presentation of Colors, a reading of the Marine Corps commandant’s birthday message and remarks from guest of honor Michael Jadoo, commandant of the Marine Corps League Detachment.

The ceremony culminated in the traditional cutting of a birthday cake with an officer’s sword. The oldest and youngest Marines in attendance shared the first slice, symbolizing the transfer of experience and knowledge between generations.

Maj. Gen. John Lejeune established the tradition in 1921, and it has since become a central part of Marine Corps birthday observances.

“This is about sharing experience and passing it on,” said Sgt. Maxwell VanArsdale, the youngest Marine at the ceremony. “Every Marine birthday gets better because it reminds us of who we are and what we stand for.”

Officer Alex Trapero, a Marine veteran who has served with APD for 23 years, was the oldest Marine Corps veteran at the event.

“The structure and courage we learned as Marines guide us every day in law enforcement,” Trapero said. “We have the courage to be the first to respond. That’s what binds us — whether in uniform as Marines or as police officers.”
