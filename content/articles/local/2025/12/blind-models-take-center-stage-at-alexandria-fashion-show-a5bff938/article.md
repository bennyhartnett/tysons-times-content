Blind and visually impaired models commanded the runway Nov. 1 as the IMperfeKtlyMade Foundation held its third annual fashion show at The Garden Alexandria on Eisenhower Avenue.

The “Unseen”-themed event featured local designers’ couture, ranging from activewear to evening gowns. Months of preparation included rehearsals, set design and guest services, with volunteers helping models navigate the experience safely.

Angela Harris founded IMperfeKtlyMade after losing her vision because of a brain tumor. The wife of School Board member Chris Harris turned that adversity into a mission to create opportunities for people with visual impairments to join activities often considered inaccessible.

“True vision extends beyond sight,” Angela Harris said. “It’s about imagination and soul.”

Host and emcee Tei Pearson-Hall also spoke about her own vision loss. A disabled Army veteran, she said fluid on her brain left her with 25 percent vision in both eyes.

“When I hear someone say we can’t do something, I am always part of the crew that says ‘yes, we can,’” Pearson-Hall said.

Model Natasha Jean traveled from Alabama for her first appearance in the show. Jean, author of *The Blind Eye*, has a prosthetic eye as a result of domestic violence and described the event as empowering for visually impaired people.

Other participants included Rosalyn Kennedy and Carmen Dennie Dukes, the show’s youngest model.

Chris Harris introduced the event’s leading sponsor, the Rev. Ray Razor, president of Washington Metropolitan Ear, an organization that provides reading services for blind people.

He also explained that the foundation’s name comes from the phrase “I am perfectly made,” reflecting its emphasis on embracing the flaws, scars and struggles that shape a person.

“Our imperfections make us who we are,” Angela Harris said.
