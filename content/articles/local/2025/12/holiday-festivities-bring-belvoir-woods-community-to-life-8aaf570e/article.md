A packed holiday calendar is helping residents at The Fairfax at Belvoir Woods celebrate the season while strengthening connections across the retirement community.

The Fairfax Chorus opened the festivities in early December. A tree-lighting ceremony, Christmas brunch and New Year’s Eve gala were also scheduled, creating what resident Lisa Makosewski called “a nice mix of things.”

Makosewski moved from Philadelphia to be closer to her nephew and niece. Although the community’s location initially caught her attention, she said visiting it made the difference.

“I walked in and felt immediately at home,” Makosewski said. Participating in community activities, including the holiday events, has deepened that sense of belonging. “It makes everybody feel festive.”

Other seasonal offerings include an Army-Navy football game tailgate party, a Hanukkah celebration dinner held in partnership with a local temple, a lecture about several British artists and a health presentation titled “Holiday Harmony.”

Recreation and Activities Director Jill K. G. Arvanitis said the community offers on-site programs and outings designed for residents with a wide range of interests and abilities. Residents sometimes compare the lifestyle to being aboard a cruise ship that never leaves port, she said.

Social opportunities can be especially important for older adults. An AARP survey found that 74% of adults questioned identified in-person socializing as an important part of their happiness, with activities in 55-plus communities offering one way to build friendships.

The Fairfax is a continuing care retirement community with more than 450 residents. Its services include independent living, assisted living, memory care and skilled nursing.

Located across Richmond Highway from Fort Belvoir and bordered on one side by Telegraph Road, the community provides access to Springfield and Kingstowne. Residents can choose among 15 apartment models and use amenities including an indoor pool, library, fitness center and pickleball courts.

The activities extend well beyond the holiday season.

“There’s always something,” Makosewski said.
