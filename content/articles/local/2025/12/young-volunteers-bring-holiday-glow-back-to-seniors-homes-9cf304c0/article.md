Recent college graduates are spending Fridays in December climbing ladders, untangling lights and restoring treasured holiday traditions for older residents across Northern Virginia.

The volunteer group, known as the Fix-It Friday Elves, began when three friends helped a neighbor who could no longer safely decorate. It soon expanded as requests arrived through church bulletins, community boards and word of mouth.

Each Friday, the volunteers now visit several homes while balancing new jobs and graduate school applications. They retrieve ornaments from attics, arrange heirloom decorations, hang garlands and illuminate homes for residents unable to do the work themselves.

Daniel Ruiz, one of the group’s founders, said his grandmother taught him that the holidays are about bringing light into dark times. After realizing that some neighbors could no longer put up their decorations safely, the friends decided to bring that light to them.

For Margaret Ellis, an 88-year-old widow in Herndon, the help restored a tradition she had struggled to maintain. She said December had become difficult because she could no longer climb up to hang lights. With her tree illuminated and her home decorated with garland again, she feels connected to memories of celebrating with her husband.

The visits also offer seniors companionship and reassurance that their traditions still matter.

Volunteer Sofia Patel of Arlington said the project is about more than decorating. The team wants older residents to feel proud of their homes and enjoy the holiday glow regardless of whether they can afford professional help.

Jordan Ryan and Jennifer Lee, both Vienna residents, joined after learning about the effort and helped widen its reach. The growing team has decorated nearly 20 Alexandria homes this season, ranging from small apartments to large porches.

Across Northern Virginia, the volunteers say they have brightened more than two dozen homes. Neighbors have supported the crew by bringing cookies and hot chocolate, while others stop to watch as the homes are transformed.

Lee said the work gives the volunteers a tangible way to build community. Seeing a resident’s expression change when the lights come on, she said, reminds the group why it returns each week.

Ellis described the impact in simple terms: The volunteers did more than hang lights—they lifted her heart.

The graduates hope to continue the project in future holiday seasons, expand into additional neighborhoods and potentially train other volunteers to reproduce the effort. For now, their attention remains on completing the December schedule and bringing holiday warmth to as many seniors as possible.
