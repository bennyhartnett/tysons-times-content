Nearly eight years after 2018’s experimental *Testing*, A$AP Rocky returned Jan. 16, 2026, with *Don’t Be Dumb*, his fourth studio album and one of the year’s most anticipated releases.

The album quickly establishes its own identity. Rather than recreating the grunge-inflected rap of *Testing* or the sharper lyricism of 2013’s *Long.Live.A$AP*, Rocky presents a more reflective portrait of his personal and artistic growth. Hard-hitting trap, introspective R&B and psychedelic melodies flow through a project that is varied but largely cohesive.

“Order of Protection” opens the album with cinematic tension and commanding swagger. “Helicopter” and “Stole Ya Flow” push the energy higher with booming bass and forceful production that complements Rocky’s aggressive delivery.

“Stole Ya Flow” is especially confrontational, taking multiple shots at Drake involving Rihanna and Drake’s feud with Kendrick Lamar. Rocky also answers jabs directed at him on Drake’s “Family Matters,” reinforcing the album’s competitive edge.

Rocky’s willingness to cross genres and work with an eclectic group of collaborators produces some of the record’s strongest moments. Lead single “Punk Rocky” blends indie rock, psychedelic textures and a softer rap performance, resulting in one of the album’s most memorable—and summer-ready—songs.

The expansive sound also makes room for appearances from Brent Faiyaz, Gorillaz, Doechii, Thundercat, Tyler, the Creator, Westside Gunn and others.

Beneath the project’s recurring themes of resilience and self-assertion, Rocky leaves space for vulnerability. “Stay Here 4 Life,” featuring Brent Faiyaz, is the album’s most emotional and melodic track, while “Playa” slows the tempo with dreamy, jazz-influenced production. Those quieter passages provide an effective counterweight to the louder trap records and help make *Don’t Be Dumb* feel like Rocky’s most mature album.

The experimentation does not always succeed. On several tracks, dense production threatens to overwhelm Rocky’s rapping, and some lyrics fall short of the album’s stronger writing. That inconsistency keeps the project from reaching its full potential.

Still, when the album finds its rhythm—particularly on “Don’t Be Dumb/Trip Baby”—Rocky delivers some of the most compelling performances of his career.

*Don’t Be Dumb* is a daring comeback that revisits elements of Rocky’s past without becoming trapped by them. Its energetic peaks, soulful detours and melodic stretches give it considerable replay value while reaffirming his place among hip-hop’s most adventurous voices.

The album is more than a return after a long absence. It is Rocky’s statement about reflection, growth and domination.

The album earns a rating of 8.5 out of 10.
