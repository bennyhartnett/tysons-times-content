A mischievous demon, two newly minted ghosts and a grieving teenager collide in *Beetlejuice JR*, now playing at Aldersgate Church Community Theater in Alexandria through Jan. 25.

The youth adaptation condenses the Broadway musical while preserving its rapid-fire comedy, offbeat music and unexpectedly tender exploration of loss. Inspired by Tim Burton’s film, the production pairs supernatural pandemonium with a story about family and belonging.

At its center is Lydia Deetz, a self-described strange and unusual teenager mourning her mother. After Lydia and her father move into a house haunted by a recently deceased couple, she encounters Beetlejuice, a demon eager to stir up trouble.

Lydia recruits him to frighten away the living, but the double-crossing specter soon reveals his true nature and unleashes chaos from the Netherworld.

The show moves briskly between sharp humor and emotional moments. Lydia’s grief gives the story weight, while Beetlejuice’s outrageous behavior keeps the production’s energy high.

A bold, continually changing set follows the manic pace of the action. Michele Koros’ playful choreography adds to that momentum, complementing the title character’s unpredictable personality.

Under musical director Michael Barranco, Eddie Perfect’s score blends rock, pop and traditional musical theater. Numbers including “Dead Mom” and “Say My Name” combine witty lyrics with the show’s more poignant themes.

Producer Judi Conti said the musical is newly licensed for youth theater and is making its debut in the area.

“There is something truly magical about watching young actors come together and put on a fabulous show,” Conti said. “It is funny, poignant, and the music is great. I think children of the ’80s will especially appreciate it as they remember the original movie.”

The cast includes performers from nine schools across the region, including more than five high schools. Director Julia St. Pierre said the production also showcases young artists and designers working on its lighting, scenery, costumes, sound and props.

“It is so exciting to put together our own vision for *Beetlejuice*,” St. Pierre said.

Beneath the ghosts, gags and spectacle, the musical examines grief, family and the struggle to find a place in the world. Katie Dunn, who plays Lydia, said she hopes audiences leave uplifted and with “a new outlook on the beauty of life and the people we have around us.”

*Beetlejuice JR* runs through Jan. 25 at Aldersgate Church Community Theater, 1301 Collingwood Road in Alexandria.
