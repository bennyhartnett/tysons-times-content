Clash Royale built its reputation on skill. Winning required players to understand card interactions, manage regenerating elixir and execute moves with precise timing—not simply spend more than their opponents.

That balance has eroded as upgrades and powerful abilities have become increasingly tied to purchases. Design changes intended to generate revenue now risk undermining the competitive integrity that helped make the game popular.

Early players had a clear progression target: build an eight-card deck and eventually raise it to level 13 through consistent play. Later updates added levels 14 and 15, sharply increasing upgrade costs while restricting the free resources available to meet them.

Those updates effectively forced longtime players to collect huge quantities of gold and cards again just to remain competitive. Progress that once appeared permanent could lose much of its value after a single update.

Level 15 made the divide especially visible through Elite Wild Cards, which are required exclusively for upgrades to that level. Players receive them in small quantities through ordinary gameplay, while paid offers make them far easier to obtain. Reaching the maximum level without spending money has consequently become unrealistic for many players.

“I have totally stopped playing the game. Supercell just started to add items just to make money and not improve the game at all,” sophomore Evan Hyun said.

Card Evolutions have widened the gap further. These upgrades give certain cards powerful abilities capable of deciding matches, but the shards needed to unlock them are often attached to paid battle passes, store bundles and limited-time offers.

Free-to-play users earn shards much more slowly. That can leave them facing online opponents who have access to match-changing mechanics that they have not yet been able to unlock.

“Evolution shards are definitely unfair, because it’s just another one of [Supercell’s] items to spend money on, tending to be very powerful upon release,” sophomore Lucas Hendrix said. “If you don’t buy them, it provides a highly unfair advantage throughout the game.”

The redesigned Pass Royale has also shifted important rewards toward paying users. Recent versions divide benefits among multiple paid tiers while reducing the value of the free track. Gold, wild cards and evolution shards are disproportionately available to customers, making the pass feel less like an optional bonus and more like a competitive necessity.

These repeated changes have weakened trust in the game’s progression system. Players cannot be sure their time and effort will retain value when a future update may introduce another costly level or ability.

Clash Royale’s troubles are not simply the result of age or a shortage of new ideas. Its growing emphasis on monetized progression is driving away players who once believed skill would determine their success.

A separate competitive mode could help restore that confidence by disabling added abilities and placing players on more equal footing. For a competitive game to endure, players must trust that victory is earned in the match—not purchased before it begins.
