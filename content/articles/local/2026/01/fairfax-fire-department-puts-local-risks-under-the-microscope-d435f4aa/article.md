Community Risk Reduction Week 2026 is highlighting how the Fairfax County Fire and Rescue Department uses local data to prevent emergencies and lessen their impact.

Community risk reduction begins with identifying and prioritizing threats facing residents and emergency responders. The department then strategically directs prevention and emergency-response resources toward the greatest risks, with the broader goal of building healthier, safer and more resilient communities.

The process includes conducting a Community Risk Assessment, developing and implementing a risk-reduction plan, and evaluating its results. Each plan details the goals, programs and resources needed to address the community’s highest-priority concerns.

The department also uses risk data to identify vulnerable demographic groups and select safety-education topics likely to have the greatest local benefit.

Its goals and programs are organized around six areas: Engineering, Emergency Response, Economic Incentive, Education, Enforcement and Equity. Equity was added to the traditional “5 E’s” model in keeping with Fairfax County’s One Fairfax initiative.

Together, those areas help the department establish clear objectives, find essential resources and partnerships, and coordinate programs designed to reduce identified risks.
