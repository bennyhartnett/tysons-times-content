Fairfax County firefighters are responding to more calls after the recent storm left thick ice on sidewalks and driveways, hardened snow across neighborhoods and dangerous sheets of ice on vehicles.

Capt. Frederick “Rick” Luong said crews have seen an increase in slip-and-fall injuries as people struggle to walk on frozen surfaces.

The ice can also slow emergency responses after firefighters arrive. Slick walkways make it harder to carry cots and other equipment into buildings and safely move patients outside.

Ice left on cars poses another threat. As vehicles travel, frozen sheets can break loose and strike other drivers, passengers or vehicles, potentially causing crashes, damage and injuries.

Snow-covered sidewalks are creating problems as well. Luong said more pedestrians are walking in streets, disrupting traffic and putting themselves in danger.

Firefighters are asking residents to clear at least 3 feet of space in every direction around nearby fire hydrants. Keeping that area accessible can save crews valuable time during an emergency.

## Clearing Snow and Ice Safely

Firefighter Emanuel Agam urged drivers to remove snow and ice from the roof, sides and every window of their vehicles before taking to the road. He said motorists should not overlook the rear windshield.

For driveways, sidewalks and areas around parked cars, firefighters recommend using a sturdy metal shovel to break apart thick ice. Residents should move slowly, keep their footing stable and use short, careful steps sometimes called the “penguin walk.”

People clearing ice should avoid overexertion and take breaks when needed. Once the thick layer has been broken up, ice melt can help make walking surfaces safer. Residents should also make sure sidewalks and hydrants remain clear.
