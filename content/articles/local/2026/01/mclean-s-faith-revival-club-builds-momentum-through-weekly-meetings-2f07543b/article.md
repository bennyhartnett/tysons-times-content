McLean High School’s Faith Revival Club closed out its January meetings on Friday, Jan. 23, with a discussion about Jesus healing a Roman official’s son, the second miracle recorded in the Gospel of John.

Junior Lily Kilorge said students examined the story’s message about faith and trust in Jesus, as well as ways they could apply those lessons in their own lives.

The student-led Christian club meets every Friday, giving participants an open setting to discuss a selected group of Bible verses. Meetings may also include review games about earlier passages and conversations about students’ personal faith.

Sophomore Sam Emory said the club aims to keep those discussions open while helping more students learn about Jesus.

Members also see the weekly gatherings as an opportunity to form connections with other Christian students. Junior Julia Huie said the group wants to help participants deepen their faith, build relationships with one another and strengthen their connection with God.

Founded in 2022, the club has steadily attracted more members. Kilorge said previous leaders had encountered difficulties securing official recognition, but the current group ultimately gained approval, allowing the club to expand its reach at McLean.

In the coming months, members plan to organize themed meetings, discussions or fellowship activities connected to Valentine’s Day and Easter.

The club associates Valentine’s Day with Saint Valentine, a martyr traditionally linked to marriages performed in defiance of Roman authorities, and with the Christian principle of loving one’s neighbors. Easter commemorates Jesus’ death and resurrection.

Although primarily Christian, the Faith Revival Club welcomes students of any background or belief who are interested in attending. Kilorge said the group strives to provide a respectful environment where students can discuss faith, learn about God, support one another and explore their beliefs without fear of judgment.
