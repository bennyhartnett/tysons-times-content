Netflix’s *Agatha Christie’s Seven Dials* has the ingredients of an absorbing period whodunit, but obvious clues and a muddled plot turn the roughly two-and-a-half-hour series into a disappointing watch.

Set in 1920s England, the adaptation follows Lady Eileen “Bundle” Brent, played by Mia McKenna-Bruce, as she investigates mysteries arising from the death of her intended fiancé, Gerry Wade. An old country estate, a masquerade party and a suspicious death create a promising setup, yet the execution rarely matches the premise.

The mystery begins at Chimneys, where Gerry’s friends plan a prank based on his habit of oversleeping. They hide several alarm clocks in his room, expecting the noise to wake him the next morning.

The answer, unfortunately, is that it does not.

While the rest of the household gathers downstairs, the alarms continue ringing in Gerry’s room. Bundle’s mother sends the butler to investigate, and Bundle follows. They discover Gerry dead.

The scene immediately offers suspicious details. A sleeping draught sits beside the bed even though Gerry was already known as an easy sleeper. Seven alarm clocks—or dials—have also been arranged in front of his bed, despite being hidden around the room the previous night. Bundle quickly takes up the investigation.

From there, the series loses momentum. Its clues are often too conspicuous to make solving the case satisfying, while familiar investigative methods are ignored in ways that leave the mystery feeling both predictable and confusing. Instead of inviting viewers to piece together a sophisticated puzzle, the show frequently makes its answers apparent.

The introduction of the Seven Dials secret society adds more clutter than intrigue. Events that need explanation are passed over too quickly, while slower and less compelling material receives substantial screen time.

The conclusion also appears designed to prepare Bundle for additional adventures. The final minutes repeatedly tie off loose ends while dropping hints about stories that could follow, making the ending feel prolonged without resolving the series’ larger pacing problems.

McKenna-Bruce and the rest of the cast deliver respectable performances, and the cinematography captures the period setting effectively. Those strengths, however, cannot rescue a whodunit that gives viewers too little opportunity to participate in the investigation. For mystery fans hoping to follow the evidence and crack the case alongside Bundle, *Seven Dials* is unlikely to justify the time commitment.
