*Primate* has the makings of a disturbing survival-horror film: an eerily intelligent chimpanzee, an isolated house and a sudden outbreak of rabies. While the 2026 movie delivers graphic violence and flashes of genuine suspense, its thin story and abandoned emotional threads drain the terror of any lasting impact.

The film follows Lucy and two friends during a weekend at her family’s home. Also living there is Ben, an unusually intelligent chimpanzee who uses a word-based tablet and understands most sign language. His almost-human communication makes him fascinating, but unsettling.

The visit turns deadly after a mongoose bites Ben and infects him with rabies. As his behavior becomes violent, the house transforms into a trap, and its occupants discover that they have lost control.

Horror is where the movie works best. Ben is a frightening presence, and the violence is both graphic and intense. Some of the strongest scenes keep a character in the foreground while Ben moves slowly in the background. By allowing the threat to creep into view before the characters recognize it, the film creates real tension.

Those effective moments are surrounded by storytelling that rarely pays off. The movie introduces romantic friction and complicated friendships as though those relationships will shape later events. Instead, characters tied to those threads die before the emotional groundwork leads anywhere, leaving the relationships feeling ornamental.

The visual techniques also become repetitive. Upside-down camera angles and recycled shots initially appear intended to reflect rising tension. Used so frequently, however, they lose their novelty and begin to feel like substitutes for more inventive suspense.

An overcrowded cast adds to the problem. Two men arrive at the house expecting a party and appear positioned to bring conflict, depth or a twist to the crisis. Their presence ultimately changes little, and their deaths register more as additional carnage than meaningful turning points.

By the conclusion, the surviving characters do not appear to carry any larger significance, and the plot offers little payoff beyond the violence itself. The dialogue leaves few memorable impressions, while the emotional stakes never become as compelling as the film seems to intend.

Ben’s near-human behavior gives *Primate* a creepy foundation, and several carefully staged scenes show how effective that premise could have been. But discarded relationships, expendable characters and overused camera tricks leave the movie feeling hollow. It produces moments of shock without building a story powerful enough to make them linger.
