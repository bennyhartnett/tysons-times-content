Northern Virginia voters entered 2026 amid a rapid series of special elections after Gov. Abigail Spanberger selected several veteran lawmakers for roles in her new administration.

The appointments reshaped the region’s General Assembly delegation just as lawmakers began considering multibillion-dollar budgets and Virginia’s plans for retail cannabis sales. They also left some communities temporarily without representation in Richmond.

Before the General Assembly opened its 164th session Wednesday, Jan. 14, voters in Fairfax, Arlington and Alexandria were already navigating the transition from former Gov. Glenn Youngkin’s administration.

## Lawmakers tapped for administration posts

Spanberger nominated Del. Mark Sickles of Fairfax, who represented House District 17, for secretary of finance. Del. David Bulova of House District 11 was selected for secretary of natural and historic resources.

Sen. Adam Ebbin of Senate District 39 was named a senior adviser responsible for overseeing the planned 2026 launch of a retail cannabis framework.

The appointments are not automatic. Virginia’s Constitution requires the General Assembly to confirm cabinet secretaries following committee reviews of their qualifications and financial disclosures.

The changes gave Northern Virginia greater influence within the executive branch, but they also created temporary gaps in the region’s legislative representation. Officials scheduled special elections quickly, although the timing differed among districts.

## New delegates take office

House District 11 voters elected Gretchen Bulova to succeed her husband, David Bulova, in a Jan. 13 special election. She defeated Republican Adam Wise and was sworn in on an accelerated schedule so she could participate when the legislative session began the following day.

House District 17 went without a representative for about a week before its Jan. 20 election. Democrat Garrett McGuire defeated Republican Christopher Cardiff for the seat previously held by Sickles.

McGuire had narrowly prevailed in a four-candidate Democratic primary in December. He now represents Franconia, Kingstowne and Huntington as legislators take up budget amendments and the cannabis rollout.

House District 23 also held a special election Jan. 13. Margaret Angela Franklin won the seat previously held by Candi Mundon King.

## Ebbin’s departure could trigger another election

Ebbin delayed his Senate resignation until Feb. 18, allowing him to represent his constituents through crossover, the deadline for legislation to clear its chamber of origin.

A Feb. 10 special election was scheduled to choose his successor. Del. Elizabeth Bennett-Parker won the Democratic primary with 70.6% of the vote and was set to face Republican nominee Julie Robben Lineberry.

A Bennett-Parker victory would extend the election cycle. Her departure from House District 5 would create another vacancy and require Alexandria voters to return to the polls once again.

## Fairfax school board seat also vacant

The region’s succession scramble reaches beyond the General Assembly. Fairfax County scheduled a March 3 special election for the Braddock District School Board seat following Rachna Sizemore Heizer’s resignation.

Tom Dannan received the Democratic endorsement with 75% of the 2,928 ballots cast during a Jan. 10 caucus. He was set to face independent candidate Saundra Davis.

Until the vacancy is filled, at-large School Board members Kyle McDaniel, Ryan McElveen and Ilryong Moon are assisting Braddock District residents.
