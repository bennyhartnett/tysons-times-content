*The Housemaid* draws viewers into a wealthy family’s unsettling home, then repeatedly overturns their assumptions about what is happening behind its orderly facade. Its controlled pacing, tense atmosphere and well-placed humor make it a particularly effective recent psychological thriller.

Sydney Sweeney stars as Millie, a young woman hired as a live-in maid by the Winchester family. Because she also lives in the house, Millie becomes immersed in its increasingly strange dynamics with little opportunity to escape them. The film quickly challenges its initial portrayal of her—and continues reshaping the audience’s perspective as the story develops.

Much of the unease comes from Millie’s encounters with Nina Winchester, played by Amanda Seyfried. Their interactions grow awkward and unpredictable, occasionally becoming funny without undermining the darker plot. These moments offer brief relief while reinforcing the sense that something inside the family is deeply wrong.

The movie keeps the nature of that problem deliberately unclear. Instead of relying only on sudden revelations, it plants foreshadowing and small clues throughout the story. Those details help later developments feel carefully constructed rather than included merely for shock value. Fans of the book may also recognize a scene designed as a visual reference to its cover.

Seyfried delivers the standout performance, giving Nina emotional range and depth. Brandon Sklenar is also strong as Nina’s husband, Andrew, and plays an important role in moving the story forward. Sweeney’s performance is more restrained, although her recurring deadpan expression can feel repetitive beside the greater dynamism of her co-stars.

With smart misdirection, mostly strong performances and an energetic plot, *The Housemaid* remains gripping from beginning to end. Its flashes of situational humor keep the film from becoming relentlessly grim, while its steady manipulation of audience expectations creates a tense and satisfying thriller.
