Don Toliver’s fifth studio album, *OCTANE*, delivers a meticulously produced 49-minute rush of heavy bass, futuristic textures and soulful R&B. Beneath that speed and spectacle, however, lies a persistent emotional detachment that gives the record an uneasy edge.

The album makes an immediate impact with “E85,” a punishing opener built to overwhelm. A Malcolm Todd sample adds an unexpected beauty to the track, helping Toliver establish a sound that feels distinct from his earlier work.

That intensity continues on “Body,” which transforms a Justin Timberlake sample into a dark song about protectiveness. Romance collides with forceful, bass-driven production as Toliver demonstrates his ability to move between moods and vocal styles.

“Secondhand,” featuring Rema, shifts into more melancholy territory. Its smooth hook lets both performers shine while the song examines the pain of releasing someone from your life. References to luxury and distraction sometimes interrupt that vulnerability, but Rema’s contribution fits the production and Toliver’s vocals especially well, making it the album’s strongest guest appearance.

“Tiramisu” glides in a different direction, pairing pure luxury with effortless cool. The track evokes a high-end car racing through a rainy night, with Toliver alternating between a crisp rhythmic flow and a polished, versatile hook.

The pace becomes frantic again on “ATM,” whose pounding bass captures the compulsive pull of late-night spending. Its aggressive production pushes Toliver to cycle through his full range of vocal techniques just to match the beat’s relentless force.

Material obsession gives way to nostalgia on “Rosary,” a Travis Scott collaboration that updates the sound of early-2000s R&B. The two artists harmonize over a classic-style vocal sample, creating a relaxed track that plays naturally to both of their strengths.

Later, “Call Back” highlights Toliver’s technical control. He finds narrow openings within the beat for an infectious, radio-ready melody, repeatedly changing his delivery as though his voice were another instrument in the production.

The clearest personal moment arrives with the emphatic closer “Sweet Home.” In a rare look at his private life, Toliver acknowledges that he has not devoted enough time to his relationship. That admission gives the album a sense of clarity after so much speed, luxury and emotional avoidance.

By combining space-age atmosphere, soulful R&B and crushing low end, *OCTANE* emerges as one of Toliver’s most polished projects. Its ambitious production and fearless vocal performances make the album a high-speed triumph worthy of an 8.9 out of 10.
