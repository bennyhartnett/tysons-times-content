Flavor Hive has grown from a modest food truck launched in early 2024 into a fast-expanding restaurant business, drawing crowds across the Washington, D.C., Maryland and Virginia region with customizable walking tacos, fusion bowls and loaded street food.

The concept first gained attention by inviting customers to bring a bag of chips—whether Doritos, Takis or another favorite—and fill it with halal-seasoned meats, cheese, vegetables and house sauces. Salad greens could replace the chips, while portions priced at roughly $10 were often large enough to share.

Social media personalities and food influencers amplified the excitement. A review from viral restaurant critic Keith Lee helped introduce Flavor Hive to a wider audience, while pop-up appearances generated formidable lines. In Richmond, some customers reported waiting for hours in cold weather to try the signature dish.

## From mobile kitchen to permanent locations

Flavor Hive was founded by Shihan Chowdhury and Amgd Gende.

Chowdhury, known online as “Chilipeppercooks,” grew up in the region and graduated from Seneca Valley High School in Germantown, Maryland. Before launching the business, he had developed an online following through food content, inventive recipes and bold flavors.

Gende brought additional business experience and entrepreneurial drive to the partnership. Together, the founders built a dining concept around affordability, customization and the social experience of sharing food.

Flavor Hive opened its first permanent restaurant in Annandale, Virginia, in summer 2025. A Rockville-area location followed in February 2026 as the business continued expanding, with Georgetown, Manassas and other communities identified for further growth.

The brand has also maintained its food truck presence at neighborhood gatherings, community events and schools. A visit to Seneca Valley High School connected Chowdhury’s growing business with the community where he was once a student.

Gende has described Flavor Hive’s mission as “bringing people together.” Customers are encouraged to choose or bring their own chips, making each order reflect personal tastes and familiar flavors.

## A customizable menu with global influences

Walking tacos remain the centerpiece of the menu. Customers can pair their chosen chips with halal chicken, beef or lamb, then add vegetables and sauces.

Rice and salad bowls offer chip-free alternatives, while loaded fries, pita pouches, quesadillas and specialty drinks extend the menu’s playful approach to comfort food. The dishes combine Middle Eastern, South Asian and other global influences in a casual, pickup-focused setting without traditional table service.

Many diners have praised the large portions, friendly employees and food served quickly and hot. Others have called the concept overhyped, pointing to lengthy waits at truck appearances or expressing mixed views about prices and seasoning.

Even with those divided reactions, Flavor Hive’s swift rise shows how effectively an interactive food idea can move from social-media curiosity to a permanent local presence.

Flavor Hive lists locations in North Bethesda, Richmond, Manassas, Annandale and several parts of Alexandria, including Huntington and Potomac Yard.
