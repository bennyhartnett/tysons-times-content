For more than two decades, Ireland’s Four Provinces has offered Falls Church something more enduring than pints and pub fare: a gathering place where newcomers can quickly feel like regulars.

The Irish pub at 105 W. Broad St. opened in 1997 and was purchased by Colm and Ciara Dillon in 2004. Colm, a native of Cork, Ireland, set out to create the kind of genuine neighborhood pub he knew growing up—not a themed imitation built around Irish symbols.

That vision rests on personal connections as much as food and drink. Colm has said success is less about serving hundreds of dinners in one night than making new friends. The approach has helped turn the pub, commonly called the 4P’s, into a destination for weekly visits, family milestones and casual conversations at the bar.

The Dillons have also supported the wider Falls Church restaurant community. Colm helped organize a restaurant-crawl fundraiser for workers at a neighboring establishment during a crisis. During the pandemic shutdown, the pub recognized customers who contributed to a GoFundMe campaign supporting its employees.

Ciara remains a familiar presence at the host stand and throughout the dining room, often remembering guests and their usual orders.

## An Irish Story in Every Corner

Dark wood, comfortable booths and an outdoor dog-friendly patio give the pub its inviting character. Inside, Irish artwork, prints and memorabilia cover the walls, including pieces from Colm’s mother’s collection.

Among the most prominent items is a framed copy of President John F. Kennedy’s inaugural address. Another section of the dining room is devoted to Irish writers.

The collection reflects the Dillons’ connection to Ireland without turning the restaurant into a caricature. That same philosophy carries into the kitchen.

## Traditional Dishes With Room for Creativity

The menu combines Irish standards with American pub food, seasonal specials and weekend brunch.

Fish and chips are prepared with a light house-made beer batter. Shepherd’s pie layers ground beef, peas and carrots beneath mashed potatoes, while bangers and mash are served with baked beans and beef gravy. The kitchen also offers a corned beef Reuben on grilled rye.

Stews become especially popular during colder weather. Guinness beef stew is a frequent choice, but Colm has singled out the Kinsale seafood stew as his favorite dish. Named for the Irish fishing village, it combines fish, shrimp and mussels with potatoes and leeks in a white-wine cream sauce.

Seasonal offerings incorporate local proteins, fresh seafood, herbs and sauces made in-house. On Sundays, a buffet-style brunch brings together Irish dishes and familiar brunch staples.

The pub’s responsiveness to its regulars was tested when Colm once switched its fries. After complaints from three consecutive tables, he returned to his office and called the supplier to restore the original version.

That willingness to listen has helped Ireland’s Four Provinces endure recessions, the pandemic and the restaurant industry’s continual turnover. Now ranked No. 11 among 263 Falls Church restaurants, the 4P’s remains rooted in the principle that has defined it since the Dillons took over: an Irish pub succeeds when it feels like part of the community.
