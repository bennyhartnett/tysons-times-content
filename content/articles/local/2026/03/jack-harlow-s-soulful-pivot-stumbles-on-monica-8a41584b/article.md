Jack Harlow trades energetic rap for vulnerability and R&B on *Monica*, but the experiment often feels more like a transition than a finished transformation.

Released March 13, 2026—Harlow’s 28th birthday—the rapper’s ninth studio album arrived after a three-year wait. Known for hits including “Lovin On Me,” “First Class” and “WHATS POPPIN,” Harlow makes a sharp stylistic turn here, favoring soulful production and smooth vocals over his familiar upbeat sound.

That change gives *Monica* its strongest moments. Harlow fully commits to a relaxed delivery, while the production draws from 1990s R&B and neo-soul. “Trade Places” and “Living Alone” stand out for their lush, nearly lo-fi beats.

The album also finds Harlow moving beyond his usual boasts. He explores romantic relationships, tells more personal stories and searches for his own voice. “All Of My Friends,” which addresses his friends’ opinions about his love life, captures the project’s warmer and more reflective approach.

Yet the same laid-back style soon becomes a weakness. Similar vocals and instrumentals cause tracks to blend together, while memorable hooks and standout verses are scarce. Harlow’s relaxed delivery can sound low-energy, and the punchy lines associated with his earlier work rarely appear.

Compared with the more explosive *Come Home the Kids Miss You* and *That’s What They All Say*, *Monica* feels muted and repetitive. Its 28-minute runtime reinforces the impression of a collection of experiments rather than a fully developed album.

There is value in Harlow’s vulnerability, polished instrumentation and willingness to explore a new sound. Still, *Monica* is an uneven renovation of his musical identity—an intriguing move toward R&B that has not yet reached its final form.

The album earns a rating of 5.2 out of 10.
