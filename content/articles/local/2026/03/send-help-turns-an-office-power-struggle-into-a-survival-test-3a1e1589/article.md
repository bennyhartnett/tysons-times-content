“Send Help” finds fresh tension in a familiar survival-thriller setup, combining sudden scares and sharp humor with a compelling reversal of power between its stranded leads.

Rachel McAdams plays Linda Liddle, a longtime employee who has been working toward a promotion. Dylan O’Brien portrays Bradley Preston, her boss, who recently assumed control of his late father’s company. Their relationship is already strained when Preston begins making decisions that jeopardize Liddle’s future at the business.

The uneasy co-workers must travel together to Bangkok for an important business trip, but a plane crash leaves them isolated and fighting to survive. Away from the office, their professional hierarchy quickly loses its force.

Preston initially behaves as though his authority still applies, but corporate leadership proves far less useful in their new surroundings. Liddle’s practical knowledge and resourcefulness gradually shift the balance of power, making their evolving relationship one of the film’s strongest elements.

The movie sustains its tension through jump scares and unexpected plot turns, repeatedly leaving viewers uncertain about what will happen next. Those shocks support the story instead of carrying it alone.

Situational comedy frequently breaks the pressure at just the right moment. McAdams and O’Brien handle those tonal changes effectively, moving between suspense and humor without making the film feel disjointed.

Creative camera work and a stylized, slightly quirky visual approach give “Send Help” a distinct identity. The leads also appear increasingly exhausted and battered rather than improbably polished, helping the survival ordeal feel more convincing.

By mixing horror, humor and a constantly changing struggle for control, “Send Help” becomes more than a succession of scares. It remains unsettling and entertaining while keeping its central conflict alive from beginning to end.
