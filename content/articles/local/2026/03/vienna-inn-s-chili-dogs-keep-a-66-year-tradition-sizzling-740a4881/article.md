Vienna Inn has served generations of customers from its longtime home at 120 Maple Avenue East since Mike and Mollie Abraham opened the restaurant in 1960.

Built around simple, hearty food, the neighborhood eatery became especially known for its chili dogs. It now reportedly sells more than 10,000 of them each month, typically loaded with chili, cheese, mustard and onions.

Vienna native Marty Volk took ownership in 2000 with a commitment to preserve what regulars loved. The restaurant has retained its wood-paneled interior, checkered floors, worn booths, long bar and walls filled with local sports memorabilia and trophies.

The throwback touches extend to the service. Customers order at the counter, use a self-serve soda station and follow an honor-system approach to payment.

Beyond chili dogs, the menu includes burgers, sandwiches, fries, onion rings, salads and other lighter choices. Breakfast is served Monday through Saturday mornings, with offerings such as Fat French Toast, hotcakes, omelets, eggs and home fries.

The familiar setting has helped make the restaurant a gathering place for families, youth teams, longtime friends and multiple generations of regulars. Its displays preserve memories of local teams, community events and neighborhood celebrations.

In 2024, Northern Virginia Magazine readers voted Vienna Inn the region’s best breakfast, selecting it from a field of more than 30 establishments.

The restaurant has also marked anniversaries with fundraisers, children’s nights and special promotions. During the COVID-19 pandemic, it added takeout, curbside pickup and outdoor tents while helping provide meals for first responders and families facing food insecurity.

After more than six decades, Vienna Inn’s appeal remains rooted in continuity. The booths, casual atmosphere and signature chili dogs offer regulars a connection to earlier visits while introducing another generation to one of Vienna’s enduring institutions.
