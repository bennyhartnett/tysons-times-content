Democrats are crowding into a Virginia congressional race before voters have approved the district they hope to represent.

Olivia Troye, a former aide to Republican Vice President Mike Pence who became a prominent critic of President Donald Trump, announced Tuesday that she will seek the proposed 7th Congressional District seat. It is one of five planned districts that would include parts of Fairfax County.

Virginia voters will decide April 21 whether to adopt the Democratic-drawn congressional map. The plan could help Democrats capture four additional U.S. House seats and give the party an advantage in 10 of Virginia’s 11 districts.

“I just feel like we need people that are going to stand up and fight,” Troye said. “And I’m not seeing that right now, across the Democratic and Republican parties.”

The proposed 7th District was drawn to strongly favor Democrats in a general election. Its territory currently falls within six separate congressional districts.

About half a dozen Democrats have already announced campaigns contingent on the map’s approval. The field includes former Virginia first lady Dorothy McAuliffe and former federal prosecutor J.P. Cooney, who served as a deputy to special counsel Jack Smith before Trump fired him.

State Sen. Saddam Azlan Salim, a Democrat representing the 37th District, and Del. Dan Helmer, a Democrat representing the 10th District, have also entered the race.

The proposed district would include Seven Corners, Idylwood, West Falls Church, Annandale, Fairfax Station, Burke and West Springfield in Fairfax County. Its population center would sit in heavily Democratic Northern Virginia, while its boundaries would extend far into rural areas that generally favor Republicans.

Other proposed districts are attracting crowded Democratic primaries, but the prospective 7th District stands out for its unusually large roster of prominent contenders.

Virginia’s proposal is part of a broader partisan redistricting battle ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, when Democrats hope to regain the House majority and gain greater power to block Trump’s agenda.

The state’s current map produced a delegation of six Democrats and five Republicans. Under the proposed boundaries, Democrats would be favored in all but one district.

The latest redistricting push began after Trump urged Texas Republicans last year to approve boundaries intended to deliver as many as five additional GOP House seats. California voters answered with a Democratic-friendly plan, while Missouri, North Carolina and Ohio adopted maps benefiting Republicans.

Maryland Democrats rejected a plan this week that would have favored their party. Florida Republicans, meanwhile, are moving forward with another redistricting proposal.
