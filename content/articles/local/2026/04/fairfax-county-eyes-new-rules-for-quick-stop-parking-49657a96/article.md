Fairfax County is considering new parking standards as delivery drivers, rideshare vehicles and curbside pickup customers increasingly compete for places to make brief stops.

The county currently has no regulations specifically governing short-term parking, despite completing its first comprehensive parking-rule overhaul in 35 years in 2023. Some properties voluntarily provide pickup or time-limited spaces, but those accommodations are not required.

The Fairfax County Department of Transportation has begun examining whether the county should establish a standard or requirement for short-term spaces, similar to existing rules for off-street parking, loading zones and accessible spaces.

“We are in the early stages of this analysis,” an FCDOT spokesperson said, adding that no proposal has been presented to the Board of Supervisors or released for public feedback.

The review will consider why short-term parking standards have not previously been adopted and how demand has evolved with the growth of deliveries, ridesharing and other quick-stop services.

County staff are still collecting information, and the spokesperson cautioned that it is too early to determine whether standards are necessary or what form they could take. A proposal could emerge as early as this summer, though no firm timetable has been established.

## Reston Complaints Highlight Parking Gaps

Hunter Mill District Supervisor Walter Alcorn said the county’s zoning ordinance does not adequately address the varied parking demands found in increasingly urban areas such as Tysons and parts of Reston.

Alcorn, vice chair of the Board of Supervisors’ transportation committee, said complaints have surfaced on several Reston roads where delivery and rideshare drivers sometimes block traffic because they lack a place to pull over.

His office has been working with residents and businesses along Reston Station Boulevard east of Wiehle Avenue after concerns arose about limited parking. Some residents worry that additional planned development will intensify the problem.

Reported issues include double parking and vehicles blocking the street. People who neither live in the area nor visit nearby businesses have also been leaving cars in free spaces throughout the day rather than paying to park at Reston Station.

Those concerns have prompted county staff to explore broader parking-program updates while supervisors address some problems street by street, Alcorn said.

## Paid Parking Remains Another Possibility

Fairfax County’s Parking Reimagined initiative, adopted in September 2023, created tiered parking requirements based on development type and density. It also introduced bicycle parking standards and revised loading-space rules, including an option for buildings smaller than 10,000 square feet to use a receiving facility for deliveries.

Alcorn called the changes successful overall but said the county needs greater flexibility for short-term and street parking. In mixed-use areas with homes above street-facing stores, he said, curb spaces should logically serve short-term retail customers while longer stays are handled elsewhere.

Paid street parking could offer another management tool. It is common in Washington, Arlington and Alexandria but remains limited in Fairfax County to a few private developments, including Reston Town Center.

County staff recommended paid street parking for Tysons in 2021 but did not support it at the time for Reston’s transit station areas. Those recommendations have not been implemented, and Alcorn said he knows of no imminent plans to do so.

Fairfax County still lacks a broader paid street-parking system, he said, but the approach is worth considering as officials look for more efficient ways to serve residents and businesses.
