Arlington County’s Project PEACE marked its 20th anniversary April 28, celebrating its transformation from a fragmented crisis response into a coordinated support system for survivors of domestic and sexual violence.

Project PEACE Coordinator LaToya Young said the initiative was co-founded in 2006 by Virginia State Sen. Barbara Favola. At the time, survivors often had to contact multiple organizations on their own, with little coordination or follow-up among agencies.

“Today is a time for celebration and reflection to honor our leaders but also the survivors and partners,” Young said. “And to reflect on what comes next.”

Project PEACE initially created a brochure identifying available housing, shelter and legal services. It has since adopted a “warm handoff” approach in which staff directly connect survivors with appropriate organizations and remain involved afterward.

That may include connecting someone with Doorways, a nonprofit providing services, shelter and supportive housing for survivors of domestic violence and sexual assault and their families. Project PEACE can also link survivors with police or legal services.

“We don’t just support them on paper or phone,” Young said. “We actively connect with the person and stay with them. It helps reduce their stress, too.”

Young said the initiative has learned that survivors often face complex needs and unequal access to assistance. People of color and immigrants can encounter substantial barriers, while cultural concerns may affect whether someone feels safe seeking help.

Project PEACE is also working to reduce “system fatigue” caused by multiple entry points and repeated requests for assistance. The goal, Young said, is to simplify access and prevent anyone from falling through the cracks.

Throughout 2024, representatives from partner agencies met monthly in goal-oriented groups. They addressed immediate community needs while identifying obstacles to survivor-centered, trauma-informed support.

The anniversary ceremony honored victims who have died, recognized survivors and renewed the program’s commitment to listening and improving.

Attendees included representatives from the Arlington County Police Department, Sheriff’s Office and Magistrate’s Office, along with longtime community partners. Speakers included Favola, Arlington County Board Member Maureen Coffey, Department of Human Services leaders and Young.
