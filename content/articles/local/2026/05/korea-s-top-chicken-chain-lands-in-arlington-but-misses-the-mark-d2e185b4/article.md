BHC Chicken brought one of South Korea’s biggest fast-food brands to Virginia when it opened its first Arlington location May 15. The menu stretches beyond Korean fried chicken to tteokbokki, kimchi fried rice and chicken sandwiches, but the chain’s celebrated flavors deliver uneven results.

The $16 Rose Rabokki earned 3.8 out of 5. The dish combines ramen noodles, fish cakes, fried sausages, a hard-boiled egg and chewy rice cakes in a spicy sauce. Its range of ingredients keeps the bowl interesting, with soft, subtly savory fish cakes standing out.

Other elements are less successful. The sausages taste good on their own, but their smokiness clashes with the rest of the dish. An overcooked egg helps temper the heat, yet the supposedly milder Rose sauce remains so spicy that it overwhelms nearly every other flavor.

The $10.50 Bburinkle Wings fare better, earning 4 out of 5. BHC’s signature seasoning blends blue cheese, cheddar, onion and garlic, though a heavy sweetness largely buries those savory notes.

The chicken itself is the highlight. Fresh, tender meat contrasts sharply with a crisp crust created by double-frying each wing with potato starch. Bburinkle Mayo Sauce, made with yogurt and Emmental cheese, adds enough saltiness and tang to rein in the seasoning’s sugary finish.

Sweet & Spicy Wings, also priced at $10.50, receive 3.5 out of 5. They retain the same juicy chicken and crunchy coating, this time covered in a traditional Korean glaze that delivers both sweetness and serious heat.

That heat can become difficult to tolerate without iced water. A $1.50 side of pickled radish offers welcome sweet-and-salty relief, although charging extra for the accompaniment feels unnecessary. As with the rabokki, the spice eventually drowns out the dish’s other qualities.

The $5.50 Bburinkle French Fries are the weakest item, scoring 2.5 out of 5. Parmesan cheese and garlic powder give them an appealing appearance, and the serving is generous enough for two people. But even when fresh, the fries are soggy. Their flavor resembles the Bburinkle Wings without the satisfying texture, leaving them indistinguishable from countless other fast-food sides.

BHC Chicken’s Arlington debut shows flashes of promise, particularly in the quality and crunch of its fried chicken. Still, overpowering spice, excessive sweetness and disappointing fries prevent the signature flavors from making a lasting impression. Despite attractive presentation and the brand’s strong reputation, the experience falls short of its “Better, Happier Choice” motto.
