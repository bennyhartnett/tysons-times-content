More than 200 vintage vendors from across the country will gather at the Clarke County Fairgrounds in Berryville for the 28th Lucketts Spring Market, running May 15-17, 2026.

The open-air market will feature American farmhouse and European country pieces, Mid-Century furnishings, garden accessories, textiles, vintage clothing and other finds. Southern Living has previously named the event among the South’s best antique shows.

Artist Marian Parsons, also known as Miss Mustard Seed, will return Friday, May 15, for a meet-and-greet and live painting demonstration from 1 to 4 p.m. Parsons formerly sold milk-painted furniture and antiques at the market and now creates oil paintings of animals, landscapes and still-life scenes.

Participating businesses include Ekster Antiques, Hip and Humble, Naked Goat Soap Company, Meanwhile Back On the Farm, Canvas and Cargo, Walsh Family Wine, JBS Mercantile, Vintage Marshall, Turkish Towels, Vanish Brewery, Chartreuse and Co., Loudoun Market and Distressed Furniture Company.

The weekend will also offer live music, food trucks, a children’s play area, the Vanish Beer Garden and Walsh Family wine.

Owner Suzanne Eblen said the markets have become a gathering place for Lucketts enthusiasts, their friends and families. Organizers aim to keep the experience fresh by mixing returning and new vendors with playful displays and opportunities to hunt for distinctive items.

## Hours and admission

The market will be held at the Clarke County Fairgrounds, 890 W. Main Street in Berryville.

Early buyers may enter from 8:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. Friday, May 15. Friday general admission runs from 12:30 to 5 p.m. General admission hours are 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Saturday, May 16, and Sunday, May 17.

A $50 early-buyer ticket includes admission on all three days. One-day general admission costs $20 and will be sold at the gate. Children younger than 12 enter free.

Tickets are nonrefundable, and the event will proceed rain or shine. Dogs are not permitted.

The Old Lucketts Store, located in Leesburg, occupies a restored general store and family home built by the Luckett family in 1879. Eblen, her husband Pat and a team of friends and workers restored the property in 1996. It now houses more than 35 dealers specializing in antiques and vintage goods and also hosts seasonal markets and sales.
