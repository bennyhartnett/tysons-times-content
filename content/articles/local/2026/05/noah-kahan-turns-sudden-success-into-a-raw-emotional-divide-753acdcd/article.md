Noah Kahan released his fourth studio album, *The Great Divide*, on April 24, delivering 17 tracks rooted in his familiar folk sound. Later that night, he added four more songs with *The Great Divide: The Last of the Bugs*.

Natural, unvarnished melodies support Kahan’s distinctive voice as the album moves through separate emotional narratives. Each track has its own character, but recurring themes of distance, loss, fractured relationships and the disorienting effects of fame hold the project together.

The opening track, “End of August,” begins as a relaxed, largely instrumental piece. Its changing seasons reflect the speed of life’s transitions and the bittersweet pull of nostalgia. Kahan also explores the feeling that his small hometown is gradually slipping away as he moves toward new experiences.

Previously released on January 30, the sixth track and title song captures the album’s central tension. “The Great Divide” examines how sudden success can pull someone away from a once-comfortable reality, creating distance as relationships weaken over time.

“Deny, Deny, Deny” is among the album’s most candid moments. It returns to conflict and deteriorating relationships while digging into their consequences, weighing accountability and action against continued loyalty.

The lengthy closing track, “Dan,” shifts the record toward a more hopeful tone. Its focus on enduring relationships and shared vulnerability offers an uplifting counterpoint to much of what comes before it.

*The Great Divide* continues Kahan’s exploration of distance, fame and internal conflict while adding new dimensions of emotional loss, moral responsibility and lasting connection. Its melodies may not represent a dramatic departure, but their warmth and restraint strengthen the album’s sensitive emotional core.
