Mental health support is available close to home for Northern Virginia residents, with trained crisis workers answering calls, texts and online chats through the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

HopeLink, a regional mental health nonprofit serving the community since 1963, operates the 988 service for Northern Virginia. Its crisis workers are trained to listen, assess safety, de-escalate emergencies and help people find appropriate local services when needed.

The organization’s crisis workers answered nearly 400,000 calls, chats and texts last year from people facing life crises. Although every conversation is different, workers frequently hear a version of the same question: “Will I be okay?”

One crisis worker said the goal is to remain with people through their uncertainty and pain, helping them reach a point where they can pursue longer-term support and envision a way forward.

The need is widespread. More than 59 million people nationwide experience mental illness, and nearly 46% of Americans are expected to meet the criteria for a diagnosable mental health condition during their lives, according to Mental Health America.

Mental Health Awareness Month in May offers a reminder that community members can help by recognizing signs of distress and responding directly.

Warning signs can include talking about wanting to die, having no reason to live, feeling trapped or believing that one is a burden to others. When someone appears to be in emotional pain, asking plainly whether they are considering suicide can open an important conversation.

People can also help connect someone in immediate crisis with support by encouraging them to call or text 988. Additional assistance is available through HopeLink and local county community services boards.

Supporting mental health nonprofits, advocating for government funding and contacting elected officials about mental health programs and legislation are other ways residents can contribute.

While mental health services have made progress, difficult periods can intensify the demand for care. Residents are encouraged to learn the warning signs, check on loved ones and use the local resources available throughout the year.
