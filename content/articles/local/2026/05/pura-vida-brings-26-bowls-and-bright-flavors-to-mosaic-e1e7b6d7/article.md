Pura Vida Miami has opened its first Virginia location in Fairfax’s Mosaic District, pairing an inviting atmosphere and attentive service with prices that may make it more of an occasional stop than an everyday destination.

The health-focused restaurant debuted Thursday with all-day breakfast dishes, bowls and smoothies. Its white exterior, large windows, rooftop and patio seating, and abundant greenery create a bright, polished look. Inside, strong lighting, faux ferns and cozy seating continue the airy design.

Customers order at a counter near the back, where menus and prepared grab-and-go items are available. Despite the challenges of opening day, employees were friendly, accommodated last-minute order changes and checked frequently on the table. Complimentary tote bags from the restaurant’s retail section were also handed out.

The food was generally good, though the quality did not always justify the premium prices.

## A standout breakfast sandwich

The $14.95 Perfect Egg Sandwich earned a 4.5-out-of-5 rating and ranked among the few items that felt worth the expense.

Served on a toasted artisanal plain bagel, it combines pasture-raised soft-boiled eggs, arugula, tomato, mozzarella, smashed avocado, olive oil and red pepper flakes. The bagel was excellent, the tomato was notably ripe and flavorful, and the eggs were cooked perfectly. Red pepper flakes supplied a welcome touch of heat.

## A costly bowl that falls short

At $25.95, the Avocado Salmon bowl was the most expensive item sampled and received a 3.5-out-of-5 rating.

Roasted salmon, cilantro jasmine rice, arugula, roasted corn and the bowl’s other ingredients worked well together, but the result was merely satisfactory. Nothing about it stood out enough to warrant the price.

## Mixed results from the smoothies

The $11.95 Chocolate PB smoothie was the least impressive order, scoring 2.5 out of 5. Its combination of peanut butter, banana, almond milk, raw honey and organic grass-fed chocolate whey protein tasted much like a standard chocolate, banana and peanut butter smoothie. It was enjoyable but unremarkable, especially at that price.

The $12.95 Coco Azul fared considerably better, receiving a 4-out-of-5 rating. Coconut water, banana, pineapple, mango, blue spirulina, collagen protein, hemp seeds and shaved coconut produced a fresh and distinctive flavor. Its cost makes it difficult to recommend alongside a full meal, but it could work well on its own as breakfast or a midday snack.

Pura Vida’s Virginia debut delivers healthy options, attractive surroundings and strong service, with a few dishes capable of impressing. The steep menu prices, however, give diners reason to choose carefully—and perhaps visit sparingly.
