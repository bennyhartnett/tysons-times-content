*Sheep Detective* sounds like a lightweight comedy built around an absurd premise: a flock of sheep trying to solve a murder. Instead, the film delivers an unexpectedly clever mystery and a moving exploration of prejudice, grief and memory without sacrificing its relentless humor.

The movie embraces its ridiculous concept from the beginning. Nearly every scene contains a joke, often involving a sheep-related pun, and that full commitment makes the comedy consistently entertaining.

Its mystery is equally effective. Rather than settling into a predictable path, the story repeatedly changes direction and gives each clue significance. The twists keep the outcome uncertain, building toward a surprising but carefully constructed final reveal.

The film’s greatest strength, however, is the emotional weight beneath the comedy. One major storyline centers on the “winter lamb,” born in winter rather than spring. Considered weak and unusual, winter lambs are often rejected by their flocks and left alone.

As the story unfolds, the winter lamb comes to represent the harm caused by judging others through assumptions instead of character. Some of the film’s strongest moments follow characters learning to welcome those they once pushed away because of fear or prejudice.

The movie also finds an inventive way to examine grief. When something unpleasant happens, the sheep collectively choose to forget it “on the count of three.” Their casual erasure of painful experiences initially produces some of the film’s funniest scenes.

Gradually, that running joke becomes a difficult question: Is forgetting pain really a healthy way to survive it?

One sheep cannot discard memories as easily as the others. He carries everything the flock has chosen to forget, including its most painful experiences. What first appears to be a curse eventually reveals its value. Remembering can hurt, but erasing sorrow can also erase the love attached to the people and moments that have been lost.

By the end, *Sheep Detective* argues that sadness and grief should not simply be ignored. Even heartbreaking memories matter. Beneath the puns and murder-mystery antics is a sincere message about acceptance, loss and the importance of remembering—making the film far more affecting than its premise suggests.
