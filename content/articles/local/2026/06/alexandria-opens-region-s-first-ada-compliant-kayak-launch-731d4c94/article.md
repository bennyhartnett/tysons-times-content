Nearly a decade of planning culminated May 9 with the opening of a public kayak launch designed to make Alexandria’s waterways more accessible to paddlers of all abilities.

City officials and community members marked the occasion with a ribbon-cutting ceremony at Four Mile Run Park. The roughly $600,000 facility is located at 3700 Commonwealth Avenue in Alexandria’s Arlandria neighborhood.

Officials described it as the first ADA-compliant kayak access point of its kind in the Washington, D.C., metropolitan area. Its features include a raised boardwalk ramp with accessible slopes, a gangway to a floating dock, a wheelchair transfer platform and rollers that help paddlers enter and leave the water.

The city developed the launch in partnership with the Four Mile Run Conservancy. Hashim Taylor, Alexandria’s director of Recreation, Parks and Cultural Activities, said the project demonstrates what public-private cooperation can achieve through shared goals and investment.

Located along the tidal portion of Four Mile Run, a Potomac River tributary, the launch provides access to calm, shallow water suitable for beginners. It also connects paddlers to the Potomac River Water Trail and nearby wetlands and natural areas that were previously difficult to reach.

The site is open from dawn to dusk for kayaks, canoes and other non-motorized watercraft.

Funding came from grants, local contributions, Alexandria’s Community Matching Fund and several environmental organizations. Taylor credited the Four Mile Run Conservancy with championing the project and raising money for it since 2017.

The launch received a Governor’s Environmental Excellence Award earlier this year for its contributions to environmental stewardship and sustainable outdoor recreation.

Kurt Moser, president of the Four Mile Run Conservancy, said the new access would help more people appreciate Four Mile Run as a natural resource and an important pocket of biodiversity within the city.

Officials also expect the facility to support environmental education and community events. Plans include guided paddling tours, cleanup efforts and youth programs centered on the local ecosystem.

The grand opening featured remarks from city leaders, music and family activities. Many attendees brought kayaks and tested the new launch themselves.
