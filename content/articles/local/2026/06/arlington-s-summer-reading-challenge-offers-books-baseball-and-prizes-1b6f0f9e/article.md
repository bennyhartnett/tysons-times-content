Arlington Public Library launched its Summer Reading Challenge at local branches Saturday, June 6, drawing children with books, a Washington Nationals mascot and a garden-themed treat.

At the “Plant a Seed, Read” activity, teenage volunteers served chocolate pudding cups topped with gummy worms to resemble rich garden soil. Young readers Roun Erikson and Reese Tsang lined up for the treats, with Erikson saying she had already begun reading. Louise Sfregola, who already had a library card, stood nearby.

The kickoff unfolded on a hot, muggy day. Jordan Ebikwi, a graduating Yorktown High School senior, helped run the activity as part of a three-week senior experience at the library.

“I just thought it would be a fun place to have my internship,” Ebikwi said.

Abe, one of the Washington Nationals’ Racing Presidents, circulated outside the library and fanned volunteers and other visitors seeking relief from the heat.

The challenge asks participants to read on 30 days between June 1 and Sept. 1. There is no required amount of reading each day, and the days do not need to be consecutive. Participants track their activity on a calendar, complete a form online or from the library and return it to any library branch.

Those who finish receive a free book and two Washington Nationals tickets. Each completion also generates a $1 donation supporting the library’s partnership with the Friends of Arlington Public Library.

Participants who read for another 30 days can become Super Readers. They receive a plantable flower or herb pot and a certificate, along with entry into a drawing for a $100 Visa gift card or tickets to the Washington Mystics or Washington Spirit.

The program is designed to help children and teenagers avoid losing academic ground during the summer while encouraging families to read or listen to books together.
