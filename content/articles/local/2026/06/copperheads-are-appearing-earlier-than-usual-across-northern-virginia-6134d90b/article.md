Copperheads are turning up near homes, gardens and pools earlier than expected in the D.C. area after a stretch of erratic weather.

K2C Snake Consultants, a nonprofit that captures and relocates snakes from Virginia homes and farms, typically handles 40 to 50 copperheads each summer. Most calls usually come in July and August.

This year, however, swings from extreme heat to chilly rain and then dry conditions appear to have accelerated the activity.

Co-owner Bill Crisp said the organization had recently removed about 20 copperheads in Prince William County, along with one or two in Fairfax County. Many were found against houses, near stairs and steps, in landscaping and gardens, or around swimming pools.

Copperheads commonly shelter in plants, rock piles and woodpiles, where they can also find prey. Crisp emphasized that the snakes generally want to avoid people and pets rather than chase or bite them.

Still, an increase in encounters can bring more bites. Anyone bitten should remain hydrated and reach a hospital as quickly as possible, Crisp said.

He advised against applying a cold compress or tourniquet, cutting the wound, attempting to remove the venom by suction, or taking medication before receiving medical guidance.

People also should not attempt to catch the snake or bring it to the emergency room. If it can be done safely, a photograph can help hospital staff identify it.
