*Forbidden Fruits* is a chaotic, visually distinctive teen drama that may delight viewers with its excess while leaving them unsure what it ultimately wants to say.

The film centers on Apple, Cherry and Fig, members of a fruit-named sisterhood who welcome a newcomer called Pumpkin. Her arrival exposes secrets and sends the group’s already unhealthy friendships spiraling into increasingly dramatic territory.

This is no restrained coming-of-age story. Witchcraft and curses complicate the girls’ conflicts, while nearly every emotion and confrontation is pushed to an extreme. The two-hour movie contains enough turmoil to fill an entire reality television season.

The heightened performances suit that approach. Rather than portraying the characters realistically, the actors embrace exaggeration to reinforce the film’s theatrical style and themes.

Costume design is among the movie’s strongest elements. Each character’s wardrobe reflects her role within the group: Apple, the queen bee and head witch, wears bold looks that command attention. Cherry’s playful, revealing outfits match her ditzy personality, while Fig’s edgier clothing complements her confidence. Pumpkin’s sweet, innocent wardrobe distinguishes her as someone who does not quite belong in the clique.

Those clear visual identities help the characters stand apart as their competing personalities fight for attention.

The movie also knows how to maintain suspense. Even when its direction is difficult to embrace, escalating tension and a steady supply of startling moments keep the story compelling. Each poor decision leads to an even worse one, making the group’s unraveling strangely entertaining.

The humor is less successful. Frequent attempts to mimic Gen Z speech feel forced, particularly the characters’ repeated use of words such as “babe.” Instead of sounding natural, the dialogue often resembles an adult’s strained imitation of younger people.

The film’s greatest weakness is its ending. Much of the story appears to be building toward a meaningful conclusion about toxic friendships, false sisterhood and social hierarchies. Yet the downfall of certain characters, along with a barrage of twists and revelations, distracts from those ideas.

The final twist is especially frustrating. The concept itself is not necessarily flawed, but the movie does too little to prepare for it. Viewers may understand what happened while remaining uncertain about what it was supposed to mean.

Despite those shortcomings, *Forbidden Fruits* remains an entertaining and memorable experience. Its stylish costumes, quirky atmosphere and relentless drama cast a stronger spell than its muddled finale.
