Fairfax County’s long-running debate over Lake Accotink appears headed toward a compromise: preserving a smaller lake in Springfield rather than restoring the existing lake in full.

Years of sediment buildup have threatened the lake, while complete preservation would cost millions of dollars. The smaller-lake proposal has emerged as a less costly alternative, but some residents remain opposed.

“Save the lake as it is. It is a treasure among the suburban sprawl,” Mike Merrigan wrote on social media.

Residents can learn more at a June 6 open house from 3 to 5 p.m. at Kings Glen Elementary School, 5401 Danbury Forest Drive in Springfield. Officials plan to discuss a dam assessment, provide an update on the feasibility study and gather public feedback. Meeting materials will later be posted online.

Lake supporters are encouraging a strong turnout, arguing that low attendance could be interpreted as a lack of public interest.

The Fairfax County Board of Supervisors unanimously advanced the smaller-lake option on Jan. 23, 2024. Then-Braddock District Supervisor James Walkinshaw said the proposal reflected recommendations from the Task Force on the Future of Lake Accotink and the community’s desire for sustainable preservation. Walkinshaw has since become a member of Congress.

The Smaller Lake Accotink Preservation Feasibility Study is examining conditions in Accotink Creek and Lake Accotink. It is also evaluating ways to preserve a lake covering 20 to 40 acres with a depth of 4 to 8 feet.

Residents unable to attend the meeting can find additional information and opportunities to comment at savelakeaccotink.org.
