Fairfax County wrapped up a busy week with several major developments that captured residents' attention, from tragic accidents to exciting retail announcements and ongoing infrastructure concerns.

## Week's Most-Read Stories

A child riding an e-scooter was killed in a Herndon-area crash, drawing nearly 17,500 readers and making it the most-viewed story of the week. Fairfax County Police later announced that reckless driving was likely not a factor in the collision that claimed the 9-year-old's life.

A special education teacher at a McLean school was charged with assaulting a student, garnering over 10,900 views. Meanwhile, a Chantilly man was found not guilty due to insanity in connection with a Reston gym shooting.

## Retail and Restaurant Moves

Eataly officially confirmed it will open a store at Tysons Corner Center, news that drew nearly 8,900 readers eager for the Italian marketplace's arrival.

A Japanese department store announced plans for a second Virginia location in Seven Corners, expanding its regional footprint beyond its current presence.

On the closure front, Alta Strada is shuttering after nearly a decade at the Mosaic District, and Ornery Beer Company will close its Fairfax City pub while maintaining its Herndon location. Vienna's Clarity restaurant is bucking the trend, opening a second location in Reston.

## County Developments

A Lego display featuring Fairfax County landmarks was unveiled in Tysons as part of the county's 250th anniversary celebration.

The Hunter Mill District planning commissioner retired after nine years of service, and the Fairfax board is seeking a timeline for a vehicle noise monitoring pilot program.

A Virginia study examining data centers' impact on groundwater is calling for tighter regulations, a timely issue as the region continues to see rapid data center development.

Herndon and Fairfax City recorded the largest apartment rent drops in the D.C. region last month, offering some relief for renters in those areas.

## Metro Service Disruption

Riders should plan ahead this weekend as track work on Metro's Orange Line will reduce service in Fairfax County.
