A tree from a neighboring property crashed through the roof of the Robert Wiehle House at 1830 Old Reston Avenue during a storm on July 5, concentrating damage on the roof and one of the home's two second-floor bedrooms. The Fairfax County Fire Marshal's Office declared the 130-year-old Victorian structure unsafe to enter, leaving the owner without a full damage assessment and facing a decision about how to proceed.

The house, constructed around 1895 for J. Robert Wiehle, nephew of the town's founder, has been listed in Fairfax County's Inventory of Historic Sites since 1977. It is one of three surviving buildings from the Town of Wiehle, Dr. Carl A. Max Wiehle's planned community that was incorporated in 1898 but largely failed after the doctor's death from pneumonia in 1901, with only 12 residential lots sold.

The owner, who purchased the property for $350,000 in 2012, faces compounding obstacles to restoration. Insurance companies have refused to cover the property since April 2025 because of its age. Repairing the damage without financial help, he said, is beyond the family's current means.

"We're not really at that stable financial situation where I can just fix it out of pocket unless I get a partnership, like a developer or somebody to come and fix it for us," the owner said.

The owner has reached out to Fairfax County's historic resources branch and says he has explored multiple paths forward. He previously discussed donating the house to the Reston Museum if he could retain use of the remaining lot. He has also indicated openness to county assistance, development partnerships, or sale to a preservation-minded buyer.

"If Fairfax County or Reston are willing to fix and then help us also along the way, we'd be more than happy to work [with them] and cooperate in preserving that again in any way possible," he said.

Walter Alcorn, supervisor for the Hunter Mill District, expressed hope that the house could be repaired and preserved but acknowledged the county's limited authority over privately owned property. The timing of the damage comes as the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors on July 14 approved redevelopment plans for the nearby Bowman House at 1850 Old Reston Avenue that include retaining and refurbishing that 1899 historic residence—a potential model for preserving similar structures in the area.

The former Town of Wiehle's old hall, later used as a distillery, remains the only one of the three surviving buildings listed in both the Virginia Landmarks Register and the National Register of Historic Places. Fairfax County and Preservation Virginia have designated it as endangered because of the risk of loss through development or neglect.

The owner described the Wiehle House's historical significance bluntly: "This is very, very unique, the way it is."
