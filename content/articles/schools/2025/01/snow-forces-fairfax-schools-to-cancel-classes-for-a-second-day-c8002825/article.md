Fairfax County Public Schools canceled classes and all activities on school grounds for Tuesday, Jan. 7, giving students a second additional day of winter break as another round of snow moved across the region.

School-aged child care centers were also closed, while employees not designated as essential received administrative leave.

The National Weather Service said in a 2 p.m. update on Monday, Jan. 6, that snow would intensify through about 11 p.m. Another 1 to 3 inches of accumulation was possible before the storm left the Washington area.

The broader forecast called for 6 to 12 inches, prompting the Virginia Department of Transportation to advise residents against driving. Many Fairfax County businesses closed or reduced their hours.

Virginia State Police responded to 33 crashes in Northern Virginia by 11 a.m., including three with reported injuries. From 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., another 25 crashes were reported, two involving injuries.

State police said troopers had been working 12-hour shifts since Sunday and that additional personnel were available. The agency urged motorists to stay off the roads so VDOT crews could continue plowing.

Nationally, at least four deaths were linked to the storm, all involving vehicle crashes in Kansas and Missouri.

Some flights at Reagan National Airport were canceled. Dulles International Airport advised travelers to come to the airport only if their flights were confirmed.

Fairfax Connector restored limited service at 2:30 p.m. after suspending operations earlier Monday. Buses began running on a Sunday schedule along routes 171, 310, 401, 402, 463, 950 and RIBS 1.

The transit system expected to resume its regular schedule Tuesday, although lingering ice and snow could cause minor delays. Riders were encouraged to allow more time and monitor service updates.

Fairfax County also reminded residents to use restraint when spreading salt. Salt will not melt snow when pavement temperatures fall below 15 degrees, and excessive use can damage drinking water, aquatic life, vegetation, vehicles, pipes and infrastructure.

Across the county, residents made the most of the snow. Children and families sledded on hills in Burke and Vienna, walkers ventured onto snow-covered paths, and homeowners and workers cleared driveways, sidewalks and public spaces.

Snow blanketed the Washington & Old Dominion Trail near Maple Avenue, Vienna Town Hall, Waters Field and the Vienna Volunteer Fire Department. Plows worked along designated emergency routes, while crews cleared areas around Reston Town Center.
