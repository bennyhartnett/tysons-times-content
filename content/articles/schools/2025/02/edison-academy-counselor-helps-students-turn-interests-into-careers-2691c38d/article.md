Cassandra Deedy’s work at Edison Academy stretches well beyond helping students select courses. The longtime counselor connects teenagers with specialized career programs while providing personal support during school, at events and at major moments in their lives.

Her efforts take on added significance during Career and Technical Education Month in February. Career and technical education courses teach the practical skills used in various occupations while preparing students for college or entry-level jobs.

Fairfax County Public Schools offers a core group of CTE courses at every middle and high school, although selections vary by campus. Subjects include business and information technology, family and consumer sciences, health and medical sciences, marketing, technology and engineering, and trade and industrial education.

Six FCPS high schools house academies, which provide advanced technical and specialized courses that combine career training with academic preparation. As Edison Academy’s counselor, Deedy explains those opportunities to students, families and counselors throughout the school system.

Deedy has worked as a school counselor for nearly 30 years, including almost two decades at Edison High School and Edison Academy. She was named a finalist for the 2024 Outstanding School-Based Professional Employee honor.

Edison counselor David Schlemmer said Deedy’s outreach, family correspondence, counselor training and school visits are crucial to the academy’s programs. What distinguishes her most, he said, is the individual care and attention she gives students.

Deedy opens her office during lunch each day for students with sensory needs. She also supports students at academy activities and athletic games after school and on weekends. Her involvement has extended to attending one student’s naturalization ceremony and another’s graduation from Navy basic training.

Principal Amanda Burke said Deedy’s readiness to help can be seen across school life.

Deedy also mentors student ambassadors and helps them prepare for future careers. Other schools have adopted her ambassador model for their own leadership programs.

She created a visitation program that allows students and counselors to experience academy classes firsthand. She also coordinates elective fairs and academy promotional materials, helping prospective students understand the available programs.

Edison is one of seven FCPS academies and draws students from 14 feeder high schools. Last year, more than 2,000 students applied for 1,200 available places in its classes.

Beyond her student-focused responsibilities, Deedy participates in employee wellness activities and community events. Schlemmer said her consistent concern for others demonstrates what educators can accomplish.
