Fairfax High School brought Shakespeare’s tangled web of romance, deception and wounded pride into the modern era with an energetic production of “Much Ado About Nothing.”

Written in the early 1600s, the comedy follows two couples whose relationships are tested by schemes, misunderstandings and sharp exchanges. Fairfax’s version retained that familiar battle between devotion and deceit while adding contemporary slang, gender-swapped roles and a setting inspired by a Santorini resort.

Isaac Frenza played Benedick with a swaggering confidence, strutting across the stage and trading cutting remarks with Kat Pascual’s Beatrice. Scoffs, dismissive gestures and other physical choices made the pair’s apparent contempt unmistakable.

As their relationship changed, Frenza and Pascual revealed more vulnerable sides of their characters. Frenza conveyed Benedick’s anger during a confrontation with his former best friend Claudio, while Pascual allowed Beatrice’s rigid composure to collapse into tears over her cousin’s reported death. Their love soliloquies further traced the characters’ emotional growth.

Nate Wheeler Smith delivered another dramatic transformation as Claudio. His character began as an earnest admirer gazing tenderly at his future wife, then erupted with a booming voice and visible rage while publicly rejecting and humiliating Hero.

As Don Pedro, Tristan Farmer served as Claudio’s dependable ally while helping the production regain its comic momentum during heavier scenes. His performance shifted between forceful strides and sudden boyish movements, bringing levity back at crucial moments.

The Mediterranean-inspired environment was central to the show’s modern identity. Set designers Maya Gijon and Griffin Reinsel, working with the Much Ado Set Crew, created blue-and-white Greek-style buildings and a pool filled with real water. A thrust-stage arrangement placed audience members directly onstage, making the resort setting feel especially intimate.

Lighting designer Nikolai Crowl and the Much Ado Lighting Crew reinforced the production’s themes through specialized effects and sharply contrasting colors.

By combining Shakespeare’s language and romantic complications with modern humor and a vacation-resort aesthetic, Fairfax High created a lively interpretation of “Much Ado About Nothing.” Beneath its comic misunderstandings, the production preserved the play’s enduring examination of love, pride and the damage caused by deception.
