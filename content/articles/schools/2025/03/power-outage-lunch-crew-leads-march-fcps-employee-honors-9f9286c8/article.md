Fairfax County Public Schools recognized dozens of employees and teams through its March 2025 FCPS CARES program, highlighting acts that ranged from feeding more than 1,600 students during a power outage to performing the Heimlich maneuver on a student.

Amanda Alsbaugh of Oak View Elementary was named the Region 4 FCPS CARES of the Quarter recipient for spring 2025. The school’s special education and local screening chair was honored for mentoring teachers, supporting administrators and advocating for children throughout assessment and eligibility processes.

## Regional honorees

Central Office recognition went to Food and Nutrition Services employees Tatiana Sanchez, Maria Angulo, Max Mongkolrattanatorn, Yolanda Barboza, Nubia Bonilla Euceda, Pakamas Davidson, Kim Ensley, Graciela Guevara, Seung Hur, Sun Kang, Japinder Kaur, Paramjit Kaur, Mi Kim, Mija Lee, Hue Long-Tran, Phuc Luu, Elka Morales Lopez, Ranjit Nijjar, Li Wang and Jun Zhu.

When Robinson Secondary School suddenly lost power on March 3, the team prepared meals for more than 1,600 students in a dim kitchen. Employees used cellphone flashlights to serve food and record lunch numbers, allowing students to choose from the regular menu despite the outage.

Region 1 honored Navy Elementary literacy leader Rebecca Schnabel. Her work implementing Benchmark Advance, coaching teachers and organizing the “Navy’s Big Top Book Bash” was credited with supporting schoolwide literacy gains. One student moved from below expectations in phonological awareness to a maximum winter i-Ready score after an eight-week intervention Schnabel helped coordinate.

Region 2 recognized Timber Lane Elementary’s ESOL team: Clara Villalobos Cortes, Catherine Muha, Scott Taggart, Tricia Tyskowski and Elizabeth Smith. Their implementation of Benchmark Express became a model for regional professional development, with recordings of their lessons shared more broadly.

Region 3 honored Edison High School employees Charles Armstrong and Greg Dombrowski. Armstrong performed the Heimlich maneuver on a student while Dombrowski quickly alerted the school nurse.

Region 5 recognized Jesus Henriquez Otero of Stone Middle School for his daily encouragement of students and staff and his care for the building. After strong winds knocked over a small tree in the parking lot, he brought the proper equipment and removed it while keeping up with his other duties.

Region 6 honored Christina Cashman, Roseann Contreras and Stephanie Dawley for organizing Lewis High School’s new “WIDA-palooza” assessment model. They prepared multilingual learners and staff, built a schedule designed to limit missed instruction and helped the school test 97% of identified students.
