For 17 years, Tashima Bradshaw has helped Fairfax County students travel safely to and from school, stepping in wherever she is needed and responding calmly when a routine ride becomes challenging.

Bradshaw joined Fairfax County Public Schools in 2008, initially driving students receiving special education services. She was promoted to a floater position in 2011 and now works with Area 3 Transportation, moving between small and large buses to cover different routes.

That flexibility often includes filling open runs or responding to last-minute bus breakdowns. Each switch requires Bradshaw to complete a full pre-trip inspection before getting behind the wheel.

For Bradshaw, the relationships she builds with students make the demanding work worthwhile.

“The connections and bonds you form with the children are so special,” she said. “I couldn’t imagine a different job.”

Her commitment was tested in September 2024, when a student had difficulty leaving a bus. Bradshaw intervened to protect the student and others, putting herself in harm’s way during the high-stress situation.

The response earned her recognition as an FCPS CARES of the Month recipient in October 2024. An assistant principal praised Bradshaw’s quick thinking, courage and professionalism, saying her actions reflected the community’s highest standards.

Transportation service also runs in Bradshaw’s family. Her mother, Antoinette, worked as a bus driver floater before retiring. Her sister, Kaneisha, served as a bus attendant before becoming an instructional assistant at a school.

Bradshaw’s story was highlighted for National School Bus Driver Appreciation Day on April 22, 2025. FCPS employs more than 1,800 drivers and attendants who transport an estimated 131,000 students each school day.
