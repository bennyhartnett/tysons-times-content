More than 700 Fairfax County Public Schools sixth graders are taking Algebra 1 this year, a dramatic increase from the roughly 30 elementary students who typically enrolled in the high school-level course in previous years.

The students are learning material two years earlier than most of their peers through an FCPS pilot program aimed at expanding access for highly qualified sixth graders. More than one-third of the division’s 142 elementary schools are participating, with enrollment ranging from one student to more than 50 at individual schools.

Eligible students took the sixth-grade Standards of Learning math exam while in fifth grade, earned a “Pass Advanced” result and reached a required threshold on other math assessments.

Students attend Algebra 1 classes at their elementary schools when enrollment supports a full class. Those at schools with fewer participants can join virtually, eliminating transportation barriers that previously prevented some children from accessing advanced instruction.

Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid said FCPS plans to extend the pilot to sixth graders at every school over the next two years. The expansion also supports the division’s 2023-30 strategic plan goal of helping students meet or exceed high academic standards and reach their highest potential.

At Pine Spring Elementary School, one student, Juliet, participates online from an advanced math classroom. Pine Spring is a Title I school receiving additional federal funding based on the proportion of students eligible for free or reduced-price meals.

Principal Nikki Yacubovich said advanced sixth graders previously might have needed to travel to a middle school offering Algebra 1, requiring a parent or another adult to provide transportation. That was not practical for many families with two working parents.

Juliet said she particularly enjoys studying the Pythagorean theorem because it is both difficult and interesting. She believes the accelerated course could eventually help her pursue a strong career or scholarship.

Yacubovich said Juliet’s participation can strengthen her confidence while reminding the wider school community of the talent within the building. She added that Title I schools work not only to close achievement gaps but also to increase academic rigor.

At Mosaic Elementary School in Fairfax, about 22 students are taking Algebra 1 with math resource teacher Amanda Blevins, who is licensed in elementary and middle school mathematics.

Because the students skipped seventh-grade math, Blevins sometimes pauses to introduce unfamiliar concepts. During one class, she reviewed box-and-whisker plots, which display a dataset’s minimum, maximum, median and quartiles, before students enthusiastically tried the work independently.

Sixth grader Mark initially felt excited about enrolling but became nervous when he realized the high school-level course would become part of his academic record. He worried about the difficulty, his grades and how much studying would be required. So far, he said, the class has been challenging but manageable with Blevins’ support.

Classmate Katherine said students are learning substantial new material and sometimes moving beyond their comfort zones, but they are also learning how to overcome mathematical challenges.

Blevins said early exposure to Algebra 1 develops abstract reasoning and problem-solving abilities, builds confidence and creates opportunities for more advanced classes in middle and high school. With many participants already interested in science, technology, engineering and mathematics careers, completing Algebra 1 Honors in sixth grade puts them on an accelerated path toward those goals.

Students including Mark, Katherine, Tarini and Roshan are already considering careers ranging from medicine, law and university teaching to politics, professional sports and aerospace engineering. Roshan described the pilot as a major opportunity to strengthen his math skills and prepare for future college and career applications.
