A student recently died from an overdose outside of school in the West Springfield community, prompting Fairfax County Public Schools to alert families on Sept. 3, 2025, and urge greater vigilance around substance misuse.

The student’s family supported sharing the news in hopes of preventing another family from experiencing a similar loss. FCPS Superintendent Dr. Michelle C. Reid warned that parents and caregivers can be caught unaware and may not recognize the risk until it affects their own household.

Substance misuse remains a concern in Fairfax County, across Virginia and nationwide. FCPS urged the community to approach substance use disorders and overdoses with compassion and understanding while working to reduce stigma and strengthen prevention.

Parents and caregivers were encouraged to have honest, ongoing conversations with children about drugs and health. Those discussions can acknowledge teenagers’ curiosity, explain how vaping, prescription drugs and marijuana may affect the developing brain and decision-making, and prepare young people to respond to peer pressure.

Families can also practice ways for teenagers to decline drugs or help a friend who is using them. Young people should understand that addiction is treatable, recovery is possible and support is available.

FCPS teaches students how substance use can affect their health, academic performance and relationships. Families concerned about a child can contact their school for help accessing services and additional resources.

The school system also maintains an anonymous safety tip line through its Office of Safety and Security, available online, by text and by phone for information about school safety concerns.

FCPS said it will continue working with families, health officials and law enforcement to protect students. The school community was also asked to keep the student’s family and the wider West Springfield community in mind as they grieve.
