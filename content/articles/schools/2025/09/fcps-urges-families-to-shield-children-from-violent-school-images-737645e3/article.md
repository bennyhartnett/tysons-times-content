Fairfax County Public Schools Superintendent Michelle Reid urged families to monitor children’s exposure to graphic footage following shootings at Evergreen High School in Colorado and Utah Valley University.

In a Sept. 11 message, Reid said images from the previous day’s attacks were circulating repeatedly on television and social media. She noted that the violence came as the country marked the anniversary of the Sept. 11 terrorist attacks, compounding feelings of sorrow and uncertainty.

Reid encouraged parents and caregivers to ask children what they have seen and how they are feeling. She warned that repeated exposure to violent images can harm mental health, particularly among young people.

Families seeking help discussing school violence can consult guidance from the National Association of School Psychologists. Reid also advised students and parents who need additional support to contact their school counselor.

Her message further acknowledged threats made Sept. 11 against several Historically Black Colleges and Universities. Reid said everyone has a right to feel safe in places of learning and called for care and compassion in responding to violence and threats.

FCPS continues to prioritize safety through a dedicated team serving students, employees and school communities, Reid said. The division has also recently announced new and expanded safety and security measures intended to strengthen protection across its schools.
