Students, educators, community members and former pupils gathered at Louise Archer Elementary School on Sept. 6 to dedicate a historical marker honoring the longtime teacher and principal.

The marker, the final installation in Fairfax County’s Black/African American Historical Marker Project, recognizes Archer’s decades of service and enduring influence on local education.

Archer spent 26 years with Fairfax County Public Schools and served as principal of the Vienna Colored School. Beyond classroom instruction, she taught life skills, paid for extracurricular activities herself and personally transported children to and from school. When the school had no cafeteria, she provided students with hot meals.

Because Fairfax County did not offer secondary education to African American students until 1954, Archer also helped pupils register for high school in Washington, D.C., so they could continue their studies.

Following Archer’s death in 1948, the Vienna Colored School was renamed Louise Archer Elementary School in her honor. Two of her former students were interviewed earlier in 2025 about their experiences.

Established in 2021, the historical marker project highlights people, places and events that shaped the Black experience in Fairfax County. It is a partnership involving the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors, Fairfax County Public Schools, the History Commission, the Department of Planning and Development, and Neighborhood and Community Services.
