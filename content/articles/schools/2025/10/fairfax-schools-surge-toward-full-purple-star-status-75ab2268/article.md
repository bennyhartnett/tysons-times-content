Fairfax County Public Schools added 49 first-time Purple Star Schools in 2025, bringing the division’s total to 145—nearly three-quarters of all FCPS schools.

Another 40 schools renewed the designation, which recognizes schools that support military-connected students and their families. The honors were announced Oct. 22 by the Virginia Department of Education and the Virginia Council on the Interstate Compact on Educational Opportunity for Military Children.

FCPS serves more than 12,700 military-connected students. Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid said the recognition reflects the school division’s strong partnership with military families.

The expansion moves FCPS closer to its goal of earning Purple Star Division status during the 2026-27 school year. Reaching that target would require every FCPS school to receive the designation.

Reid has said the program helps schools create an inclusive, welcoming environment regardless of how many military-connected students they serve.

## What schools must provide

Virginia has designated Purple Star Schools for eight years. Each designation remains valid for three years before a school must reapply.

Qualifying schools must appoint a staff contact for military-connected students and families. They also must provide relevant resources and programming, participate in professional learning about issues affecting military families, and maintain an active, student-led transition team.

Those peer teams, which can include Student Ambassador programs, welcome newly arriving military-connected students and help them adjust to their school communities.

The initiative also supports the positive-school-climate priorities in Goal 2 of the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan: Safe, Supported, Included, and Empowered.
