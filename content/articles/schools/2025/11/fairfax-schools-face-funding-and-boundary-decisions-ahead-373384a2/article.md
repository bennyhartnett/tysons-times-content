Fairfax County Public Schools offered an early look at its fiscal 2027 challenges while moving closer to recommendations in its comprehensive boundary review during the week ending Nov. 17, 2025.

The developments came during a busy week that also included the inaugural girls flag football championship, updates on inclusive instruction, recognition of a long-running financial literacy partnership and several student athletic and theater events.

## Budget forecast highlights state funding gap

The School Board reviewed the fiscal 2027 forecast during its Tuesday work session. The forecast will help shape Superintendent Michelle Reid’s proposed budget, scheduled for presentation in late January.

Officials outlined budget priorities and warned that instructional and support programs could be at risk without full funding. They also renewed concerns about Virginia’s education funding formulas, particularly the Standards of Quality formula used to estimate necessary staffing and the state’s contribution toward those positions.

FCPS officials said the formula understates actual staffing needs and fails to reflect the full cost of educating students who require additional support, including special education students and multilingual learners.

The Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission found that Virginia school divisions collectively spent $6.6 billion more than the amount indicated by the Standards of Quality formula.

School funding was also discussed Friday during a Public School Leadership Network meeting at George Mason University. Reid joined Delegate Vivian Watts, other elected officials and regional education leaders for a conversation about funding challenges.

## Boundary review enters final stages

The Superintendent’s Boundary Review Advisory Committee met Wednesday as the division’s comprehensive boundary review approached its recommendation phase.

Erik Gordon, FCPS chief of Facilities and Capital Programs, summarized the work completed so far and the remaining schedule. Committee members then met in regional and divisionwide groups to examine more than 9,000 comments submitted through the Boundary Explorer Tool.

The committee was expected to deliver its recommendations to Reid later in November. After considering them, Reid will present final recommendations and a proposed implementation plan to the School Board. The board is scheduled to review the proposal, conduct a public hearing and approve any boundary changes in January.

The School Board separately voted to make the planned western high school a comprehensive school with traditional attendance boundaries and a special-programming pathway option. It also approved an enrollment plan allowing ninth- and 10th-grade students to opt in for the 2026-27 school year.

Reid met Saturday with Herndon community members at Herndon Middle School and West Springfield community members at Irving Middle School to hear their concerns about the boundary process.

## Oakton captures inaugural flag football title

Oakton High School became the first FCPS girls flag football champion after the sport’s debut season in the division.

A total of 775 students participated in the new program, which was sponsored by the Washington Commanders. The championship arrived as girls flag football continues to expand, with college scholarships available and the sport set for Olympic competition in 2028.

Eight FCPS football teams also won playoff games during the week. Victories by Centreville, Fairfax, Lake Braddock, Madison, South County, Westfield and West Springfield advanced those schools in postseason competition. Families and spectators also encountered new security measures at the playoff games.

On Saturday, Liberty Middle School hosted the FCPS middle school cross country championships, bringing together student-athletes, staff members, families and other supporters for the culmination of the fall season.

## Inclusive teaching rollout expands

During Thursday’s School Board meeting, Reid presented an update on Universal Design for Learning and inclusive practices. The work is connected to Goal 2 of the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan, which focuses on ensuring students are safe, supported, included and empowered.

Universal Design for Learning encourages educators to identify and reduce barriers while designing instruction that can meaningfully engage students with disabilities, multilingual learners, advanced students and others with varied learning needs.

FCPS is also clarifying its approach to the least restrictive environment. Students should participate in general education classes as much as possible unless an Individualized Education Program team determines that specialized instruction offers a better route to success.

The phased rollout began with 12 schools during the 2025-26 academic year. Another 81 schools are scheduled to join in 2026-27, with the remaining schools following in 2027-28. The division said early responses from the first group have been encouraging.

Reid also thanked voters for approving the recent school bond referendum. Just over seven in 10 Fairfax County voters supported the measure, the strongest approval rate of the past three referendum cycles. She recognized Len Forkas and the Bond Citizens Committee for their work on the campaign.

## Finance Park marks 15 years with FCPS

Reid spoke Thursday to Junior Achievement of Greater Washington, whose partnership with FCPS has brought the Finance Park financial literacy program to eighth graders for 15 years.

The curriculum is taught through mathematics and civics classes and culminates in a visit to the Finance Park facility next to Frost Middle School. Students work through practical decisions involving housing, transportation, food, utilities, health care, banking, investing and philanthropy.

Over the partnership’s first 15 years, Finance Park hosted 210,000 FCPS students and delivered more than 3.92 million hours of experiential learning. More than 30,000 business and community volunteers supported FCPS field trips during that period.

## Student productions take the stage

Annandale High School presented *Annie* on Friday evening. Director Kylie Murray said the company used the production to examine wealth, generosity, connection, resilience and hope, particularly as families face uncertainty.

Herndon High School staged *Arsenic and Old Lace* on Saturday as part of its season of American classics. Theatre Arts Director Scott D. Pafumi described the World War II-era Brooklyn farce as the story of two elderly sisters whose deadly idea of community service creates chaos involving eccentric characters, inept police officers and a pair of young lovers.
