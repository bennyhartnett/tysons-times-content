Fairfax County Public Schools highlighted student achievements, upcoming health clinics and several approaching deadlines in its Nov. 12, 2025, community update. The announcements also covered artificial intelligence, school boundaries, attendance and calendar changes.

## Students earn academic and musical honors

During the 2024-25 school year, 187 FCPS students received the Advanced Placement Capstone Diploma, while 22 earned the AP Seminar and Research Certificate.

The AP Capstone program allows students to explore subjects across multiple disciplines and pursue personal interests while demonstrating research, critical-thinking and presentation skills. Access to advanced coursework is included in Goal 4 of the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan, which focuses on equitable access and opportunity.

Another 39 students from 18 FCPS high schools and two elementary schools were selected for statewide music groups. They earned places in the 2025 All-Virginia Orff Ensemble, All-Virginia Jazz Band and Jazz Ensemble, and Virginia Music Education Association Senior Honors Choir.

Students competed through a rigorous audition process and will rehearse with nationally recognized conductors alongside musicians from across Virginia.

## FCPS weighs the future of artificial intelligence

Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid reflected on discussions from the “Meeting the Moment: FCPS Forward — AI and the Future of Learning” event.

Reid said schools have reached a pivotal point at which people must actively shape artificial intelligence instead of allowing the technology to dictate the future. She cited rapid changes in employment, including a decline in entry-level positions—particularly in white-collar fields—and projections that nearly 40% of workplace skills will change by 2030.

## Free student immunization clinics scheduled

FCPS and Inspire Health Alliance will offer required school immunizations and optional flu shots to FCPS students at no charge. Participation is voluntary, and parents or guardians may make appointments.

Clinics were scheduled for:

- Saturday, Nov. 15, from 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. at Frost Middle School.
- Tuesday, Nov. 18, from 3 to 6:30 p.m. at the Willow Oaks Administrative Center.

Additional clinic dates were expected during the school year.

## Families asked to complete Impact Aid survey

FCPS asked every family to submit the Federal Impact Aid Survey as soon as possible. The online form is available through the Online Packets section of SIS ParentVUE.

Schools planned to give paper forms to families that had not completed the survey online. Families without an active ParentVUE account were encouraged to register, both to submit the survey and to access other information about their children’s education.

## Bucknell technology lab opens to young learners

A hands-on engineering and technology space recently opened at Bucknell Elementary School for students from across Fairfax County.

Created through a partnership with Virginia Tech, the Bucknell STEAM Innovation Lab is intended to serve thousands of FCPS students. Children as young as kindergarten can investigate real-world technology and engineering concepts, learn how things work and consider how they might design solutions of their own.

## Science fair seeks more than 250 judges

The Fairfax County Regional Science and Engineering Fair was scheduled for Saturday, March 14, at Robinson Secondary School.

Organizers need more than 250 volunteer judges to assess projects in fields ranging from plant science to engineering technology. Applicants ideally should have an advanced degree or substantial academic or professional experience in their chosen category, although all interested people were encouraged to apply.

Parents, caregivers, teachers and family friends may not judge a category in which their child, student or family friend’s child is competing.

## Thomas Jefferson application deadline approaches

Eighth-grade students interested in science, technology, engineering and mathematics were encouraged to apply to Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology.

Applications had to be started by 4 p.m. Nov. 12 and submitted by 4 p.m. Friday, Nov. 14.

## Hayfield production takes the stage

Hayfield Secondary School presented an energetic and emotional production of “American Idiot,” reviewed by South Lakes High School student Lana Vittor.

The review was part of the Washington, D.C., chapter of the Critics and Awards Program for High School Theatre, known as the Cappies. The program trains high school theater and journalism students as critics who attend productions at other schools and write reviews for publication.

## FCPS presses families to build attendance habits

Families were reminded that regular attendance supports on-time graduation and helps students develop time-management skills.

FCPS urged parents and caregivers to arrange travel during weekends or school holidays and, when possible, schedule appointments outside school hours. Missing two days each month—or 10% of the school year—meets the definition of chronic absenteeism.

When illness or an appointment requires a student to miss class, the family must report the absence to the school.

## Boundary feedback and western high school plans advance

The School Board discussed the comprehensive boundary review and plans for a new western high school during a Nov. 12 work session.

Public comments on draft boundary map Scenario 4 could be entered in the Boundary Explorer Tool through Friday, Nov. 14. The feedback was set to go to the Superintendent’s Boundary Review Advisory Committee as it prepared a recommendation for Reid. Residents could also contact their representatives on the advisory committee.

The board’s next regular business meeting was scheduled for 7 p.m. Thursday, Nov. 13. Its agenda included a public hearing on the name of the new western high school, programming for the school and the awarding of contracts.

## Early release and Thanksgiving closures ahead

Elementary schools in 13 pyramids were scheduled to dismiss students three hours early Wednesday, Nov. 19. The affected pyramids were Centreville, Chantilly, Fairfax, Hayfield, Justice, Lewis, Mount Vernon, Oakton, Robinson, South County, Westfield and West Potomac.

Schools and offices were set to close from Wednesday, Nov. 26, through Friday, Nov. 28, for Thanksgiving. Classes were scheduled to resume Monday, Dec. 1, and the weekly FCPS update would not be distributed Nov. 26.

## Virginia career and education data released

The University of Virginia’s Weldon Cooper Center for Public Service and the Virginia Department of Education compiled information on leading professions and high-earning degree programs for the 2024-25 school year.

The findings use occupational employment and educational-attainment figures from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, along with longitudinal wage data from the State Council of Higher Education for Virginia. Additional state resources offer information about colleges, employment projections and preparing, applying and paying for higher education.

FCPS also reiterated its commitment to providing educational and employment opportunities without discrimination. Its career pathways include business and information technology, family and consumer science, health and medical studies, JROTC, marketing, technology and engineering education, and trade and industry programs.
