Hayfield Secondary School brought the music of Green Day to the stage with a forceful production of *American Idiot*, pairing rock-concert intensity with an emotional examination of addiction, war, friendship and love.

The jukebox musical features songs by Green Day, with lyrics by lead singer Billie Joe Armstrong and a book by Armstrong and Michael Mayer. After opening on Broadway in 2010, it earned Tony Awards for scenic and lighting design, a Best Musical nomination and a Grammy for Best Musical Show Album.

Set in post-9/11 America, the story follows three friends — Johnny, Will and Tunny — as they leave suburbia and search for purpose. Their separate paths expose them to trauma, injury, self-conflict and the pull of home.

Aaron Guerrero anchored Hayfield’s production as Johnny, capturing the character’s punk defiance with grunge-inflected vocals, restless movement and expressive acting. His performance in “Boulevard of Broken Dreams” revealed Johnny’s more vulnerable side and established a strong emotional connection to the character.

Cecilia Dorrity delivered a commanding turn as St. Jimmy, filling the stage with sharp expressions, powerful vocals and relentless energy. Her performance made St. Jimmy’s influence over Johnny immediately clear.

Isaac Dorrity and Sunny Potvin, playing Will and Tunny, worked effectively alongside Guerrero while giving their characters distinct identities. Their performances handled the musical’s mature depictions of addiction, trauma and injury with emotional weight.

As Whatsername, Josie Sinniger traced her character’s development from Johnny’s idealized fantasy into a person with anger, sadness and needs of her own. Her resonant vocals and passionate performance stood out in “Letterbomb.”

The full company approached the show’s difficult subject matter directly and with maturity, sustaining both its urgency and emotional depth.

The technical team gave the production an equally bold identity. Charlie Fink, Allison Rigsbee and Dominic Sotallaro helped construct an elaborate, functional set that evoked a 2000s-era mall through period storefronts, wall-mounted records and a working stage escalator.

Lighting by Marley Peterson and Wren Randolph used colors symbolically to distinguish the characters and their different lives. Additional fixtures strengthened the atmosphere of a live rock concert.

With energetic performances, committed storytelling and ambitious technical work, Hayfield’s *American Idiot* became an exciting and emotionally charged theatrical experience.
