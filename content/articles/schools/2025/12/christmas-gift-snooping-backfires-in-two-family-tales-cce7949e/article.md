Two would-be Christmas surprises vanished before they could reach their intended recipients—one exposed by a sharp-eyed preschooler and the other undone by a 10-year-old’s overly clever plan.

A first-time grandmother spent the year buying presents for her only grandchild, Charlie, and storing them in her closet. She assumed the gifts were safe because the 4-year-old often visited but never entered that part of the house.

That changed during a recent visit when the home suddenly became suspiciously quiet. She went looking for Charlie and found him inside the closet, where the presents were sitting in plain view.

Pointing to one, he asked whether it belonged to him. His grandmother replied that it was for a special occasion. Charlie considered the answer and offered some direct advice: “You need to find a better hiding place.”

Another Christmas surprise unraveled years earlier after a 10-year-old named Alyce struggled to choose between roller skates and ice skates. Her mother warned that she had to settle on one pair because she would not receive both.

Alyce preferred roller skates, reasoning that Arlington’s climate offered more opportunities for skating on pavement than on ice. Still, she did not give her mother a final answer.

When her mother left her home alone to go shopping, Alyce slipped into the attic to investigate. There she discovered the roller skates she wanted.

Certain she had figured out the plan, Alyce decided to tell her mother that she actually wanted ice skates. She expected to receive the roller skates already hidden in the attic along with a new pair of ice skates.

Instead, her mother returned the roller skates and replaced them with ice skates. On Christmas morning, Alyce opened her present and found only the pair she wanted less—a lasting lesson in how a clever scheme can become its own undoing.
