The Fairfax County School Board approved course changes, adopted its 2026 legislative priorities and reorganized part of its facilities-planning structure during a regular meeting that stretched past midnight on Dec. 4, 2025.

The board amended the structure of its Comprehensive Planning and Development Committee by adding three community members. It also voted to dissolve the Facilities Planning Advisory Council effective April 1, 2026.

Members approved changes to middle and high school course offerings for the 2026-27 academic year and adopted the 2026 State and Federal Legislative Program.

The meeting began at 7:38 p.m. with the Pledge of Allegiance, a moment of silence and a performance of the national anthem by South Lakes High School student Sidney Walkup. The board also issued a proclamation recognizing Inclusive Schools Week.

Twelve people addressed the board during the public-comment period. Student representative Faith Mekonen was absent because of an academic obligation and submitted her report in writing.

## Superintendent review and major presentations

Superintendent Michelle C. Reid presented a monitoring report on Executive Limitation 4, which establishes human-resources guardrails for the superintendent’s operation of the school division.

The board found Reid in compliance and approved the 2025-26 monitoring report. Through its consent agenda, it also kept the human-resources policy unchanged in the Strategic Governance Manual.

Reid and Chief Operating Officer Andy Mueck briefed the board on the phasing of boundary reviews and related transportation issues. Reid and Chief Financial Officer Leigh Burden presented the fiscal 2026 midyear budget review.

Additional presentations covered Standards of Learning cut scores and accountability proposals, as well as an update on FCPS warehouse operations under the division’s strategic plan.

## Consent agenda and future votes

The consent agenda included minutes from the Nov. 12 work session and Nov. 13 regular meeting, additional advisory committee appointments and the issuance of school bonds.

Members also approved land-rights easements allowing Dominion Energy work at Mount Eagle Elementary School for the Richmond Highway Bus Rapid Transit project and awarded several contracts.

The board introduced several items that were not put to a vote that night. They included the fiscal 2026 midyear budget review, the Strategic Plan Goal 5 report on innovation and a second public-comment period for naming the new western high school.

Other future action items involved asphalt milling and paving at Woodson High School, an electronic-participation resolution, employee performance evaluations for the School Board office and individual board members, boundary-review phasing and transportation, and the Centreville High School lease agreement.

Kyle McElveen delivered an Audit Committee report, and Reid presented her superintendent’s report. Board members Sandy Anderson, Rachna Sizemore Heizer, Ilryong Moon, Seema Dixit, Karl Frisch, Robyn Lady, Melanie Meren St. John-Cunning and Ryan McElveen Dunne offered closing remarks.

The meeting adjourned at 12:10 a.m. on Dec. 5.

Upcoming meetings were scheduled for Dec. 9 for the Governance Committee, Dec. 10 for the Administration Committee, Dec. 11 for the Comprehensive Planning and Development Committee and Dec. 15 for the Public Engagement Committee.

A joint annual meeting with the City of Fairfax School Board was set for 6 p.m. Dec. 16 at Fairfax City Hall. The next regular School Board meeting was scheduled for 7 p.m. Dec. 18.
