Fairfax County Public Schools entered December with winter weather, concerts, championship celebrations and major discussions about budgets and academic standards.

After snow created challenges during the first full week of the month, facilities, grounds and transportation employees worked early shifts to keep schools operating safely. Superintendent Michelle Reid and Chief of Schools Dr. Ponce visited Shrevewood, Graham Road and Woodburn elementary schools Friday morning, greeting students, employees and families in the snow. Shrevewood also celebrated the 67th day of the school year, while student safety patrol members helped younger children at Woodburn.

Chief of Facilities Erik Gordon said FCPS deployed 46 grounds employees, supported by workers from Equipment Repair, Pest Control, the Stock Room and Grounds Management. Sixteen Plant Operations employees also assisted with custodial coordination. The work was completed without contractors, though the final amount of treatment material used was still being calculated.

The weather management team operated from the updated Woodson Complex, where its command center opened at 4 a.m. Equipment repair technician Jonathan Marchante was among those treating paved areas, including parking lots at Fairfax High School.

## Budget and academic changes ahead

FCPS and the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors met Tuesday through their Joint Budget Committee to review forecasts and prepare for the fiscal 2027 budget cycle. Reid is scheduled to present the school division’s proposed budget on Jan. 22, 2026. The county executive’s advertised budget is expected Feb. 17.

FCPS highlighted several measures of academic performance during the budget discussion: seven of Virginia’s top 10 high schools, five of its top 10 elementary schools and one of the nation’s top five high schools are in the division. FCPS also reported 261 National Merit semifinalists this year, a 40% increase from the previous year.

Potential changes to Virginia’s Standards of Learning tests were another major focus. During Thursday’s School Board meeting, Reid reviewed the effects of revised SOL cut scores and proposed accountability rules.

The State Board of Education decided in November that the score changes would begin in the 2026-27 school year rather than 2025-26. They would be introduced over four years, raising the score required to pass annually. A temporary “Approaching” category would count as proficient during the transition so schools and students would not face immediate penalties.

Using spring 2025 results as a baseline, FCPS estimated that full implementation could lower the math pass rate from 78% to 52% and the reading rate from 79% to 51%. The state board was expected to continue its deliberations Dec. 10-11, including decisions about eligibility for locally awarded verified credits and expedited SOL retakes.

Proposed revisions to the School Performance and Support Framework include extending support for newly arrived multilingual learners from three semesters to five, changing academic-growth calculations and adding history to the subjects used in the framework. Other ideas would count all tested high school students in mastery measures and expand advanced middle school coursework to include Earth science and world geography by eighth grade.

The School Board also received an update on the strategic plan’s Culture of Equity, Excellence and Accountability pillar. FCPS warehouse operations employ 50 people and serve more than 200 schools and offices. The operation handles approximately 500 deliveries and pickups each week, or about 24,000 annually.

Its fleet includes 13 distribution trucks and nine Pony mail trucks. Distribution vehicles travel more than 200,000 miles per year, while Pony mail vehicles cover 133,200 miles. A presentation showed how the warehouse transports materials—including ladybug larvae—to classrooms throughout the county.

## Airport partnership expands career opportunities

At West Potomac Academy on Wednesday, FCPS launched an expanded Ignite Partnership with the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority.

The collaboration will broaden work-based learning across the academy’s programs, giving students more exposure to high-demand professions, practical experiences and career paths connected to their classroom instruction.

That evening, families gathered at Edison High School for a Quad 3 Family Vision Group meeting. Assistant Superintendent of Instructional Services Dr. Ellen Reilly and Dr. Amy Hunter led a discussion about expanding access to Algebra 1 by sixth grade.

Gordon, Design and Construction Services Director Scott Eschbach and project manager Holli McKinley also discussed upcoming school construction and renovation work.

## FCPS teams capture four state championships

Several teams were recognized after standout fall seasons.

The Lake Braddock boys cross-country team swept the Patriot District, Occoquan Region and Class 6 state championships. Robinson’s girls repeated as Class 6 state champions, winning by 28 points and placing three runners among the top 15.

Langley won the 2025 Virginia High School League Class 6 field hockey championship and finished 22-1. The Saxons defeated Western Branch 6-0, Independence 2-0 and Madison 1-0 during their postseason run.

West Springfield captured the Class 6 volleyball title in its first state-final appearance. After losing the opening two sets against Colgan, the Spartans rallied to win the match in five sets and complete a 30-2 season.

Girls wrestling also drew a growing field Saturday at Herndon High School. The second annual Queen of the Ring tournament attracted 156 athletes, up from 140 the previous year.

Competitors represented schools across Northern Virginia, other parts of Virginia and West Virginia, including Herndon, Stafford, Prince George, Henrico, Meridian, Freedom-South Riding, Morgantown, Annandale, Langley, Robinson, Lightridge, McLean, Woodbridge, James River-Buchanan, Westfield and Chantilly.

## New leadership and employee training

Dr. Dustin Wright took the oath of office Monday as the new superintendent of City of Fairfax Schools. A former FCPS employee, Wright will lead the city’s four-school district.

The City of Fairfax operates those schools through a School Services Agreement with FCPS. The county division provides curriculum, staffing and transportation, while the city owns and oversees the buildings and manages capital improvements, renovations and the agreement itself.

Within FCPS, 24 Human Resources employees are participating in biweekly, in-person training toward Society for Human Resource Management certification. Chief of Human Resources William Solomon said the industry credential would strengthen the team’s use of current practices in areas ranging from teacher recruitment to personnel management.

## Music, theater and community events fill the week

Reid attended Carson Middle School’s choir concert Tuesday evening, where students performed for families and community members.

On Friday, FCPS held a Community Conversation at Churchill Road Elementary School, giving parents and residents an opportunity to share concerns and ideas despite the cold weather.

Later that evening, Falls Church High School staged *Between the Lines* before a large audience in its new theater. First-year theater director Madeline Willard said the production encourages teenagers to accept their differences and tell their own stories, even when fitting in might seem easier.

Saturday’s Mount Vernon High School PTSA Holiday Market raised money that will help support senior-class activities planned for the spring.

The week concluded Sunday at Christ Church in Alexandria, where Reid served as a reader during the Service of Nine Lessons and Carols. FCPS students and families joined the Advent program at the historic church, which was built more than 235 years ago and was attended by George Washington.
