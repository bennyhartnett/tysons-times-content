Fairfax County Public Schools Superintendent Michelle Reid unveiled proposed boundary changes affecting about 2,210 students while highlighting a week that also included artificial intelligence training, major science honors and student-led environmental work.

Reid presented the recommendations to the School Board on Thursday. The proposal would affect an estimated 1,174 elementary students, 447 middle school students and 589 high school students across 52 schools.

The recommendations followed a 16-month review involving data analysis and community engagement under School Board Policy 8130. The board is scheduled to vote on the boundary changes and their implementation Thursday, Jan. 22.

Reid spent part of Saturday hearing residents’ support and concerns about the recommendations after attending the General Assembly pre-session public hearing at the Fairfax Government Center.

## Nine FCPS Students Earn National Science Honors

Eight students from Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology and one from Chantilly High School were named scholars in the 2026 Regeneron Science Talent Search.

They were among 300 scholars chosen from 2,600 entrants in the national pre-college science competition. Each scholar and their school will receive $2,000.

Forty finalists were set to be announced Jan. 21. Those students will travel to Washington, D.C., in March for final judging, a public exhibition and meetings with scientists. The competition’s top award is $250,000.

## Robinson Rifle Team Wins Arlington Match

On Monday, Reid visited the Robinson Secondary School rifle team before its match against Wakefield High School.

Robinson is one of seven FCPS teams competing in the 12-team Potomac High School Rifle League, where male and female athletes compete alongside one another. The team won the league’s 2024-25 regional match.

Robinson defeated Wakefield 1582.2 to 1447.1 at the Woman’s Club of Arlington.

## FCPS Expands AI Training for Educators

Technology support specialists and school-based technology specialists attended a half-day ChatGPT training Tuesday. Sam Kanning-Caplan, OpenAI’s education lead, discussed the platform’s capabilities and potential classroom uses.

A leadership panel featuring FCPS academic, technology, equity and instructional-services officials also answered questions.

Reid said the school system plans to continue investing in training that helps employees develop practical skills and understand the ethical implications of artificial intelligence. FCPS principles call for keeping teachers in control, protecting privacy and academic integrity, and evaluating AI’s effects while adopting safeguards.

Describing the rapid rise of AI as education’s “Sputnik moment,” Reid said students are already using the technology and adults must be prepared to guide them. The division plans to evaluate tools through its procurement and privacy processes, hold community discussions and launch an FCPS AI Think Tank focused on AI-supported education.

## Community Visits Span Literacy and Juvenile Services

Reid met Tuesday with the Rotary Club of McLean at Redeemer Lutheran Church to discuss progress on the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan, budget planning, future careers and workforce development.

The club has supported Timber Lane Elementary School’s Love of Reading Program for 17 years. It provides an annual grant for books and organizes volunteers to read to students from prekindergarten through second grade.

Later that day, Reid toured the Fairfax County Juvenile Detention Center and met employees who provide education and other programming for young people involved in the juvenile justice system.

## Students Push for Systemwide Environmental Action

The Student Youth Environmental Council held its second meeting Wednesday and moved to a monthly schedule. Created by students, the council brings together young people from across FCPS who want the division to become a leader in sustainability.

Its goals include establishing student-led governance, incorporating Mission Green Global resources into existing FCPS programs and giving students a voice in division and statewide environmental policy.

Early priorities include stronger recycling programs, food-waste-reducing “share tables,” a divisionwide science fair centered on environmental problems and innovations, and resources connecting school sustainability efforts with families’ habits at home.

The council’s next meeting was scheduled for 5 p.m. Jan. 27 at the Gatehouse Administration Center Café, 8115 Gatehouse Road in Falls Church.

Reid also participated Thursday on the five-member AASA National Blue Ribbon selection panel responsible for choosing the new National Superintendent of the Year.
