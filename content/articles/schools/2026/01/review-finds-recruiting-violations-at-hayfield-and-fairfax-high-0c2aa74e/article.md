An independent review has confirmed that football programs at Hayfield Secondary School and Fairfax High School violated Virginia High School League eligibility rules in 2024, according to Fairfax County Public Schools Superintendent Michelle Reid.

The investigation also uncovered gaps in FCPS procedures and found that actions by some employees delayed or interfered with attempts to address the violations, Reid announced Wednesday.

Reid did not identify the employees or disclose whether they had been disciplined, citing the confidentiality of personnel matters. She said appropriate personnel action would be taken.

“We are firmly committed to fairness and integrity in our athletic programs and ensuring continual compliance with all VHSL rules,” Reid said in a message to families at Hayfield and Fairfax High.

## Hayfield controversy prompted outside review

FCPS first investigated Hayfield in summer 2024 after parents raised concerns about new head football coach Darryl Overton and his staff. They were accused of recruiting at least a dozen players from Freedom High School in Woodbridge, where Overton had led the football team to two consecutive state championships.

The school system initially cleared Overton and his staff of recruiting allegations, as well as claims involving bullying, misuse of funds and the use of school property for personal benefit.

The VHSL reached a different conclusion. It banned Hayfield from the football playoffs for two years after finding that the school violated a rule barring schools and employees from encouraging students to transfer for league-governed activities.

A Fairfax County Circuit Court judge temporarily halted the ban while considering a lawsuit filed by Hayfield parents, allowing the team to enter the 2024 postseason.

Reid and then-Hayfield principal Darin Thompson later withdrew the team after several opposing coaches threatened to forfeit their games. Text messages had also emerged suggesting Hayfield Director of Student Activities Monty Fritts knew about Overton’s recruiting methods before the coach was hired.

In December 2024, the Fairfax County School Board authorized FCPS to retain Chicago law firm Baker McKenzie for an independent examination of student-athlete transfers and eligibility practices. Reid also proposed an internal policy review and centralized oversight of school athletics.

Those reviews uncovered additional recruiting and eligibility concerns involving an Edison High School basketball coach and the Fairfax High football program. The Fairfax program was accused of offering payments to players who transferred to join the team during the 2024-2025 school year.

FCPS placed Fairfax High on probation in October, barring the Lions from the fall 2025 postseason after determining that coaches had violated VHSL recruiting policies.

## More oversight and training planned

FCPS worked with the VHSL last year to revise statewide regulations, including a prohibition on transfers made solely for athletic or activity purposes.

The school system has since created a centralized platform for collecting, reviewing and processing athletic transfers. Its Office of Student Activities and Athletics now oversees all transfers and examines eligibility questions.

Mandatory training on VHSL and FCPS rules has also been introduced for student activities directors, student services teams and principals.

Reid said FCPS will next establish consistent standards for scrutinizing new student registrations and transfers within the school system. It also plans to strengthen investigative resources, protocols, tools and training while extending mandatory instruction to every coaching level, including unpaid volunteers.

“We believe the remediation already underway, and the additional steps soon to be implemented, will strengthen our processes and help ensure our practices stay on the right path moving forward,” Reid said.
