Fairfax County’s sweeping school boundary plan shrank dramatically in the final two weeks before its approval, sparing 513 students while postponing several overcrowding decisions.

The number of students affected fell from 2,210 on Jan. 8 to 1,697 by the Fairfax County School Board’s Jan. 22 vote. Superintendent Michelle Reid and her staff made late revisions amid fierce community opposition, leaving families sorting through outdated maps to determine whether their children would change schools.

The board approved the amended plan by an 8-3 vote. Although the changes protected several neighborhoods from immediate reassignment, unresolved capacity problems will force officials to revisit some boundaries well before the five-year review cycle intended to provide stability.

A Jan. 21 bulletin from School Board member Robyn Lady of the Dranesville District directed families to an amended presentation showing revisions in red. The updated executive summary also identified areas slated for examination by January 2027 and during the next comprehensive review.

Confusion continued after the vote. As of Jan. 28, the official FCPS Boundary Review Scenario Explorer warned that it would not be updated until the end of January.

Reid said Jan. 26 that families directly affected by the approved changes would receive notifications by mail or email in the coming weeks.

## Areas Returning for Review by January 2027

Officials plan to examine several school boundaries and feeder patterns within one year:

- Neighborhoods assigned to Gunston, Halley, Laurel Hill and Lorton Station elementary schools.
- Bren Mar Park Elementary School’s middle and high school feeder patterns.
- The Greenway Downs, Jefferson Village, City Park Homes and Kingsley Commons neighborhoods.
- Rolling Valley Elementary School’s middle and high school feeders in School Planning Area 8922.
- Glasgow Middle School changes involving the Beech Tree and Belvedere elementary school areas.
- The Keene Mill Elementary School attendance island and surrounding schools, including Cardinal Forest and White Oaks elementary schools.

## Sites Flagged for Later Action

Other communities and schools were identified for closer examination before or during the next five-year boundary review.

Briarwood Trace and Briarwood Farms are expected to be reviewed in approximately two years. Officials want enrollment data showing how the opening of the new Western High School affects Oakton High School before making a decision.

High school students in the Tysons Green community will receive a priority-transfer option to Madison High School beginning in fall 2026, provided they supply their own transportation. A comparable middle school option for Thoreau is not currently available.

The Bull Run Elementary School boundary remains on hold and will be considered as part of the Western High School boundary study.

Officials also plan to examine Lewis High School alongside other under-capacity schools and West Springfield High School with schools operating at 105% capacity or higher.

The full-time Advanced Academic Program Center at Riverside Elementary School could move to Woodley Hills Elementary School before the next comprehensive review, potentially through an earlier soft launch.

Future work may also realign nonresidential areas, including the Buckman Road and Richmond Highway intersection and Greendale Golf Course and its surroundings. Small corrections will remain possible as well; the property at 2817 Douglas St. was handled during the current review.

A separate Advanced Academic Program decision could arrive sooner. If the board proceeds with placing AAP centers in every middle school, Reid is expected to present an implementation plan by March, following direction board members issued last fall.
