Fairfax County Public Schools’ February 2026 CARES recognitions spotlighted employees who kept buildings operating, expanded opportunities for students, supported families and responded to emergencies across the division.

The monthly honorees represented the central office and each of FCPS’ six regions, while dozens of additional submissions praised teachers, transportation employees, custodians, administrators and support staff.

## February’s regional honorees

Dave Martinez of the Merrifield Support Center received the Central Office recognition after responding to a frozen pipe that burst at the Gatehouse Administration Building on Feb. 4. He worked through the night to diagnose the problem and clear the obstruction, allowing the building to reopen fully operational the next morning.

South Lakes High School culinary educators Carlos D’Alvano and Cynthia Stowers were honored in Region 1 for running a program that teaches far more than cooking. Students develop leadership, communication, customer service, planning and problem-solving skills while learning to meet professional standards in a demanding but supportive environment.

Region 2 recognized Veronica Perez of Belvedere Elementary School for expanding access to grade-level instruction for English learners. Perez developed GLAD materials for classrooms beyond those she directly serves and created a supplemental booklet to help two students participate more independently in whole-group Benchmark lessons.

At Bucknell Elementary School, Chanel Favors earned the Region 3 honor for organizing the school’s second annual Career Day. Students from prekindergarten through sixth grade met professionals working in law, medicine, real estate, cosmetology, psychology, dentistry, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence security and entrepreneurship. Teachers praised both the diversity of presenters and the careful logistics behind the event.

Region 4 honored Centreville Elementary School instructional assistant Karen Bernat. In addition to supporting students at school and volunteering with the Centreville High School marching band, Bernat regularly helps neighborhood children reach their buses safely. She brings flashlights on dark mornings, watches icy intersections and asks drivers to wait when students are still approaching.

Von-Michael Corpuz, a special education teacher and Special Olympics coach at Chantilly High School, received the Region 5 recognition. A parent credited Corpuz with encouraging students and their families to participate together in Category B sports.

Lewis High School educator Mallorie Joyner was selected in Region 6 for her work with students who have complex academic, behavioral and social-emotional needs. Joyner was recognized for early communication with families, collaboration with district specialists, individualized support plans, staff mentoring and detailed advocacy for students.

## Transportation workers answer late-night calls

Several transportation employees were honored for urgent and safety-focused responses.

Monique Martin of Stonecroft Transportation impressed Navy Elementary students and families by clearly explaining kindergarten release procedures, organizing each stop and ensuring children approached the bus steps safely.

At about 9 p.m. on Feb. 11, Seung Yang responded after an Arlington field-trip bus had to be towed. Although he had finished work and returned home, Yang immediately retrieved another bus and transported the stranded high school students and their coach.

Carmen Galindo Calderon, an Area 7 bus driver supervisor, coordinated that replacement transportation after being contacted outside normal hours.

Lorton Center driver James McGeehan and attendant Mirta Lizarazu took an additional assignment to transport three Kings Park Elementary students home, providing a warm bus and a calm, welcoming environment.

Rachelle Wigenton, operations manager in the Area 4 Transportation Office, responded to a water-main break at Marshall Road Elementary at about 1 a.m. and relocated buses before they could be damaged.

Shan Ahmad personally cleared an icy public walkway between a daycare and a bus stop after determining that the unresolved hazard threatened students’ route. John Leishear spent several days clearing snow and ice from his assigned bus at the Gatehouse lot and also helped elderly neighbors reach a medical appointment.

Stonecroft employee Veronica Lowe was recognized for answering questions, handling unusual requests and helping schools locate buses. Carmen Galindo Calderon, Seung Yang, Monique Martin and Rachelle Wigenton joined other transportation honorees praised for placing student safety first.
