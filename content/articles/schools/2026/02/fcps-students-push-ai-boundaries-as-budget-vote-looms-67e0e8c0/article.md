Fairfax County Public Schools highlighted student innovation, employee retention and community partnerships during a busy week that also brought renewed attention to the division’s fiscal year 2027 budget.

The School Board was scheduled to vote Thursday, Feb. 26, on the proposed advertised budget. Additional state funding could substantially reduce the current shortfall, though FCPS expected to learn more from Virginia officials around March 14.

The budget follows the spending plan presented by the Fairfax County executive on Feb. 17 and discussed during a School Board Budget Committee work session.

FCPS also reported strong retention figures among school-based employees. Of those asked to complete an Intent to Return Form, 99% responded and 97% said they planned to return for the 2026-27 school year.

## More than 1,100 students enter AI challenge

Students from every FCPS high school participated in the Seize the Moment Student Artificial Intelligence Innovation Challenge, an event organized with the FCPS Department of Information Technology, Amazon and Playlab.

More than 1,100 students created custom applications with Playlab, a controlled AI environment that does not give participants access to private data or the open internet. Their projects addressed subjects including mental health resources, government policy and international students’ transitions.

The highest-placing teams from each school presented their work Tuesday evening at Herndon High School.

McLean High School ninth grader Emmanuel Beakal won the grand prize for MyCompass, an application designed to help peers find mental health resources.

Woodson High School student Joelle Rice placed second with Genetic Guard. Her application seeks to explain complex genetic information to cancer patients’ families in an understandable and reassuring way.

Both applications incorporate security measures and tell users that they are not replacements for medical professionals. The challenge supports the innovation objective in the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan.

## Aging service van gets a new purpose

A van that spent two decades helping deliver thousands of meals will now provide hands-on training for automotive technology students at Fairfax High School.

FACETS, or Fairfax Area Christian Emergency and Transitional Services, donated the vehicle Wednesday as it approached the end of its useful life on the road. Auto technology teacher John King and his students received the keys during an event also supported by McKay Chevrolet and other community partners.

FACETS provides hot meals, pantry staples and emergency assistance to vulnerable people in Fairfax County. Students will now work with the van as part of their practical automotive education.

## Environmental council expands its work

Students from eight high schools gathered Wednesday for the third meeting of the Superintendent’s Youth Environmental Council.

Participants discussed writing an opinion article about the Potomac River sewage spill, pursuing grants and recruiting more students for environmental initiatives. They also divided into four working groups focused on policy advocacy, divisionwide events, school awareness programs and practical resources such as recycling and food sharing.

The council is seeking members from every FCPS high school. Its next meeting is scheduled for March 12 from 5 to 6 p.m. at the Gatehouse Administration Center Café, 8115 Gatehouse Road in Falls Church.

## Music programs earn national recognition

FCPS also received congratulations from Sen. Mark Warner’s office for being named one of the 2025 Best Communities for Music Education by the National Association of Music Merchants.

Warner said the recognition reflected the school division’s commitment to a well-rounded education. He noted that music programs help students practice listening, communication and critical-thinking skills while encouraging creativity, confidence, goal-setting and stronger connections to their schools.

The honor also recognized FCPS for its funding, staffing and standards for music instruction.

## Community partnerships and student activities

At a Thursday meeting of the Annandale Rotary Club, FCPS leaders discussed progress under the 2023-30 Strategic Plan, budget planning, future careers and the next generation of workers.

The club supports Annandale High School through the Just World Festival, speech and music contests, student leadership opportunities and campus improvements. Its work includes a partnership with Principal Shawn De Rose and the school community.

The week concluded with several student events. Hayfield Secondary School faced Edison High School in a girls’ basketball district final at Hayfield, while Oakton High School played Centreville High School at South Lakes High School.

Students at Lake Braddock Secondary School also staged a production of “Legally Blonde” on Saturday, with performers, backstage crews and boosters contributing to the musical.

Earlier in the week, students and employees brought tropical clothing and bright colors to school for the #FCPSThaw event, an effort to add warmth and levity during a stretch of cold, rainy winter weather.
