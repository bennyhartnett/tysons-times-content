Falls Church and nearby communities have a busy slate ahead, including a 51-artist exhibition, a youth civic leadership program, a housing discussion, a used-book sale and the Hall of Fame recognition of a local wrestling standout.

## “Joy!” fills Falls Church gallery with color

Falls Church Arts will open “Joy!,” a multimedia exhibition featuring more than 50 works by 51 artists, on Feb. 28.

A free opening reception will run from 7 to 9 p.m. at the Falls Church Arts gallery, 700-B West Broad St. Visitors can meet participating artists and hear the announcement of the Juror’s Choice Award winner.

The exhibition explores joy through acrylics, oils, watercolor, ink, digital photography, textiles, batik, thread, beads, polymer clay and other materials. Juror Jennifer Kahn Barlow, an Alexandria-based artist and critic, praised the show’s vibrant palettes, varied media and personal interpretations of joy.

“Joy!” will remain on display through April 12. Visitors may vote for the People’s Choice Award through March 27, with the winner scheduled to be announced March 28.

Admission is free. Winter gallery hours are 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Tuesdays through Fridays and 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. Saturdays and Sundays. The artworks are available for purchase at the gallery and online.

## Youth representatives program enters its 13th year

Citizens for a Better City will begin accepting applications March 2 for the 13th year of its Youth Representatives Initiative.

The program, launched in 2014, places high school-age students on Falls Church boards, commissions and participating civic group boards. Its goals include introducing young people to local government, supporting community organizations and developing future civic leaders.

Applications will be available online and through the Meridian High School Guidance Department. The submission deadline is April 10. Interviews are planned for April 16, 17, 18 and 21.

Forty-one Falls Church City high school students currently serve with organizations covering recreation, transportation, housing, elections, economic development, environmental sustainability, education, historic preservation, social justice and other community issues.

Participating groups include the Architectural Advisory Board, Arts & Humanities Council, Electoral Board, Housing Commission, Library Board of Trustees, Urban Forestry Commission, Economic Development Authority, Falls Church Chamber of Commerce, League of Women Voters, Creative Cauldron and Welcoming Falls Church, among others.

## Falls Church wrestling champion earns Hall of Fame honor

Falls Church native John Hanrahan has been selected for the National Wrestling Hall of Fame’s Georgia Chapter Class of 2026.

Hanrahan graduated from Falls Church High School, where he became a Virginia state champion and reached multiple national high school finals. He remains the most successful NCAA place-winner produced by the Northern Virginia region.

At Penn State University, Hanrahan became a two-time NCAA All-American and two-time conference champion. He qualified for the NCAA tournament four times and recorded 113 career victories, then a program benchmark.

He later won a UWW Veteran World Championship in freestyle wrestling and five USA Wrestling Masters national titles.

Hanrahan also built a coaching and athletic-performance career, earning GISA Wrestling Coach of the Year honors six times. He now operates Private Training Lab in Alpharetta, Georgia, where he works with athletes and other professionals on strength and performance development.

He was recognized at center mat before the Georgia State High School Wrestling Championships. His formal induction banquet is scheduled for May 2 in Gainesville, Georgia.

“The discipline I learned in Falls Church shaped everything that followed,” Hanrahan said. “Wrestling built the foundation for my life.”

Hanrahan is also the author of “Wrestling with Angels,” a memoir about elite competition, personal hardship and redemption.

## Palchik opens housing conversation series

Providence District Supervisor Dalia Palchik will host a Community Conversation on Housing from 6 to 8 p.m. Feb. 24 at the Jim Scott Community Center, 3001 Vaden Drive in Fairfax.

The discussion is planned as the first in a series of community conversations intended to bring neighbors together around major regional policy challenges. Housing will be the initial focus. Advance registration is available through Eventbrite.

## Tysons-Pimmit sale offers more than 15,000 books

The Tysons-Pimmit Regional Library’s quarterly used book and media sale will return March 5 through March 8, with more than 15,000 books available.

The selection will cover cooking, history, biography, science and technology, culture, fiction, mystery, science fiction, self-help, business, hobbies, religion and classics. Children’s books, puzzles, games, DVDs, CDs and audiobooks will also be offered.

The sale will run from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Proceeds will support the library and related activities. Organizers are also seeking donated books and media, along with volunteers to help with the sale.
