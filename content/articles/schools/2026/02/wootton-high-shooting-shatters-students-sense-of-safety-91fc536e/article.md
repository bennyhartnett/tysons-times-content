A 16-year-old student was shot during school hours at Thomas S. Wootton High School in Montgomery County, Maryland, on Monday, Feb. 9, unsettling students at Wootton and raising new concerns about school security in nearby communities.

The victim, whose identity has not been released, was taken to a local hospital and remained in stable condition. No other casualties were reported.

Police arrested another 16-year-old Wootton student shortly after the shooting outside school grounds. The Rockville City Police Department said officers recovered a 9-millimeter handgun from the suspect’s backyard.

The teenager has been charged as an adult with attempted second-degree murder, two counts of first-degree assault, two counts of second-degree assault and several firearms offenses, including possession of a dangerous weapon on school property.

Wootton went into lockdown immediately after the shooting as authorities closed nearby roads. Students were transported to Robert Frost Middle School, where they were reunited with their families.

Freshman Izzy Wang said students were dismissed by area, placed on buses and moved to the middle school gym. She described a draining and disorganized process that lasted for hours.

“My dad went to pick up my sister around 3 p.m., but I didn’t get picked up until 8,” Wang said. “He kept saying the whole process felt messy and ineffective.”

The shooting also intensified debate over safety precautions in Fairfax County Public Schools. Wootton is not part of FCPS and did not have metal detectors, while OpenGate detection systems were installed at both main entrances to McLean High School at the beginning of the school year. McLean conducted its second lockdown drill on Feb. 5.

Some McLean students remain unconvinced that those measures are sufficient.

“While metal detectors are a step in the right direction, I don’t think they have done anything, and I don’t think they ever will,” McLean senior Kareem El-Jazzar said. He argued that students could still find ways to bring prohibited items into the building and that stronger measures are needed to prevent weapons from reaching schools.

Although Wootton is about 33 minutes from McLean and lies across the Potomac River, students noted similarities between the schools. Both serve affluent communities, are known for strong academics and regularly place highly in U.S. News & World Report rankings for Maryland and Virginia.

“These schools are so similar,” El-Jazzar said. “If a shooting can happen at Wootton, then it definitely could happen here.”

For Wang, the shooting changed how she views safety at her new school. She said Wootton once seemed comparatively secure despite her awareness of previous concerns and shootings elsewhere in the country.

“Now I feel like the possibility of a shooting at our school has increased, and that’s scary,” she said.

Montgomery County Public Schools issued a statement Feb. 10 saying the district was continuing to coordinate with law enforcement, mental health professionals and community partners.

“During this painful time, we will continue to stand together to provide care, support and safety for all,” the district said.
