Fairfax County’s newly approved school transportation plan will provide buses for eligible high school students who remain at their current schools after redistricting, but elementary and middle school students will not receive the same service.

The Fairfax County School Board approved the plan in a 7-4 vote on Feb. 26, following its adoption of new attendance boundaries in January. Members Ryan McElveen, Mateo Dunne, Ilryong Moon and Ricardy Anderson voted against the measure.

The decision expanded Superintendent Michelle Reid’s recommendation, which would have limited transportation to high school seniors. It did not, however, extend buses to affected students at every grade level.

Under the boundary changes, students in certain grades may stay at their current schools instead of transferring to their newly assigned campuses. Reid said the redistricting approval did not include a commitment to pay all related transportation expenses, and FCPS is already confronting potential budget shortfalls.

Providing transportation under the approved high school plan is projected to cost $1.35 million. Busing eligible students across elementary, middle and high schools would cost an estimated $3.1 million.

The boundary changes will affect about 1,700 students when they take effect during the 2026-2027 school year. Of those, 911 may remain at their current schools. A survey of those families found that 81% would need bus transportation.

## Board members warn choice depends on transportation

Several board members argued that the option to stay is meaningful only when families can get their children to school.

Anderson, who represents the Mason District, compared the arrangement to giving food to a hungry person but placing it 20 feet up a tree, beyond reach without a ladder or the ability to levitate.

Hunter Mill District Representative Melanie Meren said excluding younger students conflicts with the board’s July 2025 decision to let certain current students choose where to attend as boundary adjustments are phased in.

Meren also said the board was not given enough possible budget reductions to consider before deciding whether FCPS could afford the full $3.1 million. She called the situation unnecessary stress for families and noted that at least half of the needed money was available in the School Board reserve.

Moon, an at-large member, suggested examining potential savings associated with a projected enrollment decline of 5,000 students next school year. He said the relatively small group affected deserves to remain at their schools and argued that withholding transportation effectively denies them that option.

Dunne, the Mount Vernon District representative, questioned why transportation funding was not part of the redistricting package approved in January. He said the issue was inseparable from the boundary decision and reinforced his earlier conclusion that the proposal was incomplete.

Faith Mekonen, a non-voting student representative from South County High School, also opposed restricting transportation to high school students. She said staying within the same school pyramid had helped her significantly and that reassignment could separate students from distinctive opportunities and the communities in which they grew up.

## Budget gap shapes the decision

Members who supported the limited plan emphasized FCPS’ financial constraints.

At-large Representative Kyle McDaniel said he shared the goal of addressing inequities but also had to account for the school system’s fiscal position.

Vice Chair Robyn Lady, who represents the Dranesville District, said scarce funding should prioritize high school students working toward graduation requirements and post-graduation plans. She acknowledged the importance of relationships with teachers and classmates but pointed to a $43 million gap between FCPS needs and anticipated funding from Virginia and the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors.

The approved measure permits FCPS to draw on end-of-year funds if the transportation budget cannot absorb the cost. Reid said the system will look for more creative routing to limit expenses while retaining the ability to replenish transportation funds if necessary.

The new boundaries will take effect when the 2026-2027 school year begins Aug. 24.
