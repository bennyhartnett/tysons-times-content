The U.S. Department of Education has opened a Title IX investigation into Fairfax County Public Schools following allegations that a Fairfax High School student groped multiple female students.

The department announced the inquiry March 30, saying 12 girls had made credible sexual assault allegations against one male student. Its Office for Civil Rights notified FCPS of the investigation.

FCPS said it is reviewing the complaint and is prepared to cooperate fully with the federal inquiry.

Israel Christopher Flores Ortiz, 18, was arrested by Fairfax City police on March 7, two days after officers responded to Fairfax High School to investigate reports of multiple assaults.

Flores Ortiz faces 13 counts of assault and battery. Four of those charges were added March 25 after additional alleged victims came forward, police said.

Court records list alleged offenses between Feb. 20 and March 4. Flores Ortiz remains in custody at the Fairfax County Adult Detention Center and is scheduled for an adjudicatory hearing in Fairfax County Juvenile and Domestic Relations Court on April 9.

The federal investigation follows Superintendent Michelle Reid’s March 18 decision to retain McGuireWoods for an independent review of the case. The law firm will examine what occurred, establish a timeline and determine whether school policies, procedures and regulations were properly followed.

FCPS said it is also cooperating with the police investigation but cannot release further details while that inquiry remains active. The school division said student and staff safety remains a priority.

Parents of some of the female students have alleged that Fairfax High Principal Georgina Aye minimized the extent of the reported conduct in a March 12 letter to families. They also said their children have faced harassment and bullying from classmates.

The Education Department presented the new inquiry as part of a broader series of investigations involving Northern Virginia school divisions. Its announcement connected the Fairfax High case with disputes over gender-identity policies and an Arlington County indecent-exposure case involving a registered sex offender accused of claiming a transgender identity to enter girls’ locker rooms.

FCPS and Arlington Public Schools are separately suing over Trump administration restrictions on their access to federal funding. The administration argues that policies supporting transgender students violate Title IX, citing reports that female students felt uncomfortable or witnessed inappropriate touching in shared bathrooms and locker rooms.

FCPS maintains that students have the right to use names and facilities consistent with their gender identity. The division argues that rescinding those protections would conflict with Virginia law and legal precedents barring discrimination against transgender students.

The Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals heard arguments Jan. 29 in the lawsuit against U.S. Education Secretary Linda McMahon. No timetable for a ruling has been announced.

The Education Department also began investigating FCPS in May 2025 over admissions policies at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology. Federal officials contend that changes intended to diversify the magnet school’s student body discriminated against Asian students.
