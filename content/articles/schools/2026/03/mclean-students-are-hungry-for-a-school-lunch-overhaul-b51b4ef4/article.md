For many McLean High School students, lunch is less of a welcome break and more of a daily frustration. Repetitive menus, limited vegetarian choices and concerns about flavor, portion size, quality and price have left some students dissatisfied with cafeteria meals.

Vegetarian students face a particular challenge. Although a large share of the school’s population follows a vegetarian diet, the available meals are often described as lacking both variety and flavor. Nonvegetarian students also encounter a fixed calendar of recurring options that can make lunch feel predictable.

Some students remember more appealing meals in elementary school, including orange chicken and special barbecue days featuring buffet-style service outside the cafeteria. By middle and high school—when academic demands are often greater—the choices appear to become less exciting.

The issue matters most for students from busy households who cannot pack food before school. For them, the cafeteria meal may be the only practical way to eat lunch during the day. Unappealing options can lead some students to consider skipping the meal entirely.

Fairfax County Public Schools could make its menus more engaging without abandoning a schedule. A surprise-meal day or a week devoted to food from a particular culture could introduce variety, while more freshly prepared dishes could improve taste.

Better lunches could also strengthen students’ morale. A satisfying meal offers time to relax, recharge and return to class in a better mood. Although the precise academic effect is uncertain, students who eat well and enjoy their lunch break may find it easier to focus throughout the afternoon.

FCPS should give students meals they can anticipate rather than endure. More variety, stronger vegetarian offerings and fresher preparation would be meaningful steps toward a lunch program that better serves McLean students.
