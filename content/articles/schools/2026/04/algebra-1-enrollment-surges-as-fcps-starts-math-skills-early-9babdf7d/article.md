Fairfax County Public Schools is expanding access to Algebra 1 by teaching problem-solving, pattern recognition and early algebraic thinking from kindergarten through sixth grade.

Over the past three years, eighth-grade Algebra 1 participation has risen 18% across all student groups. FCPS projects that 84.5% of eighth graders will be enrolled in the course during the 2026-27 school year.

The number of multilingual learners taking Algebra 1 has more than doubled, while participation among students with disabilities has also increased. FCPS is additionally seeking to close longstanding access gaps affecting African American and Hispanic students.

Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid has described broader Algebra 1 access as an issue of equity and opportunity, citing federal civil rights data about the course’s importance to students’ future academic options.

The district credits much of its progress to daily instruction that prioritizes reasoning and conceptual understanding, rather than simply finding the correct answer.

At Rolling Valley Elementary School, Principal Veronica Del Bagno said students learn to work through problems and understand the concepts behind their solutions.

“We're helping students be able to problem solve, not just solve for the answer, but really think through so that there's a true conceptual understanding,” Del Bagno said.

Kindergarten teacher Anna Markle said children as young as 5 and 6 are learning to take risks, recognize patterns and discover what they can accomplish.

“It has opened their eyes to the problem-solving that they're capable of doing, even as young as 5 and 6 years old,” Markle said.

By second grade, students practice dividing complicated problems into smaller, more manageable steps. Teacher Kaitlin Kaplewicz said the persistence and problem-solving skills they develop now will help them confront increasingly difficult math later.

Sixth-grade teacher Sara Parrish said years of preparation make algebra feel like a continuation of familiar work instead of an entirely new subject.

“When they get to algebra, it's not like this brand new thing,” Parrish said. “It's just an extension of what they've been doing the entire time.”

Students say that early exposure is already paying off. Nicholas, a Falls Church High School freshman, chose Algebra 1 in eighth grade because he wanted a challenge. Now enrolled in honors geometry, he feels prepared and plans to study business in college.

His classmate Thi said Algebra 1 helped her move beyond earlier difficulties, master the material and become more confident academically.

“It's very important for a student to take Algebra 1 in eighth grade, because that is the building block for Algebra 2,” Thi said.

FCPS says its early-grade approach is designed to help more students reach high school ready for advanced mathematics and graduate with skills they can apply beyond the classroom.
