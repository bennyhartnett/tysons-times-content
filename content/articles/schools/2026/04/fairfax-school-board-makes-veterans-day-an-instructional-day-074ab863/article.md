The Fairfax County School Board voted April 9 to make Veterans Day a standard instructional day beginning with the 2026-27 school year, while rejecting a proposal to do the same for Indigenous Peoples’ Day.

The Veterans Day measure also directs the superintendent to create a division-wide curriculum explaining the day’s meaning and engaging community members in its observance.

A separate motion would have established Indigenous Peoples’ Day as an instructional day and created lessons about the history, sovereignty and contributions of Indigenous people, particularly First Americans. That proposal did not pass.

Board members also capped elementary early-release days at eight per school year. They then approved the amended 2026-27 school calendar based on the evening’s calendar votes.

The superintendent was directed to present recommended calendars for the 2027-28 and 2028-29 school years at the board’s July 9, 2026, regular meeting. The recommendations must incorporate broad community engagement, including in-person feedback and electronic surveys.

The meeting began at 7:05 p.m. with a performance of the national anthem by the Whitman Middle School Symphonic Orchestra, directed by Elizabeth Fogel. City of Fairfax School Board member Sarah Kelsey attended.

The board adopted proclamations recognizing Arab American Heritage Month, School Librarian Appreciation Month and the Month of the Military Child. It also honored educators who earned or renewed National Board Certification and heard comments from six members of the public.

Student representative Faith Mekonen delivered the student representative report.

## Policies and oversight

The board approved revised policies covering lactation-support programming, employee-organization recognition and sexual harassment. It also authorized factual and typographical corrections to the Strategic Governance Manual.

Superintendent Michelle C. Reid presented a monitoring report on Executive Limitation 7, which covers asset protection. The board found her in compliance, approved the 2025-26 monitoring report and later voted to retain the policy in its current form.

During the consent agenda, members approved minutes from the March 19 regular meeting and the March 24 public and closed meeting. The board also adopted policies concerning site and building acquisition, value engineering and maintenance of public-school facilities, while retiring its petty-cash policy.

Reid and Multilingual Learner Services administrators Rich Polio, Darina Walsh and Karen Kleiber presented an update on English Language Development under the division’s strategic plan.

An Academic Matters presentation was postponed until the April 23 regular meeting because of the late hour.

## Items awaiting future votes

Several proposals were introduced without a vote, including the Strategic Plan Goal 4 report on equitable access and opportunity and the 2026-27 Special Education Annual Plan and federal Part B funding application.

Future action is also expected on contracts for construction testing and inspection, a makeup-air-unit installation at Bryant High School and replacement lighting for the school’s baseball field. The board will also consider a resolution naming the Vaughn Lewis Field at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology.

Governance Committee member Rachna Meren delivered a committee report. Superintendent Matters and Board Matters were skipped because of the late hour.

The meeting adjourned at 12:18 a.m.

Upcoming meetings include committee sessions on comprehensive planning and development at 6:30 p.m. April 13, governance at 2:30 p.m. April 14, administration at 4:30 p.m. April 15, public engagement at 4 p.m. April 20 and governance at 4 p.m. April 21. The next regular meeting is scheduled for 7 p.m. April 23.
