As the 2026-27 school year nears its end, McLean High School students are confronting a frustrating reality: Their summer break may not offer enough time to recover, pursue academic opportunities and reconnect with family.

U.S. public schools average 10 to 12 weeks of summer vacation, according to MDR statistics. Fairfax County Public Schools provides 68 days—just under nine weeks and slightly below the national average.

That difference can have a significant impact on McLean students. The highly rigorous school is ranked No. 261 nationally, and many students use the summer to strengthen their college applications through jobs, internships, SAT or ACT preparation and additional classes. They also need time to rest after a demanding academic year.

Rising juniors and seniors face especially difficult choices. Many summer programs last several weeks, while the Governor’s School alone can require two to four weeks. With less than 10 weeks available, students may have time for only one or two academic programs, even when several match their interests.

Family obligations add another layer of pressure. Many McLean students come from abroad or have relatives in other countries, making summer the most practical opportunity for an extended visit. Such trips often last about a month because of the time and expense involved, leaving students to fit internships, studying and other programs into an even smaller window.

FCPS offers numerous holidays during the school year, but maintaining those breaks by shortening summer creates real tradeoffs. For McLean students juggling ambitious academic plans, family visits and the need for genuine rest, 68 days simply does not feel long enough.
