The rope hung rough and hairy from the tower at James Whitcomb Riley Elementary School, and every morning Mr. Brown the custodian gave it a pull. That clang sent a clear message across the neighborhood: walk faster, pedal harder, or face the shame of a tardy slip.

For one student who loved school, the bell meant something different. It was a daily invitation, a sound that promised another day of learning was about to begin.

Bells have a way of marking our lives. Some are welcome; others less so. The clock tower in Salzburg, Austria, bonged dutifully every fifteen minutes through the night—a relentless reminder that European cities were built before wristwatches existed.

## The Netherlands Carillon

But the bells that left the deepest impression stand in Arlington. The Netherlands Carillon, a towering gift from the Dutch people to thank America for its role in World War II, produced something beyond mere sound. Its fifty bells could play actual melodies, filling the air with tunes like "Beautiful Dreamer."

One summer evening brought a rare treat: an open house at the carillon. Visitors climbed the many steps to watch a musician work the instrument's unusual keyboard—wooden dowels arranged like oversized piano keys. The performer had traveled from Europe specifically for the concert, explaining that qualified carillonneurs were scarce in America.

He even invited guests to try the dowels themselves, demonstrating both the punch method and the stroke method of playing.

## A Perfect Summer Evening

Back on the ground, a family spread their blanket on the grass. A young girl tested new roller skates on the walkways while "Skaters Away" drifted down from the tower above.

Some memories stay etched forever: the music floating through warm air, wheels on pavement, a child skating in time to bells that crossed an ocean to say thank you.
