The Fairfax County School Board unanimously approved attendance boundary changes for Skyview High School and five western Fairfax County school pyramids on July 16, setting the changes on course to begin in the 2027-28 school year.

The decision covers the Centreville, Chantilly, Oakton, South Lakes and Westfield pyramids. Fairfax County Public Schools estimates that the new boundaries will affect 2,544 students, including 420 middle school students and 2,124 high school students.

According to the district, that total represents less than 8% of students across the western pyramids. The supplied district information does not identify the affected neighborhoods or addresses, the individual school-to-school assignments, or the feeder-pattern changes that families may experience.

Elementary school attendance boundaries will remain unchanged under the approved plan, although FCPS said some feeder patterns may be adjusted.

The boundary changes were recommended by Superintendent Michelle Reid. Along with those changes, the board unanimously approved a phased enrollment plan for Skyview and the western pyramids. It also unanimously approved a transportation model for Skyview. Details about the phases and transportation arrangements were not included in the supplied information.

“This vote reflects months of thoughtful discussion, community engagement, and careful consideration,” Reid said. “We recognize the importance of these decisions for students, families, and staff, and remain committed to supporting every school community as we move forward.”

FCPS said revised boundary scenarios were presented at community meetings in May and June 2026. The district reported that its engagement process included six community meetings and six meetings of the Superintendent’s Boundary Advisory Committee.

The district also reported receiving 15,000 map comments. It said its Boundary Explorer tool drew 25,000 unique visitors and was used for 49,000 address searches. Those engagement figures were supplied by FCPS and were not independently verified in the source material provided for this report.

Robyn Lady, identified in the district announcement as the newly elected School Board chair, credited community feedback with helping shape the decision.

“Your feedback helped inform this important decision,” Lady said. “Together, we are laying the foundation for a school community where students will learn, grow, and thrive for years to come.”

The unanimous vote settles the board’s decision on the boundary recommendation, phased enrollment plan and Skyview transportation model more than a year before implementation is scheduled to start. However, the supplied information does not include underlying school-utilization data or the criteria used to weigh community priorities. It also does not include independent reporting or responses from families, students, employees or other stakeholders about support for or opposition to the changes.

For families in the five affected pyramids, the district’s estimate provides the broadest measure of the decision’s reach: 2,124 high school students and 420 middle school students. More detailed information will be needed to show which students will receive new assignments and how any feeder-pattern adjustments will work as FCPS prepares for the 2027-28 transition.
