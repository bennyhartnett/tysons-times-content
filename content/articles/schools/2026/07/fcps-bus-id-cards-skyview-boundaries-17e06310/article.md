Fairfax County Public Schools is preparing to give every student a new identification card for the 2026-27 school year, part of a transportation technology rollout arriving as the division moves ahead with new attendance boundaries for Skyview High School and several western Fairfax County schools.

Students who ride FCPS buses will be expected to tap their cards when boarding and exiting. The system will allow the transportation office to determine which students are on a particular bus. Students who do not ordinarily use school transportation will also receive cards.

FCPS has emphasized that the technology associates a student’s tap with a bus rather than continuously monitoring the student.

“The system does not track the student’s location; it tracks the location of the school bus that they have tapped onto,” the FCPS Office of Communications said.

Upgraded tablets will give drivers route and navigation information. FFXnow reported that the tablets will also display student identities and assigned stops, while ridership information will be transmitted in real time to dispatchers who can send assistance during emergencies.

A student who forgets or loses a card can be manually checked in or out by the driver using the tablet. FCPS encourages families to request replacement cards from their schools.

The precise rollout timing differs in the supplied accounts. FFXnow reported that the technology would roll out when school begins Aug. 24, while FCPS said student tapping would start during the first few weeks of the school year.

Later in fall 2026, the division plans to replace Here Comes the Bus with a new, as-yet-unnamed family app. It is expected to show real-time bus locations and notify families when their children board or exit. FCPS also anticipates eventually using the cards for services such as meals and field trips, although those functions have not launched.

The cards will be color-coded by school. Their backs will include a QR code and information about the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

The transportation changes follow the School Board’s unanimous July 16 approval of attendance boundaries and phased enrollment for Skyview. The plan will draw students from the Westfield, Chantilly and South Lakes high school areas beginning in 2027-28.

FCPS says the assignments will affect 2,544 students. The division expects 1,159 students to be assigned to Skyview in the first boundary year and estimates attendance will reach 1,606 in 2028-29.

The published school-by-school figures do not fully explain the stated total. They identify 1,062 students from Westfield, 333 from Chantilly and 384 from South Lakes, totaling 1,779. Separate changes will reassign 186 Chantilly students and 77 Centreville students to Westfield, but even including those groups leaves part of the reported 2,544-student total unaccounted for in the supplied material.

Ninth graders within the Skyview boundary may remain on their previous high school pathway during the first year. That option ends in 2028-29. Students using the general transfer policy will not receive bus transportation under the phasing plan.

The plan also redraws the lines for Carson and Franklin middle schools, gives Franklin students two high school destinations and moves a few dozen Liberty Middle School students to Stone Middle School. Oakton’s formal boundary was not changed, though Oakton students could opt into Skyview for 2026-27.

More than 700 students volunteered to attend Skyview for 2026-27 while renovations and Virginia High School League membership remained pending. FCPS bought the former private-school campus for $150 million in June 2025 and later approved a nearly $2 million design contract for additional improvements, not construction.

The board also directed Superintendent Michelle Reid to return in October with plans for business, information-technology and skilled-trades programming at Westfield, along with a review of the boundary process and strategies to limit programming losses associated with declining enrollment.

“It’s not a pure science, and it’s not going to be perfect in our recommendations,” Reid said.
