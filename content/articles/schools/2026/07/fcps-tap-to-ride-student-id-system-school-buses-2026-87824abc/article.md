Fairfax County Public Schools plans to introduce a tap-to-ride student ID system during the 2026-27 school year, giving students cards to scan when they board and exit school buses.

The district announced the transportation modernization effort July 23. Beginning in fall 2026, students are expected to tap their cards on readers when entering and leaving an FCPS bus. The district also plans to launch a family app later in the fall with real-time bus-location updates and notifications when a child boards or exits a bus.

FCPS buses are being equipped with mobile tablets that provide drivers with turn-by-turn route maps. When a student boards, the tablet will display student information and indicate whether the student is boarding the correct bus. The tablets will also allow drivers to check students in or out manually when a card is forgotten or lost.

The tap records will allow the FCPS Office of Transportation to determine which students are aboard each bus. FCPS says the system will track the location of the bus onto which a student tapped, rather than independently tracking the student’s location.

All students will receive an ID card, including students who do not ordinarily ride a school bus. The cards will be color-coded by school. Their backs will include a QR code for additional information and information about the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. FCPS says Virginia law requires the cards to include the 988 information.

The district says the cards eventually will be usable for other services, including school meals and field trips. FCPS also says the boarding system is intended to improve the safety and efficiency of its bus operation.

The supplied announcement does not provide a firm launch date beyond the 2026-27 school year and fall 2026. It also does not specify how long boarding records will be retained, who can access them or what security controls will apply. The announcement does not establish whether families will be offered parental-consent or opt-out procedures.

No reactions from families, students, drivers or privacy experts were included in the supplied information.
