Fairfax County Public Schools is centralizing athletic eligibility decisions and expanding oversight after an external investigation found that football programs at Hayfield Secondary School and Fairfax High School violated Virginia High School League rules.

Superintendent Michelle Reid and School Board Chair Sandy Anderson informed families this week that Chicago-based law firm Baker McKenzie had completed its investigation into student-athlete transfers and eligibility practices across the school division.

Under the resulting changes, the central office—not individual high schools—will determine transfer eligibility. The division is also developing standards to review new student registrations with the same scrutiny applied to transfers between county schools.

The investigation followed allegations in 2024 that then-Hayfield football coach Darryl Overton improperly encouraged former players from Freedom High School in Woodbridge to transfer to Hayfield. Overton was in his first season coaching at Hayfield and also worked at the school as a security specialist.

Hayfield played one game before withdrawing from the postseason. Reid later publicly apologized for the school division’s handling of the controversy.

Fairfax High School’s program also faced allegations that coaches paid thousands of dollars to recruit a football player.

School Board member Mateo Dunne said athletes deserve confidence that rules will be applied fairly.

“When a student wants to play on a team or play a sport, whether it’s a team sport or an individual sport, they want to know that it’s a level playing field,” Dunne said Friday. “They want to know that there are rules. They want to know that the refs will judge in a neutral manner.”

Dunne said selecting an outside firm that had not worked for the county during the previous 10 years was essential because it “quickly became apparent that the internal investigation was not credible.”

In response to the Hayfield case, FCPS began using a platform that centralizes the collection, review and processing of transfer requests. The division also authorized its Office of Student Activities and Athletics to investigate concerns about student eligibility.

Mandatory training will now extend to coaches at every level, including unpaid volunteers, after the investigation identified gaps in existing requirements.

Reid and Anderson said the publicly announced changes exclude confidential personnel matters, adding that the superintendent has taken and will continue to take appropriate personnel actions.

After Hayfield’s 2024 season ended, Overton became director of football at the St. James Performance Academy.

Dunne said FCPS has since demonstrated more consistent enforcement and stronger oversight across multiple schools.

“What we want to make sure is that there is no repeat of Hayfield,” he said, adding that the division must protect the integrity of its athletic programs while providing accountability and transparency.
